# Published winners stall past this cascade

None of the architectures that won a published record-linkage contest, or that lead a 2025–2026 matcher table, apply as an upgrade to a cascade that already retrieves **99.75%** of true pairs and scores **0.985** macro F0.5 on the public board. The decisive contest margins were a regex and mock-label system at F **0.99** against a neural cascade that had fallen to F **0.47** (SIGMOD 2020), a participant-reported blocking recall of **52.9%** inside a hard pair budget (SIGMOD 2022), and, on a different task, a dictionary **0.0008** IoU ahead of an encoder (SNOMED 2024, a gap the organizers call not significant). The only study that starts from blocker recall already at **97.7–100%** finds more retrieval worth **+0.3 to +0.4** percentage points, or zero, and finds that hard vetoes can cost purity once the matcher is already clean. No paper in that set reports macro F0.5. Two files that raised this team's dev-eval from **0.99030** to **0.99079** and **0.99061** scored about **0.98475** and **0.98476** publicly, so a higher dev number is not a better architecture. The only edit still open, adding back **8,506** high-scoring judge vetoes on the unlabelled country, touches **8,321** entities, leaves dev at **0.990306**, and cannot close a **0.005** gap. Its public effect is unmeasured. External lexicons, geocoders, and self-training stay out.

## Contest wins landed where the matcher or the blocker was still weak

The current file is already the shape later entrants are still building: canonical strings, a blocker, two gradient-boosted stages, a LoRA pairwise judge of about **4B** parameters inside a roughly **5.3B** stack, a stacker, and a one-owner assignment. Each Source 2/3 record has at most one Source 1 owner, a Source 1 entity keeps a mean of about **3.46** matches or none, a correct empty prediction scores **1.0**, and the unlabelled country is **15%** of Source 1. Train labels exist only for the US and India. That is many-to-one assignment onto a clean reference, scored as macro F0.5, not transitive clustering of a dirty catalog. No finished Kaggle, KDD Cup, SIGMOD, DrivenData, or WDC contest from 2023 through 26 Sep 2026 is that task with a published winner. SIGMOD's last entity-resolution editions were 2020 and 2021 for matching and 2022 for blocking only; 2023–2025 were nearest-neighbor graphs, hybrid vector search, and an in-memory join ([SIGMOD contest archive](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/); [SIGMOD 2025 task](https://sigmod-contest-2025.github.io/)). The Amazon ML Challenge 2026 is the same three-source, macro-F0.5 problem and, on 26 Sep 2026, was still inside its 72-hour window, so it has no winner ([challenge PDF](https://d8it4huxumps7.cloudfront.net/files/6ab674645103d_emails_comms_amazon_ml_challenge_2026.pdf); [prep note](https://builder.aws.com/content/3HiM6zDmFrF98fRzOUETnGFDoqz/amazon-ml-challenge-2026-your-complete-prep-guide-with-live-demo)).

SIGMOD 2020 is the clean case of a hand rule beating a fine-tuned matcher, and it does not transfer. UniMoRe's token blocker already had recall **0.99** and precision **0.28** on the labelled pairs. DeepMatcher RNN on the full camera catalog then scored F **0.47** (recall **0.85**, precision **0.32**). The shipped regex system scored F **0.99**, precision **0.99**, recall **0.98**. The abstract's ceiling for the ML attempt is "not reaching 0.49." On the labelled sets the same RNN looked solved: F **0.96** on W and F **0.9826** on Y after title truncation. The failure they name is brand-specific one-character edits and brands in the hidden set that never appear in the labelled file ([Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)). All five finalists scored F **0.99**. First versus second was runtime, **0.61 s** against **10.65 s** on the winner's laptop, after mock labels alone had already scored F **0.97** and about 200 inspected titles had tied the field at **0.99** ([Blacher et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf)). That is a **0.52** F gap against a collapsed matcher, then a **0.00** F gap between rule systems. This file's public **0.985** against dev **0.99030** is a five-thousandth disagreement, not a labelled-versus-secret collapse of half an F. Copying Wikipedia camera aliases, eBay brand fields, or a hand legal-form list would also break the ban on external data.

The official leaders table did not render, second place used no neural model at all, and no writeup isolates the Jaccard term ([WBSG README](https://github.com/abrinkmann/acm_sigmoid_2022_challenge); [11th-place README](https://github.com/theodoratrz/sigmodContest2022); [April README](https://github.com/rutgers-db/SIGMOD2022-Programming-Contest-Public)). A blocker whose published glory is keeping about half of true pairs is not a lesson for a blocker at **99.75%**. The license card for that distilled checkpoint was not re-opened. Its pretraining corpus is also extra data this team cannot add.

Foursquare 2022 remains the nearest finished noisy-place contest, and this pass did not reopen it because no new primary writeup changed the leak story. Those cascades — several blockers, a gradient-boosted tree, cross-encoders, then a cluster repair — are the family already running here. They had latitude and longitude, they optimized IoU rather than macro F0.5, and their honest band is not a route from **0.985** to anything higher. Shopee's private **0.780 / 0.779 / 0.779** separated neighborhood blending and thresholds on image and text groups of a much smaller catalog ([leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/leaderboard)). KDD Cup tracks, SNOMED span linking, and OAEI instance matching are not this problem. DogMa's instance F-measure of **0.90** (precision **0.91**, recall **0.89**) against BaselineAltLabel **0.84** is the first year a system beat those baselines on that track, and the pages read here do not establish whether DogMa is rules, an embedding, or an LLM ([OAEI 2025 results](https://oaei.ontologymatching.org/2025/results); [results paper](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2023984/FULLTEXT01.pdf)). KIRI's dictionary beat SNOBERT by **0.0008** IoU, with overlapping bootstrap intervals, and both beat a weak BERT benchmark by about **0.24**. On concepts absent from training the dictionary's edge was **0.270** against **0.235** and **0.231** ([JAMIA](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/9/1397/8199759); [DrivenData](https://drivendata.co/blog/snomed-ct-entity-linking-challenge-winners)). That is a thousandth on clinical linking, concentrated on unseen concepts, and the winning artifact is a lexicon. It does not apply.

The two validation scores are different splits ([Siva documentation](https://github.com/Siva402-ai/amazon-ml-challenge-2026/blob/main/Documentation_template.md); [DevanshSrajput README](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge)). Both force the constraint this file already enforces: many noisy records attach to one reference, and a noisy record receives at most one reference. Their training EDAs agree with the cardinality, not with a new model. A matched reference has about **3.7** or **3.67** links, singletons are about **5.6%**, and matches do not cross countries. One of them calls the unlabelled slice France at **15.0%** of test Source 1. That country name is entrant EDA, not an organizer statistic. Neither entrant describes a reversed validation ranking. ## Retrieval past 97.7% recall moves tenths of a point

Pavani, Aluri, Jadhav, Prasad, and Sanka are the only 2025–2026 study found here that measures blocking recall and then ablates what happens after it. On six dedup benchmarks, blocker recall at k=20 is already **100.0** on DBLP–Scholar for a single HNSW index, the ensemble, and the ensemble plus identifier blocking. MusicBrainz 200K is **97.7 / 98.1 / 98.1**. NCV is **97.7 / 97.8 / 98.1**. A single index misses 67 true MusicBrainz pairs; the ensemble recovers 11 of them, **+0.4** percentage points. Identifier blocking recovers 7 true pairs on NCV, **+0.3** percentage points, and adds nothing on DBLP–Scholar, which has no identifier fields ([Pavani et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26298v1)). With pair completeness already **99.75%**, that is the right order of magnitude for a better embedder, and it is the wrong sign for a project that has already closed a bigger embedder. The missing quarter of a percent of true pairs is at most a small recall term. It funds a macro-F0.5 move on the order of a thousandth only if those misses are ordinary, and the papers do not measure a country-level amplification that would turn them into five thousandths. Sudowoodo reports blocking recall at or above **98%** on some Magellan sets and spends the gain on a smaller candidate list, up to **84.8%** fewer candidates on Abt–Buy, not on a matcher or a rule after recall is frozen ([Sudowoodo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.04122)). MERAI does not report blocking pair-completeness at all. At a Splink threshold of **0.99** its prose gives dedup precision **100%** and recall **92.5–95%** against Splink precision **60–76.5%**, using Magellan-style string features and weighted disjoint cliques, on North Carolina voter snapshots up to **15.7 million** records. No component-level delta is given at fixed blocking recall ([MERAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767)). A **100%** precision on voters is not a method that beats **0.985** on businesses.

What still moves precision in that Walmart pipeline, after a tournament-winning matcher and a global threshold, is a threshold that depends on how many fields are filled, and hard per-field vetoes. Cluster purity moves from a weak baseline, not from a file that is already at **0.985**. Table V's path on Restaurants is **51.6 / 53.1 / 84.4** for baseline, sparsity-aware thresholds, and hard vetoes. MusicBrainz 200K is **77.7 / 86.1 / 89.7**. The table's veto step is **+31.3** purity points on Restaurants and **+3.6** on MusicBrainz. The prose's **+32.8** on Restaurants is the baseline-to-final gap, so the paper disagrees with itself by **1.5** points on what the veto step did. On Geo Settlements, DBLP–Scholar, and NCV the veto column is **0.2–0.3** points worse than sparsity alone ([Pavani et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26298v1)). Vetoes are how you climb out of a **51.6** purity restaurant file. They are not free precision on a clean file. This team's neighbour-house deletion is the same idea on labelled dev, and it is falsified: last-digit house pairs at stage-2 probability at least **0.5** match **95.0%** of the time when an exact-house sibling exists and **94.9%** when it does not. At probability at least **0.99** both rates are above **0.998**. The sibling carries no separation. Do not ship it again.

Their clustering ablation is a warning about the wrong post-process, not a better assignment for this metric. Pair F1 for a center-based baseline, connected components, and a verified merge is **0.540 / 0.000 / 0.277** on MusicBrainz 200K and **0.667 / 0.002 / 0.667** on NCV. On Cora, where validation precision is **99.7%**, transitivity is the gain (**0.321** to **0.885**). On Geo, raw transitivity hurts (**0.889** to **0.771**) and verified merge wins (**0.972**). The authors tie the collapse to a baseline whose pairwise precision is about **0.5**, and they say verified merge cannot save that matcher. Those pair-F1 numbers are not the tournament matcher F1 in their Table III, where MusicBrainz DeepMatcher is **0.964** and NCV LightGBM is **0.993** ([Pavani et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26298v1)). Read it as a direction under F0.5: one accepted false link can merge records, and F0.5 prices a false merge at twice a miss. Do not turn the current one-owner rule into connected components across Source 1. The bipartite-matching literature that freezes the scored graph and varies only the clustering algorithm did not yield a numeric F1 delta in this pass. Whether unique mapping, Hungarian assignment, or a rival-aware cut would beat the current one-owner rule on macro F0.5 is unmeasured. Hungarian and unique one-to-one mapping would also throw away true links beyond the first, against a mean of about **3.46**.

The matcher tournament in the same paper is not conditioned on blocking recall above **98%**, and it does not crown a new network. Under a cap of 10,000 labels, DeepMatcher and LightGBM each win three of six sets. A GAT wins none. The largest family gap is DBLP–Scholar, LightGBM **0.541** versus DeepMatcher **0.160**. NCV is saturated: **0.992 / 0.993 / 0.989**, a margin under **0.001** ([Pavani et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26298v1)). Swapping the trees for DeepMatcher or a graph net is not what that table supports once the blocker is full and the public score is already **0.985**. Ditto's ablations, AnyMatch's, and MatchGPT's are on benchmark pair sets that those papers do not document as having pair completeness above **98%** of the raw tables. AnyMatch's mean zero-shot F1 is **81.96**. Replacing GPT-2 with T5 costs **2.38** mean F1. MatchGPT with GPT-4 is **86.36**, and Unicorn's zero-shot mean on that table is **77.54** ([AnyMatch](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04073v1)). Ditto's domain-knowledge injection is **+1.2%** average F1 on ER-Magellan, and its dirty-Walmart gap over the older character-aware matcher is **85.69** against **53.8** ([Ditto](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00584)). Those numbers justify a transformer when the alternative is a weak product-title matcher on a pre-blocked file. They are not a delta on top of this cascade. GPT-4 is outside the Apache or MIT and 8B rules anyway.

## Scaling the judge ties on English F1 and over-accepts under shift

The second-hand claim that Qwen3-4B equals Qwen3-8B at **84.1** is one cell, and it is not a dirty-entity or unseen-language result. In Zhang, Li, Calixto, Groth, and Schelter, the Qwen3 cross-encoder's macro F1 over nine English datasets is **83.0** at 0.6B, **84.1** at 4B, and **84.1** at 8B. The generative matcher does not tie: **84.4 / 86.8 / 87.7**. Drop Semi-Heter and Semi-Rel and the cross-encoder averages become **84.4 / 86.4 / 86.7**, so 8B still leads 4B by **0.3** once the two shift sets are gone. On WDC Products with 80% corner cases the 8B cross-encoder leads the 4B, **83.2** versus **81.7**, and both lead that paper's unstable Ditto re-run of **57.9±21.4**. On structured Walmart–Amazon the 4B cross-encoder leads the 8B, **91.6** versus **90.7** ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24688)). The nine datasets are product, publication, book, movie, and software matching in English. "Unseen" in that paper means unseen attribute combinations and cross-dataset transfer, not a held-out country. Off-diagonal F1, 8B models only, is **30.3** for bi-encoders, **64.9** for cross-encoders, and **73.8** for generative matchers. The generative head's advantage is schema shift. Three aligned fields — name, address, country — are not that shift. The study does not contain this team's Qwen3.5-4B LoRA, so a claim that an 8B Qwen3.5 judge would tie or beat the current judge on the public board is unmeasured. An 8B cross-encoder also sits on the parameter cap by itself. The published English macro does not show a reason to spend that step, and a bigger embedder is already closed.

The same paper is evidence against "use a larger model to correct the shifted country." On Semi-Rel, negative pairs with identical titles are **0.5%** of training negatives and **0.4%** of validation negatives, then **28.7%** of test negatives. Cross-encoder F1 falls from **97.6±0.2** at 0.6B (precision **96.9**) to **89.5±4.7** at 4B (precision **81.7**) and **85.0±11.4** at 8B (precision **76.4**), while recall stays near **99**. Larger models lean harder on a title-agreement shortcut that validation rewards and the test punishes. When the authors rebuild the split so the title-collision rate is about **6%** in train, validation, and test, cross-encoder F1 returns to **98.2 / 98.3 / 98.1**. On Semi-Heter, **82%** of positive test pairs have attribute combinations absent from train and validation, cross-encoder recall stays at **42.6 / 48.9 / 51.1**, and F1 is **58.7 / 62.8 / 65.1**. Cut that rate to **17%** and fine-tune, and recall rises to about **96–97** and F1 to **97.1 / 97.3 / 96.1** ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24688)). The refit works when the training distribution is corrected to the true mechanism. It is not "add decoys and ownerless records." This team's orphan-world refit is the unmatched-cue case. It is closed, and the two later files that moved dev up scored about **0.98475** and **0.98476** on the public board. No entity-resolution paper found here refits on a validation set enriched with decoys and ownerless records and then loses to the untouched source model. That specific loss remains the team's unpublished result. Zhang's successful protocol used a redesigned labelled split. It is not available for a country with no labels, and self-training on that country is closed.

Steiner, Peeters, and Bizer measure the complementary failure inside entity matching. Fine-tuning helps in-domain transfer and hurts cross-domain transfer. Product-to-scholar, Llama 3.1 8B averages **−11.05** F1 against its own zero-shot baseline. Scholar-to-product, the same 8B averages **−1.11**, while GPT-4o-mini averages **+1.60**. Llama 70B also fails to beat zero-shot on the cross-domain scenarios they report ([Steiner et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08185v2)). The simpler model won the shifted domain. That does not license replacing this LoRA with Llama or with an API. Llama's license is not Apache-2.0 or MIT, GPT-4 is not an 8B local model, and zero-shot Qwen3.5 against this **0.985** file is unmeasured. The directional point is the one the stand-in already shows: a judge fit on the source countries can be wrong on a country it never saw. The published repair is a labelled rebalance, not a larger fine-tune. LoRAs trained only on the previous model's mistakes are closed. That selection is the error-mining pattern Steiner's setting punishes on the shifted domain, and it is not reopened here.

A character or byte matcher is not the missing architecture either. No benchmark found here pits a character-level or byte-level matcher against a word-level transformer on organization names or legal-form tokens and crowns an Apache or MIT winner at or under 8B on that slice. On entity-matching benchmarks, fastText's character n-grams beat other static word vectors on out-of-vocabulary tokens and still lose to contextual wordpiece and sentence transformers ([Zeakis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12329)). On Magellan's dirty splits, Ditto's wordpiece models beat DeepMatcher+, the character-aware predecessor, by a wide margin: iTunes–Amazon dirty **95.65** against **79.4**, Walmart–Amazon dirty **85.69** against **53.8** ([Ditto](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00584)). The byte-level win that does exist is French XNLI, not names. ByT5-Small at 300M scores French **75.0** and average **67.2**; mT5-Small at the same size scores French **62.4** and average **63.0**. The authors still call subword models the practical choice on latency and memory ([Sun et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07111)). Whether a byte encoder would raise macro F0.5 after a **99.75%** blocker is unmeasured. Peeters and Bizer's cross-language product result, English pairs lifting German matching once some German labels exist, uses target-language labels this task does not have and is not a recommendation ([Peeters and Bizer 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.03338v1)).

No 2024–2026 paper found here trains a gradient-boosted tree on a frozen language-model score plus string features and beats that language model on dirty textual entity matching. The closest boosting paper is animal re-identification: LightGBM at one threshold scores **0.629** private against an average meta-model at **0.608**, on visual embeddings, and the team finished 7th ([Fedorchenko and Arefiev](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4038/paper_240.pdf)). Where string features and a neural matcher were compared on Amazon–Google, Magellan's similarity learner scores F1 **49.1**, a hybrid **69.3**, BERT **73.1**, and BERT-ER **75.3** ([BERT-ER](https://windofshadow.github.io/_pages/pdf/BERTer.pdf)). Ditto's reported gains are over similarity-feature systems, not over a stack that already contains the language-model score ([Ditto](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.00584)). Zeakis and coauthors' supervised averages put Ditto at **0.93** and Unicorn and RoBERTa at **0.92**, and those are the matchers themselves ([VLDB Journal 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00778-024-00879-4)). The current stacker is the scored file, not a literature result to extend. Pair features in the stacker are closed. Whether deleting the stacker would beat **0.985** is unmeasured, and the two public regressions are the reason not to find out by chasing a higher dev score.

## The open token-swap add-back is not a published win

The only edit still open is an add-back on the unlabelled country: **8,506** pairs, all with stage-2 probability at least **0.99** (median **0.998**), **8,485** of them judge vetoes, **7,748** of them the same house with exactly one token swapped on each side. In-country, a judge veto of that slice matches **0.967** of the time in the US and **0.734** of the time in India. A judge that never saw the country had veto precision **0.044** in the **0.99–0.999** band and **0.000** above **0.999** on a labelled stand-in, and those vetoes were true matches. The edit touches **8,321** entities. It cannot close a **0.005** gap. On the current stacker its dev score is **0.990306**, against the file of record at **0.99030**, because the rule adds nothing on labelled countries. The public score of that edit is unmeasured. It is not a method that beats **0.985**.

No 2020–2026 paper found here publishes precision for the rule "same house number and street, names differ by exactly one token." The literature splits that pair by which token changed. Dalvi, Olteanu, Raghavan, and Bohannon treat a place name as a core token plus background tokens. "Fresca's" is the core of "Fresca's Peruvian Restaurant," and "Guggenheim" is the core of the museum but the background of "Guggenheim Starbucks." They report **90%** recall at **90%** precision from names with contextual weights, and they give both errors this slice mixes: "Newpark Mall Sears" versus "Newpark Mall Sears Outlet" can be two stores, and "Central Park" versus "Central Park Cafe" changes meaning by city. A single global weight cannot express that ([Dalvi et al. 2014](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www2014/proceedings/proceedings/p409.pdf)). The Census Longitudinal Business Database, after identifier passes, blocks single-unit establishments on state, street, and ZIP and then requires at least two common name words that are unique inside the block. It does not keep a pair merely because the street matches, and it publishes no precision for those passes ([Chow et al. 2021](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28839/w28839.pdf)). "Collège Saint-Joseph" versus "Pharmacie Saint-Joseph" shares one content word and would not fire that pass. A legal-form list would mark some of the swapped tokens as noise, and it is an external lexicon. Kruse and coauthors' **0.9969** precision is classification of the legal-form tag, not linkage precision ([Kruse et al. 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352946513_Developing_a_Legal_Form_Classification_and_Extraction_Approach_for_Company_Entity_Matching_Benchmark_of_Rule-Based_and_Machine_Learning_Approaches)). Firm-level harmonization that collapses "Pfizer Limited" and "Pfizer Italia S.r.l." is the wrong decision for an establishment ([Terrorizer](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12083v1)). Libpostal's ignorable-token dictionaries are out of scope ([libpostal v1.1](https://github.com/openvenues/libpostal/releases/tag/v1.1)).

Name-only scores that do have numbers answer a different question. On Cohen's business-name collection, as rerun by Jimenez, Gonzalez, and Gelbukh, interpolated average precision is **0.529** for character bigrams, **0.704** for SoftTF-IDF, and **0.776** for their best soft-cardinality variant. On census personal names the order flips, and bigrams win ([Jimenez et al. 2010](http://www.gelbukh.com/CV/Publications/2010/Text%20Comparison%20Using%20Soft%20Cardinality.pdf)). SoftTF-IDF counts two tokens as a soft match only when Jaro–Winkler is at least about **0.9**. "Collège" versus "Pharmacie" fails that inner test. What remains is the IDF of the surviving tokens, which can be computed from the file in hand and is not a gazetteer. No paper retrieved here reports precision for that score, or for edit distance of the single substituted token, on same-address pairs. Using a large edit as a hard veto would, on the business-name evidence, point the wrong way for true descriptor swaps. Splink's shipped name comparison has no level for "all tokens agree except one." Its reversed-columns level is forename and surname order, a different error ([Splink comparisons](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/comparisons/out_of_the_box_comparisons.html)).

No retrieved system wins by overturning a language model's non-match when a string score is high. Peeters and Bizer use Jaccard only to pick demonstrations, and they raise zero-shot F1 by up to **7.85%** that way. Writing rules into the prompt raises F1 by **6** points to **88.29**, with recall held at **98%** and precision up by nearly **10** points. The model still decides ([Peeters and Bizer](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.03423)). That prompt is ChatGPT, outside the license and the parameter cap. ComEM stacks a smaller language model and a larger one. It does not publish a test of cutting the list with a string threshold, and the appendix remark that one could do so is unmeasured ([ComEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v1)). Zhang and coauthors never combine a string score with the language-model decision, and on same-title negatives the larger cross-encoder's precision falls from **96.9** to **76.4**. Scaling makes false matches worse. That shift is lookalike titles, not a one-token descriptor swap, so it does not by itself decide this slice. It does forbid a blanket "high string similarity means match" rule, and it forbids "use a larger model to override the veto." A policy "if string similarity is at least t, ignore the judge" has no published F1 in the sources read here. The labelled stand-in is the team's measurement that vetoes in the top probability band were true matches when the judge had not seen the country. It is not a published winning architecture, and the entity count already says it cannot close the public gap.

Ignoring the judge on a label-free slice is also not a theorem with an F0.5 attached. Label-shift corrections such as black-box shift estimation are consistent when the label prior changes and the class-conditional stays fixed. If the true-positive rate moves as well, the source confusion matrix is the wrong map ([Lipton, Wang, and Smola](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lipton18a.html)). One observed veto rate is a single equation in the match prior and the match and non-match agreement rates. It cannot separate them. Fellegi–Sunter needs several comparison fields before those rates are estimable together, and Winkler reports that the match-conditional agreement probability "can vary substantially from one region of the U.S. to another" ([Winkler, Census RRS2006-02](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2006/adrm/rrs2006-02.pdf)). The US **0.967** and India **0.734** figures on this slice are already that instability inside the labelled countries. The stand-in's **0.044** and **0.000** show the unseen-country veto rate has moved again. Constant-likelihood recalibration of the judge is not identified, because the true-veto rate moves between countries and between dev and test. Do not reopen it. Ben-David and coauthors' domain bound has a joint-error term that needs target labels. Unlabelled divergence does not say the source expert is wrong ([Ben-David et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-009-5152-4)). A country absent from training is a novelty flag in the reject-option sense, and the 2024 survey does not prove the expert's error rose and reports no entity-resolution F-score ([Hendrickx et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-024-06534-x)). Learning to defer to an unseen expert needs a demonstration set of that expert's decisions. The cited gains, up to **28%** relative accuracy on medical images and up to **91.3%** fewer expert labels, are not macro F0.5 and are not label-free ([Tailor et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02683); [conformal deferral](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12573v3)). Masking or clipping the judge is closed.

No result found here fits a threshold on the worse of two source groups and shows that this threshold is right for an unseen group. Jones and coauthors prove a case in which every threshold fails the worse group: if the margin distributions are log-concave and one is a translation of the other, worst-group selective accuracy is at most the group-agnostic reference at every non-negative threshold. On CelebA, worst-group accuracy falls as the model abstains more ([Jones et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14134)). DomainBed's training-domain validation, the practical version of "tune on the sources, test on a hidden domain," leaves ERM at **52.0±0.1** on ColoredMNIST, the dataset where the unseen domain flips the source correlation ([DomainBed](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.01434)). A plain threshold can swap the same way on a much smaller scale: in the CLEF re-identification paper, validation prefers **0.6** and the private split prefers **0.75**, with private scores **0.618** versus **0.629** ([Fedorchenko and Arefiev](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4038/paper_240.pdf)). Copying the US threshold, or the worse of the US and India, onto the unlabelled country is unmeasured, and the assumption it needs is the one that has already failed. The empty prediction is not the published precision lever either. The optimal-threshold theorem is for F1. A calibrated score is called positive when it is at least half the F1 the rule will achieve, and an uninformative classifier maximizes expected F1 by predicting every example positive ([Lipton, Elkan, and Naryanaswamy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.1892)). F0.5 weights precision more, so the threshold moves up relative to F1. "Moves up" is not "emit the empty set," and no paper found here shows that returning an empty match set, scored as **1.0** when the gold set is empty, is what maximizes macro F0.5. NIL detectors in entity linking are the nearest cousin. They need a trained NIL class or a labelled threshold, and their gains are out-of-KB F1, including about **30–40%** over a rule baseline, not this metric ([BLINKout](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07189)). On the matching contest, one entrant's predict-nothing baseline scores **0.0558** macro F0.5 because singletons are about **5.6%** and a matched reference has several links ([DevanshSrajput README](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge)). That is their fold, not this file. It is enough to refuse "when in doubt, predict nothing" as a winning architecture. Whether a new F0.5 threshold on this stacker would beat **0.985** is unmeasured. The entrant sweep says a tenth of threshold is about a thousandth on a weaker cascade, and this team's dev-tuned files have already lost on the public board.

Dev versus the public board has a documented cousin and a documented opposite, and the team has already run the experiment. Teney, Lin, Oh, and Abbasnejad measure inverse correlation of in-distribution and out-of-distribution accuracy on real data, including across epochs and seeds, and conclude that picking the best in-distribution model misses the best shifted model ([Teney et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00613)). Miller and coauthors report the contradiction: strong positive correlation, with R² **0.995** on CIFAR-10.1 across 1,060 models ([Miller et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.04649v2)). Two observed inversions on this task are enough to stop using this dev set as a ranker. They are not a general inverse law, and they are not a reason to ignore the public file in favor of a story about methodology. No 2022–2026 entity-resolution winner was found who wrote that validation ranked their submissions backward and that they therefore shipped a different system. SIGMOD 2020 is the nearest primary fact: labelled F **0.96** and **0.9826** collapsed to secret F **0.47**, and the winners shipped rules. That is a change of method class after a collapse, not a choice between two strong submissions ([Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)). The non-linkage contests where someone wrote "ignore CV" had six training rows or a public blend that fell from 3rd to 22nd. They do not apply.

## Conclusion

The applicability test is a count, not a model class. The unlabelled country is **15%** of Source 1, which is large enough for macro F0.5 to feel, and the only rule aimed at it changes **8,321** entities, which the team has already bounded as too few to close five thousandths. Every published margin that looks like a win sits on the other side of that test. Rules beat a neural matcher when the matcher had collapsed to F **0.47** and the identity was a catalog key. A tiny embedding won a blocking contest at about half recall. A dictionary beat an encoder by less than a thousandth of IoU on a different task. Extra retrieval after **97.7%** recall bought tenths of a point of pair completeness. A 4B cross-encoder tied an 8B cross-encoder on English pair F1 and, under the shift the authors actually built, the larger model became more willing to say match. None of those scores are macro F0.5 on a candidate set that is already this complete.

What still applies is the constraint the file already implements, and the closures around it. Keep one owner per Source 2/3 record, and do not import transitive closure. Keep the public **0.985** file as the control, because the two uploads that beat its dev score lost on the board. Do not refit on a proxy shift, do not recalibrate a veto rate when the true-positive rate is what moved, and do not spend the parameter cap on an 8B matcher the English macro does not pay for. The stand-in says the unseen-country vetoes in the top band were true matches. That is a direction, not a win: the add-back's public effect is unmeasured, its dev effect is **0.990306**, and no paper publishes a precision for the one-token rule that would turn those **8,506** pairs into a leaderboard result. Until a number on the public board says otherwise, the cascade at **0.985** is the architecture the published winners have not been shown to beat.
