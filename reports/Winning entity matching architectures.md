# Preserve the cascade and recalibrate France

The contest this linkage actually resembles is Kaggle Foursquare Location Matching in 2022, and the architecture that still stood after the leak was removed was a cascade — complementary blockers, a string-feature gradient-boosted tree, a multilingual cross-encoder, then a cluster repair — not a single network. On the organizer’s non-overlapping rescore the written-down ceiling is **0.932782** mean row-wise IoU for an unpublished team and **0.920594–0.922937** for the published cascades; the official **0.977683** is a train/test identity join, not a model ([organizer rerun](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/338035)). Academic pairwise SOTA on the official dirty Magellan candidate files is still **Ditto (2020)**, a BERT-class cross-encoder; under an Apache-2.0 and at-most-8B cap, a 2026 factorial study finds a **Qwen3-4B cross-encoder** tied with the 8B cross-encoder at macro F1 **84.1** and ahead of an 8B bi-encoder, with a generative head winning only when the schema itself shifts ([Qwen3 entity-matching study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24688)). A classical similarity learner still ties clean citation data, wins three of six sets in a 2026 LightGBM-versus-DeepMatcher tournament, and is the matcher inside the only system shown at tens of millions of name-like records. For this macro-F0.5 job — **1.7 million** duplicate-free reference businesses, a mean of about **3.4** Source 2/3 matches or none, fields limited to name, address, and country, US and India labeled, France not — the system to keep is the cascade already at about **0.985** on the public leaderboard. The change worth making is to stop the second tree from borrowing French recall from a judge that over-rejects there. Blanking that judge, treating a pairwise F1 winner as this leaderboard, and the orphan refit at **0.98476** are the moves the measurements rule out.

## Foursquare’s 2022 cascade is the contest this task resembles

No 2023–2026 company, customer, or POI contest with a documented winner turned up. The near analog is Foursquare (14 April–7 July 2022, 1,079 teams): one-to-many identity among “over one-and-a-half million” noisy place rows, scored as mean per-row Jaccard of match sets, with self-matches required and clusters written symmetrically ([competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching)). It is not macro F0.5, it has latitude and longitude this task does not, and it has no held-out country. It is still the only large noisy name-and-address leaderboard whose placed systems were written down. Shopee Price Match Guarantee (2021) is the next-closest decision-layer analog and a weak blocking analog. The SIGMOD programming contests of 2020 and 2021 are small catalogs whose identity is a brand-and-model key. SIGMOD 2022 is blocking only.

Official Foursquare first place (re:waiwai) built about 40 candidates from 100 geographic neighbors and 100 name-embedding neighbors, already at a stated max IoU of **0.979**, then a LightGBM on roughly 120 string features (CV **0.875**), then CatBoost (CV **0.878**) and two cross-encoders, the heavier `mdeberta-v3-base` at CV **0.907**. The blend weighted that DeBERTa-class model **0.38**, XLM-RoBERTa-large **0.29**, CatBoost **0.32**, and LightGBM **0.01**, reaching CV **0.911**, and a 50%-overlap merge moved CV to **0.9166** ([1st-place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution)). The same writeup’s leak, a join on name and coordinates, moved one stage-2 submission from **0.900** to **0.971**. Their non-overfit stack went from **0.941** without that join to **0.977** with it, on a test that still overlapped train. Fourth place’s leak alone moved **0.939** to **0.957** ([4th-place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/youri-vincent-th-o-4th-place-solution)). Once the organizer rescored the non-overlapping files, re:waiwai’s **0.977683** became **0.920594** and Psi’s neural-only **0.967847** became **0.919698**. Psi then sits about a thousandth behind re:waiwai and about three thousandths behind a LightGBM-plus-encoder-plus-GNN cascade at **0.922937** ([discussion 338035](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/338035)). The GNN’s author called about **+0.02** the important honest gain, an order smaller than the leak ([merge-master writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/team-merge-master-13th-place-solution-gnn)). Reading **0.97** as evidence for CatBoost plus two large encoders is the mistake. The honest 2022 band that was published is about **0.92–0.93** mean IoU, and Ri’s extra hundredth has no architecture attached.

Inside that honest band, model family is a small lever and candidate generation is a hard ceiling. Second place reached max IoU **0.9895** with geographic neighbors unioned with name TF-IDF, then still **0.986** at **4.1** candidates per id after a transformer gate, while a single blocking model’s leaderboard score was **0.918** — most of the remaining loss was precision and cluster assembly, not pairs the blocker never proposed ([2nd-place blocking notes](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336072)). An 11th-place trail starts at **0.922** from one multilingual BERT blocker and ends at **0.947** after more encoders, imputation, and a Dijkstra repair, a **+0.025** path in which the graph step is **+0.002** ([MCD3](https://www.mcd3.co.jp/blog/techblog/so-nakashima-4sq)). First place’s own cross-validation shows the same shape: about **+0.03** from a weak LightGBM to `mdeberta-v3-base`, then **+0.004** from ensembling, then **+0.0056** from the overlap merge. Second place did put the transformer probability into the gradient-boosted tree’s feature list, which is the contest version of a neural score inside a GBDT, but they published no ablation of that feature against the transformer alone, and their headline jump was the leak ([2nd-place stack](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336090)).

Shopee says the same thing on a different metric and a much smaller set (hidden test on the order of 70,000 images, groups capped at 50). Private **0.780**, **0.779**, and **0.779** separated ArcFace neighborhood blending, a LightGBM-plus-GAT reranker, and a secondhand CatBoost stack ([leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/leaderboard)). The winner’s development trail, which must not be quoted as the private score, moves from a full-data embedding model at **0.757** to **0.776** by unioning image, text, and combined matches, then to **0.793** by iterative neighborhood blending and two thresholds they say were the major remaining boost ([1st-place discussion](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238136)). Second place restricted pairs to the union of 50 image and 50 text neighbors; a GAT then moved a development score only from **0.790** to **0.792** ([2nd-place discussion](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238022)). Eighteenth place still took silver at **0.749** with ResNet-18 held to the same decision code, and called a group-size histogram match (**0.739** to **0.759**) a larger single change than upgrading the towers ([18th-place discussion](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/discussion/237972)).

SIGMOD 2020 is the result that does not transfer. Human rules reached F **0.99** (precision **0.99**, recall **0.98**) while “existing state-of-the-art ML-based methods” did not reach **0.49**, on about 30,000 camera specifications whose identity is a brand and a model ([Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)). The official winner was the fastest high-F entry, mock labels, not a learned ranker ([Blacher et al.](http://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf)). SIGMOD 2021’s published winner is the same idea: regex extraction, alias maps, and exact ids ([winner repository](https://github.com/yinpeiqi/SIGMOD-2021-program-contest)). Citing either contest against a transformer on abbreviated business names repeats a result that the Foursquare winners had already left behind. SIGMOD 2022 is the blocking lesson only: with a 35-minute CPU box, 16 cores, and 32 GB, a distilled transformer plus FAISS plus a Jaccard rerank beat a pure similarity join, and even the winner, on a secondhand figure, kept only about half of true pairs inside a one-to-two-million-pair budget ([Mannheim announcement](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/); [11th-place README](https://github.com/theodoratrz/sigmodContest2022)). No placed system in this set is an 8B model or a commercial entity-resolution API. Foursquare and Shopee ran offline, with public encoders in the hundreds of millions of parameters plus a gradient-boosted tree, because the binding constraints were RAM and a few hours of inference ([Foursquare rules](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching)). An 8B Apache cap is looser than the box those teams actually won in. It does not license a hosted LLM.

## Dirty pairwise SOTA is still Ditto, and trees still tie clean fields

There is no 2024–2026 model that is SOTA on every standard entity-matching set, and almost every neural F1 below is **pairwise F1 on a pre-blocked candidate file** of a few hundred to a few tens of thousands of pairs. That is not end-to-end macro F0.5 on 1.7 million businesses. Treating a benchmark F1 winner as the winner of this leaderboard is a category error. On the canonical fully supervised Magellan splits, the reference numbers are still Ditto, a fine-tuned BERT, DistilBERT, or RoBERTa cross-encoder from 2020: dirty Walmart–Amazon **85.69**, textual Abt–Buy **89.33**, textual Company **93.85** ([Ditto](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00584)). The same paper’s dirty Walmart–Amazon gap versus the classical Magellan matcher is **85.69** against **37.4**, and versus the best pre-transformer neural matcher **53.8**. Dirty iTunes–Amazon is **95.65** against Magellan **46.8**. Textual Abt–Buy is **89.33** against **43.6**. Those are the numbers that justify a transformer, and they are numbers about product titles and shuffled columns.

On clean, aligned, high-overlap fields the same classical matcher does not lose in any way that should reorder a production stack. Structured Fodors–Zagats is a tie at **100**. Structured DBLP–ACM is Magellan **98.4** versus Ditto **98.99**. Structured DBLP–Google Scholar is **92.3** versus **95.60**. The structured exceptions are product-like: Walmart–Amazon **71.9** versus **86.76**, Amazon–Google **49.1** versus **75.58**. Turn summarization off on the long Company file and the transformer baseline falls to **41.00**, below Magellan **79.8**. Later fully supervised gains on the official dirty files are fractional or unreproduced: Unicorn’s structured Walmart–Amazon is **86.93** against Ditto **86.76**, and it loses Beer by about seven points ([Unicorn](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3588938)). A 2026 compilation’s AttendEM **86.29** on dirty Walmart–Amazon and SupCon **94.29** on Abt–Buy come with an explicit failed-reproduction warning and should not be quoted as established SOTA ([Bamberg thesis](https://fis.uni-bamberg.de/bitstreams/841b3b7a-9870-4155-aaa8-5aa50a0ff1ea/download)). HierGAT, Sudowoodo, CSGAT, and CollaborEM do not take the fully supervised lead. Sudowoodo is a small-label method; with 500 labels its Abt–Buy F1 is **81.1**, under full-data Ditto **89.33** ([Sudowoodo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04122)). No 2023–2026 paper in this review establishes a public transformer SOTA on dirty business name plus street address. Ditto’s **96.5** F1 on two private company tables of **789k** and **412k** rows is an existence proof that a cross-encoder was run near a million records, not a public split ([Ditto](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00584)).

Where labels exist and the schema is stable, the licensed recipe inside 8B parameters is a fine-tuned cross-encoder. The July 2026 Qwen3 factorial study, 1,215 fine-tunes on Magellan, Machamp, and WDC, is the head-to-head that matches the license: Qwen3 weights are Apache-2.0 ([Qwen3 technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)). Macro test F1, best variant per architecture, is bi-encoder **72.5 / 79.0 / 81.5** at 0.6B / 4B / 8B, cross-encoder **83.0 / 84.1 / 84.1**, generative **84.4 / 86.8 / 87.7** ([Qwen3 entity-matching study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24688)). An **8B bi-encoder still loses to a 0.6B cross-encoder**. Scaling the cross-encoder from 4B to 8B adds about **zero** macro F1. Their Ditto reimplementation macro is **73.2**, so the eleven-point cross-encoder gap is a within-paper delta on different splits from the 2020 table (their Walmart–Amazon Ditto is **85.5**, their cross-encoder 4B is **91.6**, their generative 8B is **90.6**). Whether **91.6** beats original Ditto **86.76** on the official file is unmeasured. The generative lead is concentrated on schema-heterogeneous Machamp sets: Semi-Heter books, generative 8B **91.5** versus cross-encoder 8B **65.1**, mostly a recall gap (cross-encoder recall stays near **43–51**, generative recall **84.8**). On aligned fields the two heads are close, and the cross-encoder is the precision-heavy one. A **0.6B** cross-encoder already runs near **473** pairs per second in that study. Llama-3.1-8B is out of scope: the license cited for it is the Llama community license, not Apache-2.0 or MIT, even though in-domain fine-tuning moves its WDC F1 from **53.36** to **69.19** on a non-identical test ([fine-tuning study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08185)).

Zero-shot giants do not overturn that recipe once target labels exist, and they are illegal here as APIs. MatchGPT’s best-prompt GPT-4 F1 values are **89.61** (hard WDC), **95.78** (Abt–Buy), **89.67** (Walmart–Amazon), **76.38** (Amazon–Google), **89.82** (DBLP–Scholar), **98.41** (DBLP–ACM), against in-domain Ditto **84.90, 91.31, 86.39, 80.07, 94.31, 99.00** ([MatchGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11244)). The best-LLM-minus-best-PLM deltas are **+4.71, +4.47, +2.65, −3.69, −4.49, −0.33**. The same Ditto, trained on another dataset and tested on WDC, falls by about **36 to 56** F1. AnyMatch’s leave-one-out GPT-2 (124M, MIT) posts mean F1 **81.96** against MatchGPT GPT-4 **86.36** and a transfer Ditto **66.05**, then collapses on the hard product sets: Walmart–Amazon **61.51** versus **89.67**, WDC **63.31** versus **85.83** ([AnyMatch](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04073v1)). Those means are not comparable to in-domain Ditto. The EDBT 2025 claim that AnyMatch on LLaMA-3.2 matches GPT-4 on average uses a non-Apache base, and the per-dataset table was not extracted ([EDBT 2025](http://dx.doi.org/10.48786/edbt.2025.75)). ComEM’s pipeline — retrieve ten neighbors, then a ranker, then a selector LLM — reports mean F1 **86.42** on **400** queries, against a Ditto of **80.69** on that different candidate distribution; its Walmart–Amazon Ditto cell is **57.75**, which does not refute the 2020 file’s **86.76** ([ComEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v3)).

A gradient-boosted tree still wins or ties where the match is an aligned string rather than a paraphrase buried in a product title. The Magellan column in Ditto is a classical learner on similarity features, not a named LightGBM rerun, and no 2023–2026 paper re-benchmarks LightGBM against Qwen3 on the dirty Magellan files. The 2026 tournament that does run LightGBM (attribute features, at most 10,000 training records, LLM used only as a labeler) splits the board: LightGBM pair-F1 **0.969** versus DeepMatcher **0.948** on Restaurants, **0.541** versus **0.160** on DBLP–Scholar, **0.993** versus **0.992** on NCV; DeepMatcher wins Cora **0.968** versus **0.891**, Geo Settlements **0.979** versus **0.960**, and MB 200K **0.964** versus **0.948**. Each family wins three of six. A GAT wins none ([tournament preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26298v1)). MERAI, the Commonwealth Bank pipeline, is the scale result: blocking, Magellan token and edit features, a supervised classifier among trees, naive Bayes, SVM, XGBoost, and random forests, then a high threshold. The paper does not say which classifier won, so “a GBDT beat Ditto” is not a result — Ditto was not in the comparison, and the matcher is itself a model on GBDT-style features. What it does show is pairwise precision **100%** and recall **95.0 / 92.5 / 93.7** on three North Carolina voter deduplications (F1 **97.4 / 94.1 / 96.7**), linkage F1 **98.3 / 97.3 / 96.2**, a run at **15.7 million** records, and bank jobs to **33 million**, while Dedupe failed past about **2–3 million** ([MERAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03767v1)). Splink on the same voter tasks is the precision failure in dedup (**60–76.5%** precision) and the recall failure in linkage (**78.3–85.2%**). No paper in this review trains or selects on F-beta with beta below 1. Precision-heavy behavior shows up as MERAI’s threshold, not as a different network.

Name and address at a million rows are won or lost in blocking before any of those F1 values apply. On Papadakis et al.’s dirty synthetic scale test, standard blocking at **2 million** entities holds pair completeness **0.81** and candidate precision about **0.55–0.60**; FAISS falls to recall **0.67** and precision **0.018**, ScaNN to **0.70** and **0.02**, and DeepBlocker does not get past **100,000** entities ([filtering benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12521)). Sorted neighborhood was implemented and dropped. Syntactic blocking beat semantic embeddings for filtering because entity vocabularies are full of out-of-vocabulary tokens; embeddings help the matcher. Those dense indexes are fastText-era, not Qwen3. Whether a modern bi-encoder would move that recall at 2 million dirty records is unmeasured. Sparkly’s recall@10 of **86.57–99.96%** is on small clean benchmarks ([ComEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v3)). MERAI’s index emitted on the order of **10^8** pairs from multi-million voter files and did not publish pair completeness, so the fraction of true duplicates it dropped is unknown.

## France is a shift the papers never scored as over-rejection

The measured language-shift results are product matching inside Latin script, not business name plus address, and not a held-out country. No paper reports a US-and-India to France drop, a macro F0.5, or an entity-matching number for E5 or BGE-M3 under a language holdout. France shares a script with the US side of this training distribution, so the nearest analogue is English-to-French or English-to-Portuguese product matching, not an Indic-script break. On German phone offers, 1,800 German pairs give mBERT F1 **87.69**, German BERT **73.43**, XLM-R **73.40**, and English BERT **65.27**; adding English pairs helps the monolingual English encoder by **9** points and barely moves mBERT ([Peeters and Bizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03338)). On a student benchmark where French and Spanish offers are test-only, in-language transformer F1 sits near **0.95–0.97**, zero-shot pairwise transformers fall to **0.82–0.91** on toys and **0.69–0.85** on phones, and English plus German labels with no French labels recover **5** pairwise F1 on French toys and **7** on French phones ([Mannheim cross-lingual project](https://data.dws.informatik.uni-mannheim.de/Web-based-Systems-Group/StudentProjects/2020/Cross-lingual-Product-Matching-using-Transformers/)). One fully visible pairwise cell, English-trained mBERT on Spanish toys, is **0.8214** against a Magellan baseline **0.6581**. English WDC products matched to Brazilian Portuguese invoices are harsher on the wrong encoder: zero-shot mBERT **78.9**, English BERT **62.5**, XLM-R **57.8**; about **600** Portuguese pairs bring every model to about **90**, and XLM-R jumps **+32.6** to **90.4** ([Alves et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3605098.3636001)). The pattern is stable. A multilingual encoder drops and then recovers once any target-language labels exist. This task has no French labels, so that recovery is unavailable. Character and edit features are the piece that does not need a French lexicon. They are a floor, not the winner, on the one product cell where both were scored. They are also the features MERAI actually trusts at voter scale. A pure English encoder is the component the product studies show falling over.

The shift the LLM papers did measure is unseen products and unseen datasets, and the failure mode is not over-rejection. Fine-tuned Ditto and RoBERTa drop tens of F1 points on products absent from training; zero-shot GPT-4, which was never fit to the source shop, beats the best transferred PLM by **34** F1 on that WDC slice ([MatchGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11244)). Fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B on WDC raises in-domain F1 from **53.36** to **69.19** and lowers DBLP–ACM from **85.52** to **74.52**; every fine-tuning recipe in that study underperformed Llama’s own zero-shot on the cross-domain sets, and precision was not split from recall ([fine-tuning study](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08185v1)). Specializing that 8B model on embedding-neighbors of its own errors is the best in-domain WDC number in the table, **74.37** (**+5.18**), and one of the worst cross-domain results, **−12.53** and **−12.08** F1 on the two scholar sets versus ordinary fine-tuning. Dropping the pairs a stronger model already gets wrong erases GPT-4o-mini’s fine-tune and lands at **77.06**, beside a **77.44** zero-shot. The Qwen3 study’s cross-dataset means go the same direction and name the error: off-diagonal F1 is **73.8** for generative matchers, **64.9** for cross-encoders, and **30.3** for bi-encoders, while scaling a cross-encoder from 0.6B to 8B under that shift drops precision from **96.9** to **76.4** at recall still near **99** ([Qwen3 entity-matching study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24688)). That is over-acceptance. False merges, not refusals. It is the expensive direction under F0.5.

The current judge’s over-rejection on France is therefore a team measurement, not a published law of language shift. It is compatible with a cross-encoder that the same Qwen3 paper already describes as precision-heavy when attributes move, and it is compatible with a LoRA fine-tune that has memorized US and Indian legal forms, street abbreviations, and transliteration habits. It is not evidence that a larger generative model will be better calibrated on French, and the published scaling result says a larger fine-tuned encoder becomes more willing to say match. Training the 4B judge only on the first LightGBM’s mistakes is the error-selection setup above: in-domain gain, cross-domain damage, plus the retrieval analogy that hard negatives mined from a blocker are contaminated with missed true matches ([Hard Negatives, Hard Lessons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16967)). AnyMatch’s practical countermeasure is a cap of two negatives per positive plus hard positives, not hard negatives alone ([AnyMatch](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04073v1)). Self-training on unlabeled French pairs is plausible and unmeasured; Sudowoodo’s pseudo-labels stay inside one dataset. No industrial system above claims a win over Ditto, and none reports a held-out country.

Blocking can still be country-scoped without French labels, because a business match across countries is the rare case, not the modeling problem. That is an inference, not a measured pair-completeness. What fails to transfer is a matcher whose token inventory was fit on US and Indian strings. Splink’s maintainers, on company name plus address, describe a different and fully measured trap: more than fifteen blocking rules were required so that name overlap was always present, after a default model assigned very high probability to different names at the same address, because correlated address fields stack their weights ([Splink discussion 1975](https://github.com/moj-analytical-services/splink/discussions/1975)). Fusing street and locality into one comparison is the supported fix. Adding a language-model score does not, by itself, repair a double-counted address.

## Many-to-one abstention is the decision macro F0.5 is scoring

Nothing in the literature runs independent thresholds against Hungarian assignment, connected components, and a rival-aware matcher under macro F0.5 at a mean degree of 3.4. The cardinality of this task is still readable from the systems that exist. Source 1 is duplicate-free. A Source 2 or Source 3 row should attach to at most one reference business. A reference may keep many source rows, or none. That is many-to-one. Papadakis et al. compared eight bipartite algorithms on ten clean-clean, one-to-one datasets: every method pushes precision above recall, unique-mapping clustering (greedy, both ends free) is the best or statistically tied for best F1, and connected components on the bipartite graph have precision almost twice recall and finish second-worst on F1 ([VLDB Journal 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00778-023-00791-3)). Exact Hungarian is excluded as cubic, and both Hungarian and unique mapping force degree one on the reference side. They would discard true extra matches beyond the first. The F1 gaps in that paper are small, and the paper does not recompute F0.5. `recordlinkage`’s one-to-many linker and Splink’s `cluster_using_single_best_links` — mark the reference duplicate-free, leave the source files unmarked, keep a link only if it is mutually best — are the primitives that match the constraint ([Splink clustering](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/api_docs/linker_clustering.html)). No paper reports their F1 or F0.5 against a plain threshold. Connected components remain the industrial default and the wrong constraint here: two distinct reference businesses that share a lookalike at one address become one cluster, which is the false merge macro F0.5 prices at twice a miss. MERAI refuses that glue on deduplication by splitting components into disjoint cliques, and on linkage it does not cluster at all ([MERAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03767v1)).

Rival context is the decision-layer result that does have a measured gain, and it is not a cardinality rule. GNEM scores one record jointly against its candidate set and reports up to **+8.4** F1 versus prior pairwise neural matchers, and **+4.39** F1 on textual Abt–Buy versus pairwise models ([GNEM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3442381.3450119)). ComEM’s listwise select prompt beats pairwise matching by **16.02** F1 on average in the v3 study, and by up to **8.15** F1 versus a compare prompt; selecting is best on **6 of 8** datasets. Mistral-7B-Instruct mean F1 moves from **45.91** (matching) to **73.80** (selecting), with position bias able to drop a true match that is not near the front of the list ([ComEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v3)). CaRL-EM’s learned choice among match, compare, and select is the closest “there may be zero matches” signal: its best macro of two F1s is **79.14** against Ditto **59.75**, and the “none” F1 is where match-style prompting is weak ([CaRL-EM](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01195v1)). That is not macro F0.5, and it emits a single identity or none, not a set of mean size 3.4. A 2026 engineering sketch at this scale — about **1.7 million** source-1 rows, macro F0.5, one LightGBM after a top-10 union — reports validation macro F0.5 **0.8899**, leaderboard **0.875**, and a near one-to-one constraint under which **99.81%** of matched records belong to exactly one source-1 entity ([Amazon-ML-Challenge sketch](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge)). Those scores are not this file’s **0.985**. Copying that sketch’s one-to-one rule onto a mean of **3.4** matches would throw away true links the metric still counts as recall. Copying its leaderboard number onto this cascade would invent a regression that was not measured.

Threshold search on validation F0.5 is ordinary classification practice. No entity-resolution bake-off shows it beating assignment. Hand’s practitioner’s note defines a beta-weighted F-score and says beta is usually 1 ([arXiv:1509.04238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.04238)). Wang et al. show that pairwise precision of a fixed threshold falls as the file grows, which is the relevant warning at 1.7 million rows ([arXiv:1509.03302](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.03302)). How macro F0.5 treats a reference whose gold set is empty is undefined in these papers. The task statement allows an empty prediction. A false merge on a true non-match is the error the weight of two is there to punish. Abstention when the string model and the judge disagree is the direction that weight implies. It is unmeasured as a formula on this leaderboard.

## Keep the five-stage cascade and stop borrowing recall from the judge

The current file — multi-key blocking plus a bi-encoder, a LightGBM on string and rival features, a LoRA pairwise judge around 4B parameters whose score is one feature in a second LightGBM, then a precision-heavy rule — already sits in the family that took the honest Foursquare band and that MERAI runs at 10^7 records. It scores about **0.985** publicly. A refit that helped an orphan development split scored **0.98476** and did not beat it. No opened paper reports this exact stack, so no paper can be cited for the second tree beating the judge alone. Foursquare’s official second place did feed a transformer probability into a gradient-boosted tree and did not show that the tree added anything once the leak was removed. The stack is a measured operating point on this leaderboard, not a published SOTA. Replacing it with Ditto’s 2020 F1, with MatchGPT’s GPT-4, or with a generative 8B head would optimize a different test, violate the API ban or spend a parameter step the Qwen3 study found worth about zero macro F1 on a cross-encoder, and has not been shown to move macro F0.5.

The first stage stays a union, and it is the stage every winner protected. Country-scoped standard blocking and hashed name and address keys, with street and locality fused so a shared building cannot vote twice, unioned with a multilingual bi-encoder used only as another candidate generator, then a cardinality cap. Pair completeness is tuned toward the **0.9** floor Papadakis et al. used. On their 2 million-entity dirty set that floor is already missed by standard blocking (pair completeness **0.81**, precision **0.55–0.60**), and q-gram blocking holds recall only by letting precision fall to about **0.001**. This is the Foursquare union (max IoU already **0.979–0.9895** before the heavy ranker), the SIGMOD 2022 pattern of a small neural index reranked by a symbolic score, Splink’s multi-rule union, and MERAI’s block-size cap. Dense nearest-neighbor search alone is the latency play that gave up recall at 2 million entities (**0.67–0.70** against standard blocking **0.81**). A learned bi-encoder as the only blocker is not what those million-scale experiments crown. Phone and house-number indexes are a natural extension of Splink’s premises rules and were not in the examples read; this schema does not offer them as separate fields. The recall ceiling of the union on this French slice is unmeasured.

The second stage is the recall owner, and it has to be able to stand without the judge. A LightGBM on lexicon-free string features — Jaro, Levenshtein, token ratios, character n-grams — plus rival features, at a low threshold. The string features are what tied clean Magellan files, what MERAI thresholded to **100%** precision at voter scale, what Foursquare’s stage-2 tree used to cut candidates, and what won three of six sets against DeepMatcher under a label cap, including the noisy bibliographic set. Rival features are the GNEM result (**+4.39** to **+8.4** F1) and ComEM’s listwise gain (**+16.02** F1 for selecting over pairwise matching) expressed as tree inputs rather than as a graph network. That encoding is the current system. It was not separately ablated in those papers. The 2026 single-tree sketch shows that stopping here, and then forcing one-to-one, is a different and weaker operating point (**0.875** on its own leaderboard). It does not show that the tree should be deleted once a 4B model exists. On this schema the three fields are short and aligned, which is the regime where a similarity learner stays close and where Ditto’s Company-file collapse (F1 **41** without summarization) does not apply. The tree is also the component whose features do not need a French lexicon. Leaving French recall entirely to a US/India LoRA judge contradicts both the language-shift tables and the team’s own over-rejection.

The third stage is one Apache-2.0 cross-encoder, Qwen3-4B, not a prompted API, not Llama-3.1-8B, and not an 8B generative model. The factorial study is the support: at equal size the cross-encoder beats the bi-encoder, 4B matches 8B on macro F1 when the schema is stable, and the generative head’s large win is Semi-Heter schema shift (**91.5** versus **65.1**), which three aligned columns are not. Qwen3-4B-Instruct is Apache-2.0 at **4.0B** parameters ([model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507)). LoRA versus a full fine-tune of that 4B cross-encoder was not given as an F1 table; the current LoRA judge is inside the **0.985** file, so swapping the adapter method is unmeasured. Fine-tune on US and India pairs with a capped negative ratio. Do not mine the training set down to the first tree’s mistakes if the hope is France: that selection gained **5.18** in-domain WDC F1 and lost about **12** F1 cross-domain. Do not blank the judge. The team measurement is that blanking it collapses recall because the second tree uses the score as a recall feature. A precision-heavy judge is useful under F0.5 exactly when something else still proposes the true pair. Deleting the score removes the proposals the tree had learned to trust, including on the countries where the judge is well calibrated. Replacing the judge with a bigger model is the Qwen3 scaling result in the wrong direction: under dataset shift, larger cross-encoders over-accept.

The fourth stage is the second LightGBM, kept because it is the scored system and not because a paper showed it beats the 4B model. The orphan refit is the Foursquare lesson at a much smaller delta: a development cut that is easier to move than the leaderboard crowned a change that lost a thousandth (**0.98476** against about **0.985**). France has no labels, so any refit selected on US/India orphan recall is being chosen on the wrong country. The one change that matches the stated failure is unmeasured and should be reported that way: give the stacker an explicit country interaction, or train it with the judge score sometimes masked, so a French true match that the string model already ranks highly cannot be vetoed by an over-rejecting judge, while a French lookalike at the same address can still be vetoed. Masking at inference only, with no retraining of the tree, is the blanking experiment and is already known to collapse recall. The literature’s alternative — let the 4B cross-encoder be the final classifier after the candidate cap, which is ComEM’s shape with a fine-tuned open model instead of a selector API — is also unmeasured against **0.985**. It is the right fallback if a masked-judge retrain cannot make the string features carry recall. It is not the first edit.

The fifth stage is a many-to-one rule aimed at macro F0.5, not a third model. Each Source 2/3 record links to at most one Source 1 id. Each Source 1 id may receive many links or none. The threshold is chosen for macro F0.5, not for F1. Connected components are not run across Source 1. Hungarian and unique one-to-one mapping are not used. Group-size-dependent thresholds, the idea fourth place on Foursquare actually kept, are unmeasured on this metric. So is the numerical effect of rival features once this cardinality constraint is already in force. Expectation under F-beta, as a utility, was not found as a winning entity-resolution layer.

Two comparisons should not be used to steer the edit. A single LightGBM at leaderboard **0.875** is a different decision rule on a write-up that forced near one-to-one matching; it is not a measurement of this cascade with the judge removed. Ditto’s dirty-Walmart gap of almost **50** F1 over Magellan is real and irrelevant to a file whose public score is already **0.985** on short aligned fields: that gap was measured on shuffled product attributes, at a few thousand pre-blocked pairs, under F1. The same paper’s clean citation ties are the closer academic analogue, and they say the tree stays.

## Conclusion

The **0.985** cascade is already the architecture the closest contest and the only ten-million-row name-like system both ended up running: several blockers, a string model that can abstain, a small cross-encoder, and a precision rule that does not glue reference records together. The literature’s attractive substitutions each attack a different error than a false merge on unlabeled French addresses. A generative 8B head spends its published advantage on schema heterogeneity this task does not have, and larger fine-tuned encoders under dataset shift become more willing to match, which F0.5 prices twice. A pairwise F1 leader on a pre-blocked product file never saw a candidate distribution whose precision is near **0.02**, never saw macro F0.5, and never saw a country with no labels. One-to-one assignment and the **0.875** engineering sketch both delete the extra matches a mean of **3.4** says are real. Connected components recreate the shared-address merge MERAI’s clique splitter was built to cut.

What the measurements change is the direction of the dependence inside a cascade that should otherwise be left alone. The judge over-rejects on the unseen country, and the second tree has learned to ask that judge for recall. Those two facts invert the only division of labor the papers support: lexicon-free string features hold the floor under language shift, and a fine-tuned cross-encoder spends its conservatism on precision. Reversing that dependence — string model proposes, judge disposes, country allowed to change the weight, empty allowed when they disagree — has no published macro F0.5. The control is the current file. The orphan refit’s **0.98476** is the evidence that a split one can see will bless an edit the public score does not.
