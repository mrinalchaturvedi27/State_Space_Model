# Label-free calibration for an unseen country under macro F0.5

Scope: one scorer, two labelled countries (US, India), one unlabelled country (France, same Latin script, unseen French legal forms and street words). Metric is macro F0.5. No target labels. Computable from train labels plus unlabelled test inputs only. Leaderboard search, external lexicons, commercial APIs, and self-training on test inputs are ruled out as the safe path (last item also because a one-round pseudo-label gain was followed by recall collapse in the task brief). Sagawa et al. group DRO is a training objective, not a threshold rule; it is not used below as a calibration method.

## What label-free calibration methods exist under F-beta or a precision constraint?

### Takeaway
The methods that need no target labels all buy their guarantee by assuming the target is only a covariate shift or a label shift of the source, or by treating an unlabelled score histogram as a two-class mixture. None of them directly maximises macro F0.5 on a harder country. The procedures that do control a precision-like risk (Fellegi–Sunter error bounds, conformal risk control) need either labelled target scores or a known likelihood ratio plus a monotone loss, and F0.5 is not monotone in the threshold.

### Cited Findings
- Fellegi–Sunter ranks pairs by \(R = P(\gamma \mid M) / P(\gamma \mid U)\) and uses two cutoffs: link if \(R > T_\mu\), clerical review if \(T_\lambda \le R \le T_\mu\), non-link if \(R < T_\lambda\). The cutoffs are set from a priori bounds on the false-match rate and the false-nonmatch rate. For any fixed pair of those bounds the rule minimises the clerical region. — [Winkler, Census rrs2007-05](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2007/adrm/rrs2007-05.pdf); [Census rr91-9 restating the 1969 theorem](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/1991/adrm/rr91-9.pdf)
- In linkage practice the false-match (Type 1) bound is set tighter than the false-nonmatch (Type 2) bound, because a false match biases later analysis and a miss can be recovered in a later pass. Undecided pairs go to clerical review, not to an automatic link. — [Shlomo, handbook chapter PDF](https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/id/fb2e9e86-c2ea-484b-82cd-2597c78e2486/1007164.pdf)
- Under conditional independence, the EM algorithm estimates the \(m\)- and \(u\)-probabilities with no labelled pairs (Winkler 1988). The same Census report states the disadvantage: CI-EM probabilities “often do not correspond accurately to underlying truth at differing error-rate levels” (Winkler 1993; Belin and Rubin 1995). Dropping CI can separate matches better, but the CI fit is what people use for speed. — [Winkler, Census rrs2007-05](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2007/adrm/rrs2007-05.pdf)
- Optimality of the likelihood-ratio rule “is heavily dependent on the accuracy of the estimates” of \(P(\gamma \mid M)\) and \(P(\gamma \mid U)\). On one Census-style test, at a 0.2% false-link cap, a 3-class independent EM designated 9808 links and a 3-way interaction EM designated 9601. — [Winkler, 1992 JSM proceedings](http://www.asasrms.org/Proceedings/papers/1992_140.pdf)
- The Fellegi–Sunter rule is “very effective for link identification but generally less reliable for result evaluation.” Specification error, including failure of conditional independence, “may cause loss of precision in the distributions estimates and consequently strong bias in the error estimates,” while the weight can still rank pairs correctly. — [Tuoto, 2016](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/e383532e-502a-15e8-e053-a505fe0a3de9/Tuoto_New-proposal_2016.pdf)
- In the static latent-class EM used for linkage, the algorithm can converge to the boundary (conditional probabilities 0 or 1) without a maximum inside the parameter space, because the likelihood is not strictly concave on a subspace of log-odds. Regularisation or priors are the usual patch. Applying a pair-wise Fellegi–Sunter rule inside clusters of more than two records is not supported by the original theory and can be logically impossible. — [Thibaudeau, WIREs Computational Statistics, 2025](https://wires.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wics.70015)
- Belin and Rubin (JASA 1995) fit a mixture of transformed normals to the weight histogram to estimate the false-match rate at each cutoff, with no training labels in the base strategy. In simulations that violated the model, the procedure was “slightly conservative on average in the sense of overstating false-match rates,” and one-sided interval coverage was close to nominal. That is an average over their simulation grid, not a guarantee for a new language. — [Belin and Rubin, JASA 1995](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01621459.1995.10476563)
- Label shift means \(p(y)\) changes and \(p(x \mid y)\) does not. Black-box shift estimation (BBSE) estimates test \(p(y)\) from a source confusion matrix. It is consistent for biased or uncalibrated predictors only if that confusion matrix is invertible and the label-shift assumption holds. — [Lipton, Wang, and Smola, ICML 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03916); [PMLR version](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lipton18a.html)
- Maximum-likelihood label-shift (MLLS) needs a calibrated classifier plus the same invertibility condition. Its finite-sample error splits into miscalibration plus estimation error. BBSE is the coarser, less efficient special case. — [Garg, Wu, Balakrishnan, and Lipton, NeurIPS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07554)
- LaSCal is a label-free calibration-error estimator and a post-hoc calibrator. The authors describe it as consistent only under label shift (\(p(y)\) changes, \(p(x \mid y)\) fixed), using importance weights. They present it as the only label-free consistent calibration-error estimator under that assumption in their comparison. — [Popordanoska, Radevski, Tuytelaars, and Blaschko, LaSCal](https://www.proceedings.com/content/079/079017-2088open.pdf)
- A later label-shift note states the modelling assumption explicitly: no concept drift, so \(p_t(x \mid y) = p_s(x \mid y)\). EM prior updates (the Saerens-style baseline they cite) need that invariance. — [LEIP note, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01651v1)
- Weighted conformal prediction gives a distribution-free interval when test and train covariates differ but \(P(Y \mid X)\) is the same, and the likelihood ratio \(d\tilde P_X / dP_X\) is known or estimated from unlabelled test covariates. — [Tibshirani, Barber, Candès, and Ramdas, NeurIPS 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019)
- If the ratio is wrong, or the shift is not covariate shift, that exact coverage does not transfer. Coverage bounds under covariate shift grow with a bound on the density ratio and with estimation error. High-dimensional ratios are hard to estimate even with unlabelled target points. — [Barber, Candès, Ramdas, and Tibshirani, conformal beyond exchangeability](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.13415v5); [multi-source conformal note, Sept 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14531); [training-conditional bounds under covariate shift](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16594v1)
- The same conformal family treats label shift as a different weighting problem from covariate shift (Podkopaev and Ramdas, as cited in the 2026 unification paper). It still assumes label shift, not a change in \(P(Y \mid X)\). — [Barber and Tibshirani, Electronic Journal of Statistics, 2026](https://projecteuclid.org/accountAjax/Download?downloadType=journal%20article&urlId=10.1214%2F26-EJS2513&isResultClick=True)
- Conformal risk control bounds \(\mathbb{E}[\ell(C(X_{n+1}), Y_{n+1})] \le \alpha\) for a loss that is monotone in the size of the prediction set, using a labelled calibration sample. The covariate-shift extension still needs the likelihood ratio. The canonical set-valued loss in their release is a false-negative proportion, which shrinks as the set grows. — [Angelopoulos, Bates, Fisch, Lei, and Schuster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814); [code notes](https://github.com/aangelopoulos/conformal-risk/blob/main/README.md)
- Prediction-powered inference corrects a predictor with a small gold sample drawn from the same population as the large unlabelled sample, and returns valid confidence intervals for means, quantiles, and regression parameters. It does not assume a particular model class. An extension to known covariate shift or label shift is noted in follow-on guides; the base theorem is same-population. — [Angelopoulos, Bates, Fannjiang, Jordan, and Zrnic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633); [Science summary](https://www.science.org/doi/full/10.1126/science.adi6000); [Demystifying PPI, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20819v1)
- For F1, if scores are calibrated probabilities, the threshold that maximises F1 on a batch is half the F1 that this same rule achieves. The optimal call for one row depends on the score distribution of the whole batch, not only on that row. If the scorer is uninformative, the F1-optimal rule labels every row positive. Macro averaging then pushes rare, weakly predicted labels toward very low thresholds. The authors say the phenomena extend to \(F_\beta\), but the proved all-positive theorem is for F1. Empirical threshold search on a validation fold has a winner’s curse: at low prevalence a badly high threshold can beat the true optimum with probability that does not shrink in \(n\) until \(n\) is very large. — [Lipton, Elkan, and Naryanaswamy, ECML PKDD 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4442797/)
- Open-world threshold calibration (OpenGCN) predicts pairwise connectivity on the unlabelled test graph and reads TPR/TNR off that graph at several distance cutoffs. It is aimed at visual embeddings with disjoint test classes, not at record linkage, and it does not claim an F0.5 guarantee. — [arXiv:2305.12039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12039)
- Selective classification abstains on low-confidence inputs. Most of that literature assumes the deployment distribution equals the training distribution. A 2024 TMLR paper treats label-shifted and covariate-shifted points as well, and proposes margin scores for deep classifiers. It does not give a record-linkage threshold. — [Liang, Peng, and Sun, TMLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05160)
- Post-hoc calibration fit on in-domain validation (temperature scaling) is well calibrated on i.i.d. test data and can be worse than no calibration once the data are shifted. Uncertainty quality falls as shift grows, for every method they tried. A threshold on predictive entropy does not cleanly separate shifted data from in-distribution data. — [Ovadia et al., NeurIPS 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.02530)
- Group DRO minimises worst-group training loss. On over-parameterised nets it does nothing without strong regularisation, because zero average training loss already implies zero worst-group training loss. Reported gains (about 10–40 points of worst-group accuracy on NLI and two vision tasks) come from training with group labels plus \(\ell_2\) or early stopping. The paper does not state a post-hoc threshold rule for an unlabelled group. — [Sagawa, Koh, Hashimoto, and Liang, ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08731)

### Inferences
- Four label-free families are actually on offer, and they answer different questions. (1) Marginal matching: choose the cutoff so France’s mean number of matches per reference, its empty-set rate, or its score histogram matches the US/India values. This is label-shift prior correction (BBSE, MLLS, Saerens-style EM) or a quantile match of scores. (2) Worst-source threshold: pick the cutoff by leave-one-country scores on the labelled countries only (next section). (3) Conformal or prediction-powered intervals: these need either exchangeable labelled calibration points from the target, or a correctly specified covariate/label shift. (4) Abstention: Fellegi–Sunter’s middle band, or a selective classifier. The contest scores a set, not a “clerical” label, so the band has to be mapped to link or non-link.
- F0.5 is a poor fit to conformal risk control. Raising the threshold does not move F0.5 monotonically: precision usually rises and recall falls. Risk control applies to a monotone proxy (for example a false-match count that shrinks as the linked set shrinks), and only with labelled calibration data or a known density ratio. It does not, by itself, return the F0.5-maximising threshold.
- PPI does not become available just because France inputs are unlabelled and US/India labels exist. The gold sample has to be from the same population as the unlabelled sample, unless the shift is a known covariate or label shift. US/India labels are the wrong population for a France interval.
- CI-EM on France pairs is the classical unsupervised calibrator and the one the linkage literature most clearly warns against for error rates. Ranking can still be usable when the estimated false-match curve is biased (Tuoto). Setting the F0.5 cutoff from that curve is the step that fails. Belin–Rubin’s “slightly conservative” average is not a reason to fit their mixture on France: it was conservative when the weight law was close to a transformed-normal mixture, and a harder country is exactly the case where non-match weights move into the match region and a two-component fit understates false matches.
- Abstention is safe only as a non-link. There is no scored third decision. Mapping the uncertain band to non-link is the Fellegi–Sunter precision-first default (tighter false-match bound, clerical rather than automatic link). Mapping it to link is the over-merge. An all-empty submission is not that default: the task brief scores a correct empty set at 1.0, but an empty prediction on a reference that has matches has recall 0, and \(F_\beta = 0\) whenever recall is 0 and the usual formula is applied. That empty-versus-nonempty convention was not found in the papers above; it is the definition of \(F_\beta\), plus the brief’s statement about correct empties.
- Lipton, Elkan, and Naryanaswamy’s all-positive rule is for F1, not F0.5. For a low match prevalence, predicting every candidate positive makes precision collapse, which F0.5 penalises more than F1. Do not import the F1 plug-in. Do import the winner’s-curse warning: a threshold that wins on one finite country fold can be the wrong side of the grid, so a single direction’s argmax is a noisy estimate.
- OpenGCN-style transductive calibration would try to invent France labels by predicting edges on the unlabelled graph. That is a learned pseudo-label, not a guarantee. If the connectivity model is source-trained, it has the same failure mode as copying the source match rate. It is not the safe one-day method.
- Self-training on France inputs, leaderboard probes, and external French lexicons are out of scope for the safe recommendation. Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz also flag oracle tuning on the test domain as not a valid model-selection method, even when the number of test queries is capped. — [Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz, ICLR 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.01434)

### Gaps
- No source found that proves a finite-sample bound on macro F0.5 for a threshold fit with zero labels from a country that is harder than every source. The conformal and label-shift theorems stop where \(P(Y \mid X)\) or \(P(X \mid Y)\) changes.
- No 2020–2026 survey was found that treats marginal matching, worst-group thresholds, conformal risk control, and Fellegi–Sunter EM as one catalogue. The pieces sit in different literatures.
- The score this contest assigns to an incorrect empty set was not found in a paper. Only the brief states that a correct empty set scores 1.0.
- Sagawa et al. do not yield a threshold rule. Worst-group validation accuracy is a common selection practice around that literature, but this pass did not find a theorem that says “take the min across source countries’ F0.5 curves.”

## Which methods survive when the target is harder than every source, and how should the worse leave-one-country direction set the threshold?

### Takeaway
Copying the source match rate, the source empty rate, or the source score histogram is valid only under label shift or a stable score-to-label map. If France is harder than both labelled countries, those copies over-merge. Weighted conformal and prediction-powered inference do not apply either, because they need \(P(Y \mid X)\) unchanged or gold labels from France. The conservative labelled-data rule is to maximise the worse leave-one-country F0.5, not the average. That rule protects the weaker labelled country. It is not a lower bound for France.

### Cited Findings
- Leave-one-domain-out cross-validation trains on all but one source domain, scores the held-out domain, and selects the hyperparameter that maximises the average of those held-out scores, then retrains on all sources. The stated assumption is that domains are draws from a meta-distribution and the goal is expected performance under that meta-distribution. Training-domain validation instead assumes train and test are similar. Test-domain validation is an oracle and is not a valid benchmark method. — [Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz, ICLR 2021, §3.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.01434)
- In those experiments, training-domain validation beat leave-one-domain-out on average, and oracle test-domain selection was about 2 points higher still. Model selection changed results as much as the choice of domain-generalisation algorithm. With a shared protocol, no included algorithm beat ERM by more than about one point on average. — [Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz, ICLR 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.01434)
- Without target labels, domain adaptation can be impossible. Covariate shift alone is not enough: either the unlabelled distributions must be close, or some hypothesis must have small error on both domains. The negative results hold for every learner that sees source labels and target inputs but no target labels. Reweighting source examples by the target covariate density is not trustworthy: there exist tasks that are indistinguishable from the learner’s inputs, yet reweighting helps one and causes “dramatic deterioration” on the other. — [Ben-David, Lu, Luu, and Pál, AISTATS 2010](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/david10a.html)
- BBSE and LaSCal estimate or correct for a new \(p(y)\) only when \(p(x \mid y)\) is unchanged. If the class-conditional feature law changes, the source confusion matrix is the wrong instrument and the estimator is not the one their consistency proofs cover. — [Lipton, Wang, and Smola](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03916); [LaSCal](https://www.proceedings.com/content/079/079017-2088open.pdf); [Garg et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07554)
- Temperature scaling fit on in-domain data does not stay calibrated under shift and can make Brier score worse than the uncalibrated model. — [Ovadia et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.02530)
- A threshold that is optimal for F1 is a property of the whole score batch (Lipton, Elkan, and Naryanaswamy, Theorem 1). A cutoff that was right for one country’s score distribution is not implied to be right for another country’s, even before any change in difficulty. — [Lipton, Elkan, and Naryanaswamy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4442797/)
- CI-EM error probabilities are already unreliable at different error levels inside one linkage problem. A harder overlap of match and non-match weights makes that bias worse, and boundary EM solutions appear when components are not separated. — [Winkler, Census rrs2007-05](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2007/adrm/rrs2007-05.pdf); [Thibaudeau 2025](https://wires.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wics.70015)

### Inferences
- “France is harder than US and India” means the precision at a given score, or the Bayes error, is worse than in both sources. That is a change in \(P(Y \mid X)\) or in \(P(X \mid Y)\), not a pure prior change and not pure covariate shift. Under that reading:
  - Matching France’s predicted mean degree to the source mean (about 3.46 in the brief), or France’s empty rate to the source empty rate (about 5.6% singletons in the brief), loosens or tightens \(\tau\) until the count matches. If scores on France are less separated, the \(\tau\) that restores the source count sits at lower precision than it did in the source. The count match hides the precision loss. Because F0.5 weights precision more than recall (\(\beta^2 = 0.25\)), that is the expensive direction. This is why copying the match rate over-merges when the target is harder. It would be the right correction only if France were a label shift with the same class-conditional score law and the same true prior.
  - Quantile-matching the France score histogram to a source histogram is the same idea one level down. Ovadia et al. show the map from score to accuracy does not survive shift, and a calibrator fit in-domain can make it worse. Histogram matching is not a precision constraint.
  - Weighted conformal and PPI are invalid here for the same reason. \(P(Y \mid X)\) is not held fixed, the likelihood ratio is not known, and there is no France gold sample. Estimating a three-field text likelihood ratio from unlabelled France rows is the high-dimensional case the conformal follow-ups flag as difficult, and a bad ratio voids the coverage claim.
  - EM false-match curves fit on France are unsupervised evaluation, which Tuoto and Winkler separate from ranking. On a harder country the non-match component is closer to the match component, which is the regime where CI-EM error rates and boundary solutions are already known to fail inside one domain.
- What remains valid as a conservative use of the labelled countries is a change of objective, not a France fit. Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz average the held-out domains because they assume the test domain is another draw from the same meta-distribution. If France can be worse than every source, that expectation is the wrong functional: the average of US→India and India→US is optimistic relative to a country outside the convex hull of those two difficulties. Ben-David et al. say no algorithm that sees only source labels and target inputs can guarantee the harder domain. So the worse-direction threshold is a constraint on the labelled transfers, not a coverage guarantee for France.
- Concrete rule, using only US and India labels. Fix the scorer family. For a grid of thresholds \(\tau\):
  1. Train without India, score India, record macro F0.5 as \(F_{\mathrm{IN}}(\tau)\). Train without the US, score the US, record \(F_{\mathrm{US}}(\tau)\). Threshold selection must not see the country it is judged on. A model trained on both countries, with \(\tau\) picked on a pooled validation set, is Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz’s training-domain validation, which assumes the test country resembles the pool.
  2. Set \(\tau_{\mathrm{cons}} = \arg\max_\tau \min\{F_{\mathrm{IN}}(\tau), F_{\mathrm{US}}(\tau)\}\).
  3. Do not select \(\arg\max_\tau (F_{\mathrm{IN}}(\tau) + F_{\mathrm{US}}(\tau)) / 2\). That is leave-one-domain-out as published, and it can accept a \(\tau\) that is fine on the easy direction and bad on the hard one.
  4. Do not replace step 2 by “take the stricter of the two univariate argmaxes.” The task brief says a stricter threshold tuned on one direction does not transfer to the other. Those two procedures differ. Example: the easy direction peaks at a strict \(\tau\) and the hard direction peaks at a milder \(\tau\), and the strict \(\tau\) cuts the hard direction’s recall. The worse direction’s own peak, and usually the maximin, stay at the milder \(\tau\). The strict univariate peak is exactly the non-transferring choice. Maximin moves toward the worse curve only as far as that curve still supports. It is allowed to sit between the two univariate peaks. It is not “stricter than both.”
  5. After \(\tau_{\mathrm{cons}}\) is fixed, do not move it so that unlabelled France has mean degree 3.46 or empty rate 5.6%. That reintroduces marginal matching. In particular do not lower \(\tau\) below \(\tau_{\mathrm{cons}}\) to chase France’s raw score mass. Loosening is the over-merge. Tightening past both source curves is also unsupported: the brief’s non-transfer result, plus Ovadia et al. on high confidence under shift, say a “very sure non-match” from a source-trained judge is not a France decision.
- Tie-break inside a flat region of the min curve: among \(\tau\) whose min F0.5 is within noise of the maximum, the higher \(\tau\) matches the Fellegi–Sunter habit of a tighter false-match bound. Do not leave the flat region. Lipton et al.’s winner’s curse is why a single noisy peak on the smaller country should not be trusted to several digits; with only two directions, the min curve will be coarse.
- The number \(\min\{F_{\mathrm{IN}}(\tau_{\mathrm{cons}}), F_{\mathrm{US}}(\tau_{\mathrm{cons}})\}\) estimates transfer between these two countries. The brief’s US↔India loss of about 0.005–0.019 macro F0.5, mostly precision, is a lower illustration of a country shift that still shares more lexical material than France (unseen French street words and legal forms). It is not an upper bound on the France drop. Ben-David et al. are the formal version of that warning.
- Group DRO does not substitute for this rule. It trains on groups it can see. France is not a training group. Using it here would spend the day on a regularised retraining objective that still needs a threshold chosen by the rule above.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that studies this exact maximin-on-F0.5 construction for record linkage. The construction is Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz’s leave-one-domain-out with the average replaced by the minimum, which matches a harder-than-every-source target better than their stated meta-distribution assumption. It is an inference, not a named method.
- No quantitative result was found on how large the extra drop is when the held-out domain is outside the source difficulty range, for entity resolution specifically. DomainBed’s oracle-versus-LODO gap (on the order of 2 accuracy points on vision benchmarks) is a different metric and a different modality.
- Whether US→India or India→US is the worse direction, and at which \(\tau\), is an empirical fact about this scorer. It is not in the papers.

## Which name and address features survive an unseen Romance vocabulary, and which fail?

### Takeaway
Character edit distance, Jaro (without a lexicon), character n-grams, exact house-number equality, and order-free token overlap still fire when both French strings share character sequences the training countries never listed. Learned word IDF and a closed list of English legal suffixes fail, because they are estimates or lists from the source vocabulary. Cross-lingual product matching is a different task and is only a bound: those papers still use target-language labels or external web identifiers.

### Cited Findings
- Edit distance (Levenshtein; Monge–Elkan as an affine Smith–Waterman variant), Jaro, and Jaro–Winkler do not use a word list. Jaro’s score is the share of characters that fall inside a matching window, plus a penalty for transpositions. The Winkler variant boosts pairs that share a long common prefix. Token Jaccard is \(|S \cap T| / |S \cup T|\) and ignores token order. TF–IDF cosine weights a shared token by its corpus document frequency, so rare tokens dominate (“Ray Mooney” versus “Ray Charles”). — [Cohen, Ravikumar, and Fienberg, IIWeb 2003](http://curtis.ml.cmu.edu/w/courses/index.php?title=Cohen_2003_a_comparison_of_string_distance_metrics_for_name_matching_tasks); [companion write-up with the Jaro formula and the TF–IDF rarity example](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/kdd-2003-match-ws.pdf); [IEEE Intelligent Systems version of the same comparison](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~ai-lab/pubs/marlin-ieeeis-03.pdf)
- On the name-matching collections in that paper, token TF–IDF was the best pure token metric, an affine-gap edit distance was the best edit metric, and the best overall method was Soft-TF–IDF: TF–IDF with exact token hits replaced by Jaro–Winkler approximate hits. Those collections are in-language name lists, so the IDF corpus matches the names being scored. The paper does not test French legal forms held out of the IDF table. — [Cohen, Ravikumar, and Fienberg, IIWeb 2003](http://curtis.ml.cmu.edu/w/courses/index.php?title=Cohen_2003_a_comparison_of_string_distance_metrics_for_name_matching_tasks)
- Overlapping character n-grams recover much of the benefit of stemming “without any knowledge of the target language.” “N-grams are also entirely language-neutral; no knowledge of a language is required to apply n-gram tokenization.” A word that is absent from a dictionary contributes nothing to a word index; a character n-gram index still sees pieces of it. — [Mayfield and McNamee, SIGIR 2003](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/860435.860528)
- Census linkage comparison spaces treat street number as its own agreement bit, separate from street name and from the largest name component. House-number agreement is allowed to interact with street-name agreement; conditional independence of those bits is false, which is why interaction EM exists. The equality test itself is on the number, not on a street-word lexicon. — [Winkler, Census rrs2007-05](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2007/adrm/rrs2007-05.pdf); [Winkler 1992](http://www.asasrms.org/Proceedings/papers/1992_140.pdf)
- Product matching is a different task (offer title and description, pair F1, product identifiers). Peeters and Bizer fine-tune transformers for German phone offers and always discuss a non-zero German labelled set in the text. With 1800 German pairs, English BERT scored 65% F1, about 6 points behind a bag-of-words SVM; German BERT beat that SVM by about 2.5 F1; multilingual BERT reached about 87% F1. Adding 7200 English pairs raised German BERT by about 16 F1 and multilingual BERT to about 91.4% F1. With only 450 German pairs, English pairs helped a lot (about 86.8% F1 once 3600 English pairs were added; doubling English again added about 1 F1). The SVM did not gain from English pairs. The largest reported mix in the text is about 94.5% F1 at 3600 German plus 7200 English. English pairs were gathered from the web via schema.org identifiers. A column of zeros appears in their size table, but the fetched table has no row labels, so those cells are not used here. — [Peeters and Bizer, WWW Companion 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03338)
- A Thai–English procurement matcher (again not business-address linkage, and it uses labelled PR–CR pairs) reports that character-level features dominate word-level features, and that lexical signals contribute about 70% of the ranker versus about 20% for semantic embeddings. The same abstract says systems that demand exact character identity and strict word order fail on cross-language queries. — [IEEE document 11276116](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11276116/)

### Inferences
- France-versus-France pairs are not a translation problem. Both sides use French street words and legal forms. The failure is that US/India training never listed those types. A feature survives if it scores two French strings using only their own characters. A feature fails if its parameters are a table built from English or Indian tokens.
- Survives, without any French list:
  - Levenshtein and Jaro, because they compare characters in a shared Latin script. “SARL” versus “SARL”, or “rue” versus “rue”, matches even if those strings never appeared in US data.
  - Character n-gram overlap (Mayfield and McNamee). Shared French stems still produce shared n-grams. No stemmer and no suffix dictionary.
  - Exact equality of the house-number token (Winkler’s street-number bit). Digits do not depend on Romance vocabulary. This is the right hard constraint when names are noisy: a one-word descriptor change at the same number is a different event from a different building.
  - Token-set overlap (Jaccard), which ignores order, so “12 rue de la Paix” and “rue de la Paix 12” still agree. The tokens must match as strings; they do not need to have been seen in training.
- Fails, or becomes a biased weight:
  - Word IDF fit on US and India. Cohen’s TF–IDF result is conditional on the IDF corpus being the same kind of names. An unseen French token has document frequency 0. Implementations then drop it or give it the maximum IDF. Dropping it deletes the only evidence that two French names share a content word. Max IDF treats every unseen token as maximally rare, so an accidental shared function word or a shared legal form is over-weighted. Soft-TF–IDF inherits the same IDF table; only the approximate-match piece (Jaro–Winkler) is lexicon-free.
  - A closed English legal-form list (Inc, LLC, Ltd, and similar). It will not strip SARL, SAS, SA, EURL. Residual suffixes look like name disagreements. No controlled French measurement of that list was found; the failure is the closed vocabulary, not a published error rate. Building a French list would be an external lexicon, which the rules forbid. Deleting the English list, rather than translating it, is the allowed move.
  - Jaro–Winkler’s prefix bonus is not a lexicon, but it was designed for shared surname prefixes. Many French addresses share a short function-word prefix (“rue ”, “les ”). The bonus can inflate non-matches. Prefer plain Jaro, character n-grams, or a house-number gate over a Winkler prefix tuned on English surnames. This is an inference from Cohen’s description of the bonus, not a French experiment.
- Word-order models and exact whole-string equality are the wrong invariance. The Thai procurement abstract says strict word order and exact character identity fail when the two strings are in different languages. Here both strings are French, so order-free token overlap and character n-grams are the relevant invariants; a translator is not required and is not allowed (external data).
- Peeters and Bizer are an upper-bound caution only. Product offers are not name-plus-address-plus-country, the metric is pair F1 rather than macro F0.5, models are large pretrained transformers rather than an 8B-capped Apache/MIT constraint applied at zero target labels, and every result they discuss in prose uses some target-language pairs plus web identifiers. Even then, an English-only pretrained BERT fine-tuned on German lost to a lexical SVM, and extra English pairs did not help that SVM. Nothing in that paper says a zero-label France neural matcher will gain. Do not quote 91% or 94% as an achievable France F0.5.
- The brief’s generator fact (same-address, one-word descriptor swaps are almost always true matches in the labelled data) tells you which failure hurts. A feature or a judge that treats one leftover token as strong negative evidence will reject true pairs that differ by a legal form or a descriptor. That is an argument for house-number equality plus a soft character comparison, and against an English suffix list that leaves a French suffix in place. It is not an argument for lowering the France threshold to the source match rate.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures Jaro, character n-grams, house-number equality, word IDF, and legal-suffix lists on French business names under macro F0.5 with US/India training only.
- Mayfield and McNamee’s n-gram result is about retrieval and stemming, not entity resolution. Cohen’s result is about in-language names. The transfer to French addresses is an inference from the definitions.
- The unlabelled zero column in Peeters and Bizer’s size table could not be read reliably from the fetched page (no row labels). It is not cited.
- No source found for a recommended character n-gram order (2, 3, or 4) on French street strings. Picking one on US/India F0.5 and freezing it is the allowed tuning; picking it on France is not.

## One France-specific upload: least harmful default when over-merge versus over-reject is uncertain

### Takeaway
When the sign of the France error is not identified, any upload that reweights or re-thresholds using France’s unlabelled marginal can help one indistinguishable problem and badly hurt another. The least harmful use of a single France-specific change is to turn off source-vocabulary features for France (word IDF, English legal-form lists) and keep character-level and house-number comparisons, then freeze the threshold at the worse-direction leave-one-country value. Do not spend the upload on a France match-rate, an EM cutoff, a one-country rejector, or test-set self-training.

### Cited Findings
- Reweighting labelled source rows by the unlabelled target covariate distribution is not a safe default. Ben-David, Lu, Luu, and Pál construct domain-adaptation tasks that look the same to any learner with source labels and target inputs, yet reweighting significantly helps one task and dramatically hurts the other. Covariate shift does not remove the need for a small joint error or a small unlabelled divergence. — [AISTATS 2010](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/david10a.html)
- Leave-one-domain-out’s average is the right selection criterion only if the test domain is drawn from the same meta-distribution as the sources. Oracle selection on the test domain uses target information and is not a valid method; they still cap test queries in the oracle condition and tell readers to label those numbers as oracle. — [Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz, §3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.01434)
- In-domain post-hoc calibration can increase error under shift. High confidence on shifted inputs is not a reliable reject signal: entropy thresholds do not separate shift from in-distribution data cleanly, and uncertainty degrades as shift increases. — [Ovadia et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.02530)
- If a human cannot review the middle band, Fellegi–Sunter still separates a tight false-match cap from a looser false-nonmatch cap. The undecided region is not automatically declared a match. Linkage write-ups set the Type 1 bound tighter than the Type 2 bound because false matches are the damaging error. — [Census rrs2007-05](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2007/adrm/rrs2007-05.pdf); [Shlomo handbook chapter](https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/id/fb2e9e86-c2ea-484b-82cd-2597c78e2486/1007164.pdf)
- Unsupervised error-rate estimates are the fragile output of EM. The rank can be acceptable while the false-match curve is biased. — [Tuoto 2016](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/e383532e-502a-15e8-e053-a505fe0a3de9/Tuoto_New-proposal_2016.pdf); [Winkler, Census rrs2007-05](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2007/adrm/rrs2007-05.pdf)
- A one-country fine-tune that rejects the other country at high confidence is the pattern Ovadia et al. document for shifted predictive distributions: confidence fit in one domain does not mean correctness in the next. The brief adds that those particular rejections were wrong. That empirical claim is not in Ovadia et al.; the mechanism (shift breaks confidence) is.
- Cross-language product matchers that actually gain from the high-resource language still use some target-language labels and, in Peeters and Bizer, external web identifiers. A lexical SVM gained nothing from the extra language. — [Peeters and Bizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03338)

### Inferences
- The sign is uncertain for a concrete reason, not as a generic caution. Marginal matching and EM cutoffs on France over-merge if France is harder than both sources (section 2). A judge fit on one labelled country, or a threshold stricter than both leave-one-country curves, over-rejects: the brief says the strict one-direction threshold does not transfer, and high-confidence rejects of the other country were wrong. Those two failures point opposite ways. A method that can move either way, and whose direction cannot be checked without France labels, is the class Ben-David et al. show can deteriorate on a task that is indistinguishable from one it helps.
- Least harmful default, one upload, one day, no target labels:
  1. Do not upload a France-only change to the positive rate, the empty rate, the score histogram, a temperature, or an EM \(m/u\) cutoff. All of those are functions of unlabelled France scores. Ben-David et al. cover the reweighting version. Label-shift theory covers the prior version and says it is unidentified once \(p(x \mid y)\) changes. Tuoto and Winkler cover the EM error-rate version.
  2. Do not upload a France rejector trained on one country’s labels. That is the over-reject that already failed in transfer, and Ovadia et al. say the confidence will not be checked by a higher score.
  3. Do not upload self-training on France pseudo-labels. The brief reports +0.0049 macro F0.5 for one round on a leave-one-country proxy and then a recall collapse, and flags test-input self-training as a fair-play grey area. It is also a second, unlabelled, estimation step of the kind section 2 says is biased when the country is harder. Ruled out.
  4. Do not spend the upload on leaderboard or test-label queries. Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz’s oracle condition is the same pattern, and they do not treat it as valid.
  5. The change that does not require knowing the sign is a France-only feature swap that removes source-vocabulary parameters and leaves script-level comparisons: drop word IDF learned on US/India and drop English legal-suffix deletion when the country is France; score name and street with character n-grams, Levenshtein or plain Jaro, token-set overlap, and exact house-number equality. Retune the single threshold only on US and India, by \(\tau_{\mathrm{cons}} = \arg\max_\tau \min\{F_{\mathrm{IN}}(\tau), F_{\mathrm{US}}(\tau)\}\), and ship that same \(\tau\) for France. No French list is required, so the external-lexicon ban is respected. This can still hurt if character n-grams collide more in French than in the source countries, but it cannot silently implement a France prior, and both directions of that feature choice are measurable on the labelled countries before upload.
  6. If the one allowed change must be a France threshold and the features cannot move: ship \(\tau_{\mathrm{cons}}\) and do not adjust it on France inputs. That is “no France-specific threshold,” which is the status quo the impossibility result supports. Using the upload to set a different France number is the harmful class unless the number is constrained to \(\tau_{\mathrm{cons}}\). Setting France’s \(\tau\) above both univariate source optima, or below the maximin, both claim a sign the data do not identify.
- Why this is the precision-heavy default without pretending empties are free. F0.5 and the Fellegi–Sunter Type 1 bound both say a false merge is the costlier automatic error, so the uncertain band maps to non-link rather than to link. The maximin threshold already implements that, up to the point the worse labelled country can bear. Going further, toward predicting empty for every low-confidence reference, spends recall at a rate the worse country already voted against. A correct empty scores 1.0 only when the reference is truly a singleton (about 5.6% in the brief). An incorrect empty is a total miss on that reference under the usual \(F_\beta\) formula. Abstaining on everything is not the minimax action.
- What “worse of the two directions rather than the average” means for the upload, in one line: the number that ships is the threshold that maximises the minimum of the two leave-one-country F0.5 curves, not the mean of the curves, not the mean of the two best thresholds, and not the stricter best threshold. France’s unlabelled inputs do not enter that number.

### Gaps
- Ben-David et al. show that some reweighting problems are unidentifiable and can deteriorate. They do not compute the regret of a character-n-gram swap on business addresses. The claim that the feature swap is the least harmful France-specific upload is an inference from that impossibility result plus the feature definitions, not a theorem they state.
- No decision-theory paper found in this pass gives a numerical minimax regret for macro F0.5 under a bounded but unknown country shift of a few points. The brief’s 0.005–0.019 US↔India gap is the only scale available, and it is not a France measurement.
- No paper found that quantifies how much Jaro–Winkler’s prefix bonus inflates French street non-matches. The recommendation to prefer plain Jaro or n-grams is a mechanism argument.
- Whether the feature swap should be global rather than France-only is not settled by the literature. France-only respects the one-upload constraint and avoids changing US/India behaviour that is already labelled. If character n-grams also help US and India, a global swap could be better, but that is a second change and was not the question.
