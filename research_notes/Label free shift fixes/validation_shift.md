# Validation when held-out labels and covariate reweighting rank models backward

Scope: how to build a validation score that orders models the way a shifted test set will, after ordinary held-out labels and importance reweighting have both failed. Setting is business entity resolution scored by macro F0.5 per reference entity, with a correct empty prediction defined to score 1.0. Numbers below that describe this dev/test gap are team measurements supplied in the task brief. They are not re-derived and they are not published results. Literature claims are separately sourced. Nothing in the literature reviewed here was run on these two submission files, so no method is reported as having flipped 0.985 above 0.98475.

Venue correction the report should not repeat: Moreno-Torres et al. 2012 is *Pattern Recognition* 45(1):521–530, not a JMLR paper. The JMLR paper in this lineage is Sugiyama, Krauledat, and Müller 2007. The dataset-shift book is Quiñonero-Candela, Sugiyama, Schwaighofer, and Lawrence (eds.), MIT Press, 2009.

## When is this covariate shift versus prior shift versus concept drift / label shift, and which model-selection methods are valid for each?

### Takeaway

Covariate reweighting is a valid model-selection correction only when \(P(Y\mid X)\) is the same on dev and test. Label-shift (prior-shift) reweighting is valid only when \(P(X\mid Y)\) is the same. A change in \(P(\text{match}\mid\text{pattern})\) for a rare pair type is real concept drift in Gama’s sense; it is not fixed by weights that depend only on \(X\) or only on \(Y\). The team’s adversarial AUCs show that \(P(X)\) differs, but that does not identify which factor of the joint moved, and the failed dev reweighting is evidence against a pure covariate-shift explanation of this particular ranking reversal.

### Cited Findings

- Team measurements (task brief; no public URL): a stacker change that raises dev macro F0.5 from 0.99030 to 0.99079 or 0.99061 drops the public leaderboard from 0.985 to about 0.98475. Per reference entity, stage-2 says match and the judge says no at rate 0.015 on dev, versus test 0.145 (US), 0.100 (India), 0.295 (France). Test has about 1.9× more records with no owner. Adversarial validation of dev vs test inside a score band has AUC 0.88 (US) and 0.83 (India); the shifted features are mostly counts and IDFs that depend on split size, plus house-number relations. Simulating extra ownerless records, and reweighting dev, both failed to rank the leaderboard winner above the later files. Among confident US pairs, “house number differs only in the last digit” has prevalence 0.8% on dev and 2.5% on test, and that pattern is a true match on 95% of labelled dev pairs.
- Dataset shift means the training and test joint distributions differ. The term is from Quiñonero-Candela et al.; Storkey’s chapter splits the mechanisms into simple covariate shift, prior probability shift, sample selection bias, imbalanced data, domain shift, and source component shift. — [Quiñonero-Candela et al. book PDF, Storkey ch. 1 TOC](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/47647/1/Joaquin%20Quinonero-Candela_2009.pdf); [Moreno-Torres et al. 2012, Definition 1 and §3](https://sci2s.ugr.es/sites/default/files/ficherosPublicaciones/1425_DatasetShift_TerminologyReview_PR_3rdRD_11_paper_JournalVersion.pdf)
- Covariate shift (Shimodaira; Moreno-Torres Definition 2): \(P_{\mathrm{tr}}(y\mid x)=P_{\mathrm{tst}}(y\mid x)\) and \(P_{\mathrm{tr}}(x)\neq P_{\mathrm{tst}}(x)\). Moreno-Torres restrict this name to \(X\to Y\) problems (features cause the label). — [Shimodaira 2000, JSPI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378375800001154); [Sugiyama, Krauledat, Müller, JMLR 2007](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v8/sugiyama07a.html); [Moreno-Torres et al. 2012, Definition 2](https://sci2s.ugr.es/sites/default/files/ficherosPublicaciones/1425_DatasetShift_TerminologyReview_PR_3rdRD_11_paper_JournalVersion.pdf)
- Under covariate shift, ordinary cross-validation is no longer unbiased for the test risk. Importance-weighted cross-validation (IWCV), which multiplies the held-out loss by \(w(x)=p_{\mathrm{test}}(x)/p_{\mathrm{train}}(x)\), is almost unbiased for that risk even under covariate shift, for any loss, including classification. The unbiasedness proof assumes the importance ratio is known; experiments replace it by an estimate. — [Sugiyama et al., JMLR 2007, §§2–3](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/sugiyama07a/sugiyama07a.pdf)
- Importance-weighted empirical risk minimization is consistent for the test risk under covariate shift even if the model is misspecified, but it is not efficient and can be unstable at finite \(n\). Under correct specification, ordinary (uniform-weight) maximum likelihood is asymptotically optimal for expected Kullback–Leibler loss, and the density-ratio weight is the asymptotic optimum only under misspecification. For moderate samples the weight should be chosen between those extremes by an information criterion (\(IC_w\)), not fixed at the full ratio. — [Shimodaira 2000 abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378375800001154); [Sugiyama et al., JMLR 2007, §2.2](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/sugiyama07a/sugiyama07a.pdf); [Sugiyama review notes](http://www.ms.k.u-tokyo.ac.jp/sugi/2012/CSAreview.pdf)
- Direct importance estimation (KLIEP) avoids estimating the two densities separately and has its own cross-validation for the kernel width. The ratio still targets \(p_{\mathrm{test}}(x)/p_{\mathrm{train}}(x)\), an \(X\)-only weight. — [Sugiyama et al., Ann. Inst. Stat. Math. 2008](https://www.ism.ac.jp/editsec/aism/60/699.pdf)
- In over-parameterized networks that separate the training data, importance weights affect optimization early and then wash out; \(\ell_2\) regularization and batch norm restore some of the effect, dropout does not. This is a statement about weighted training of deep nets, not about weighted evaluation of frozen prediction files. — [Byrd and Lipton, ICML 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/byrd19a.html)
- Prior probability shift (Moreno-Torres Definition 3): defined for \(Y\to X\) problems (label causes features), \(P_{\mathrm{tr}}(x\mid y)=P_{\mathrm{tst}}(x\mid y)\) and \(P_{\mathrm{tr}}(y)\neq P_{\mathrm{tst}}(y)\). The same paper’s closing bullet list instead writes prior shift as \(P(y)\) changing while \(P(y\mid x)\) stays fixed. Those two statements are not equivalent: if \(P(y)\) changes and \(P(x\mid y)\) is fixed, Bayes’ rule generally changes \(P(y\mid x)\). Later label-shift papers use the Definition 3 version. — [Moreno-Torres et al. 2012, Definition 3 vs closing terminology list](https://sci2s.ugr.es/sites/default/files/ficherosPublicaciones/1425_DatasetShift_TerminologyReview_PR_3rdRD_11_paper_JournalVersion.pdf); thesis restatement of the same conflict is in [Moreno-Torres 2013 thesis PDF](https://sci2s.ugr.es/sites/default/files/ficherosPublicaciones/1631_Thesis_JGMorenoTorres_2013.pdf)
- Label shift is the modern name for that prior shift: \(p(y)\) changes, \(p(x\mid y)\) does not. Black-box shift estimation (BBSE) estimates test \(p(y)\) without test labels by inverting a source confusion matrix. Consistency needs that matrix to be invertible. It does not need the predictor to be calibrated or accurate. It does need \(p(x\mid y)\) to be unchanged; the estimator is not valid when the class-conditional feature law moves. Saerens et al. estimate the same prior by EM, reweighting a frozen predictor’s posteriors. A 2025 Bayesian smoother (GS-B³SE) still treats BBSE as the estimator that applies when \(P(X\mid Y)\) is preserved. — [Lipton, Wang, Smola, arXiv:1802.03916](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03916); [Toth et al. on Saerens and BBSE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.11695.pdf); [Graph-smoothed Bayesian BBSE, arXiv:2505.16251, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16251v1)
- Concept shift (Moreno-Torres Definition 4) is the narrow case: \(P(y\mid x)\) changes and \(P(x)\) does not (in \(X\to Y\) problems), or \(P(x\mid y)\) changes and \(P(y)\) does not (in \(Y\to X\) problems). If both the marginal and the conditional change, their residual category is just “dataset shift,” not concept shift. They call concept shift the hardest of the pure types. — [Moreno-Torres et al. 2012, Definition 4 and closing list](https://sci2s.ugr.es/sites/default/files/ficherosPublicaciones/1425_DatasetShift_TerminologyReview_PR_3rdRD_11_paper_JournalVersion.pdf)
- Real concept drift (stream literature) is broader than Moreno-Torres concept shift: it is any change in \(p(y\mid X)\), with or without a change in \(p(X)\). Virtual drift is a change in \(p(X)\) that does not affect \(p(y\mid X)\), i.e. covariate shift. Only changes that affect the decision require adaptation. — [Gama, Žliobaitė, Bifet, Pechenizkiy, Bouchachia, ACM Computing Surveys 46(4):44, 2014](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2523813)
- Sample selection bias has four cases (Zadrozny): (1) selection independent of \(x\) and \(y\) (no bias; MCAR); (2) \(P(s\mid x,y)=P(s\mid x)\) (bias depends on features only; MAR); (3) bias depends only on \(y\) (a prior change); (4) selection depends on \(x\) and \(y\) jointly (NMAR), in which case one cannot recover the feature-to-label map from the selected sample unless an extra variable that controls selection is observed. The importance correction she recommends for evaluation, weight \(P(s=1)/P(s=1\mid x)\), is derived for case 2. She argues evaluation correction matters more than training correction. Case 2 does not change \(P(y\mid x)\). — [Zadrozny, ICML 2004](https://www.math.kth.se/matstat/gru/sf2935/zadrozny.pdf)
- Local learners (those that model \(P(y\mid x)\) directly, including logistic regression) are asymptotically unaffected by case-2 selection bias, provided \(P(s=1\mid x)>0\). Global learners (decision trees; naive Bayes) are affected because the bias changes \(P(x)\). The same paper notes this is the covariate-shift situation. — [Zadrozny, ICML 2004, §§2–3](https://www.math.kth.se/matstat/gru/sf2935/zadrozny.pdf)
- Under feature selection bias, the order of learners by ordinary cross-validation can be no better than chance. Fan and Davidson’s reverse-testing procedure selected the best learner 84% of the time on their biased/unbiased pairs, versus about 40–68% for cross-validation and leave-one-out; they include a credit-card fraud dataset among the applications. — [Fan and Davidson, KDD 2006 PDF](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~davidson/Publications/kdd06.pdf)
- In general, predicting target accuracy from labeled source data plus unlabeled target data is as hard as identifying the optimal predictor. Any accuracy estimator, including ATC, therefore depends on extra assumptions about the shift. ATC sets a confidence threshold on labeled source data and predicts target accuracy as the fraction of unlabeled target points whose confidence exceeds it. It beat prior estimators by about 2–4× on their shift suite. It does not recover a target risk when the assumption relating confidence to correctness fails. — [Garg, Balakrishnan, Lipton, Neyshabur, Sedghi, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04234)
- Where in-distribution accuracy is linearly related to out-of-distribution accuracy (“accuracy on the line”), the best in-distribution model is the best out-of-distribution model. The line is strong on several image and WILDS shifts and weaker on some CIFAR-10-C corruptions and on Camelyon17-WILDS. A Gaussian model in that paper ties the slope to how the shift changes the data covariance. — [Miller et al., ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/miller21b.html)
- Domain generalization model selection is part of the learning problem. Training-domain validation assumes train and test are similar enough that source error ranks target error. Leave-one-domain-out assumes a meta-distribution over domains. Test-domain validation is an oracle and is not a deployable criterion. An algorithm that does not state its selection rule is incomplete. With the selection rule fixed, ERM matched specialized domain-generalization algorithms on DomainBed. — [Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz, ICLR 2021, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.01434)
- Random \(k\)-fold splits can themselves induce prior shift and covariate shift. Stratified folds are the usual control for prior shift; partition-induced covariate shift is often ignored and can bias the performance estimate. — [Moreno-Torres, Sáez, Herrera, IEEE TNNLS 2012](https://sci2s.ugr.es/keel/papers/classification/2012-Garcia-TNNLS.pdf)

### Inferences

- Map the three pure cases to a legal selection method, and stop there. If \(P(Y\mid X)\) is invariant, the valid unlabeled-target selection tool is IWCV or an equivalent weighted dev risk, with \(w(x)\) estimated on the inputs the loss actually depends on (Shimodaira; Sugiyama JMLR 2007). If \(P(X\mid Y)\) is invariant and only \(P(Y)\) moves, the valid tool is a \(Y\)-weight \(w(y)=P_{\mathrm{te}}(y)/P_{\mathrm{tr}}(y)\), with \(P_{\mathrm{te}}(y)\) from BBSE or Saerens EM (Lipton; Saerens via the 2021/2025 expositions). If \(P(Y\mid X)\) moves inside a region of \(X\), neither weight is a functional of the observed shift: the importance-sampling identity \(E_{\mathrm{te}}[\ell]=E_{\mathrm{tr}}[w(X)\ell]\) requires \(E[\ell\mid X=x]\) to be the same law, which is exactly the covariate-shift assumption those papers state. Gama’s real concept drift covers this case whether or not \(P(X)\) also moves. Moreno-Torres would not call it concept shift unless \(P(X)\) is unchanged; they would call the joint change residual dataset shift. The report should use Gama’s “real vs virtual” split when talking about drift, and Moreno-Torres Definition 3 (not their closing bullet) when talking about prior shift.
- Label shift is not concept drift. Label shift changes \(P(Y\mid X)\) only through the class prior, with \(P(X\mid Y)\) fixed. A pattern whose match rate falls while the same feature vector is held fixed, and whose neighboring patterns do not move by the same prior odds, does not factor into a single \(w(y)\). BBSE then estimates the wrong prevalence. Ownerless rate is a candidate prior-shift story only if “no owner” is a label margin, or a pure cause of the label, and \(P(X\mid Y)\) is stable. The brief’s 1.9× figure is a change in a record attribute. It becomes label shift only under that extra assumption, which the failed ownerless simulation does not support.
- Adversarial AUC 0.88 / 0.83 is evidence that \(P(X)\) differs inside the score band. It cannot distinguish virtual drift from real concept drift, because the adversary never sees \(Y\). Counts and IDFs that are functions of split size are a feature-construction artifact: they move \(P(X)\) even when the entity population does not. A density ratio fit on those coordinates estimates the artifact. House-number relations that survive after those features are removed are the part of \(P(X)\) that could be decision-relevant.
- The 95% match rate is a labelled-dev conditional. The 0.8%→2.5% figure is a prevalence ratio on confident US pairs. The 10–20× lookalike-conflict rates are events defined by the stage-2 model and the judge, per reference entity, not an estimate of \(P(\text{match}\mid\text{last-digit pattern})\) on test. A 3× prevalence change and a 10× conflict change are not the same ratio and do not share a denominator. One cannot conclude from the brief that the conditional flipped. One can conclude that if it flipped, \(X\)-reweighting is the wrong selector, and that the brief already records \(X\)-reweighting and ownerless simulation failing to restore the leaderboard order.
- The measured dev/LB sign reversal (dev up by about \(3\)–\(5\times 10^{-4}\), public score down by about \(2.5\times 10^{-4}\)) is an empirical case where higher source score is worse on the public test for these files. Accuracy-on-the-line does not hold for this comparison. Training-domain validation is the DomainBed criterion that would have picked the worse public file. That does not say which shift caused it.
- Zadrozny case 4 is the selection mechanism that would make a labelled dev conditional unusable even after \(X\)-reweighting. If dev pairs were kept by a blocker or an annotator whose decision depends on both similarity and the true match bit, \(P_{\mathrm{dev}}(y\mid x)\neq P_{\mathrm{test}}(y\mid x)\) on the pairs that remain. Case-2 weights do not fix case 4. Entity-resolution candidate generation often behaves like case 4, but that is a structural reading of Zadrozny, not a measurement of how this dev set was sampled.
- Shimodaira’s correct-specification result cuts the other way for training: a conditional model that is well specified on the dev support does not need importance-weighted training to be consistent. IWCV can still be needed for selection among misspecified or differently regularized models, because the test risk reweights regions. Byrd and Lipton say that for interpolating neural nets the training weight may not move the predictor at all. Neither result says a weighted metric on frozen files will recover a leaderboard. The team’s failed reweighting is an evaluation result, so Byrd and Lipton do not explain it. Unstable weights can also fail to resolve a \(2.5\times 10^{-4}\) gap even under true covariate shift; the brief does not report effective sample size, so that remains possible.
- Garg et al.’s hardness result is the ceiling for every label-free method in the next section. No procedure that sees only train labels and unlabeled test inputs identifies test macro F0.5 for arbitrary shifts. Each design below is valid only inside its assumption, or it is a stress test that changes the assumption rather than estimating the leaderboard.

### Gaps

- Storkey’s prose definitions of domain shift and source component shift were not re-read from the chapter body; only the section list and Moreno-Torres’s formalization were checked. Do not attribute a sentence-level definition of “domain shift” to Storkey from this note.
- Zhang, Schölkopf, Muandet, and Wang, “Domain Adaptation under Target and Conditional Shift” (ICML 2013), was not opened in this pass. Target shift there is the label-shift object already covered via Lipton and Moreno-Torres Definition 3; conditional shift is Gama’s real drift with \(P(Y)\) fixed. No 2024–2026 paper found here replaces that taxonomy. The 2025 BBSE smoother still assumes label shift.
- Koh et al., WILDS (ICML 2021), was not re-fetched. Their subpopulation-versus-domain protocol should not be quoted from memory. Subpopulation shift in that line of work is the special case of virtual drift in which the test mass sits on groups that are rare in train, which is still covariate shift if \(P(Y\mid X)\) is stable inside the group.
- The brief does not include an estimate of \(P(\text{match}\mid\text{last-digit house number})\) on test, nor the effective sample size of the failed reweighting, nor whether “reweighting dev” meant a weighted metric, a weighted training loss, or both.

## What validation designs are known to work when P(Y|X) itself changes for a rare subclass, so importance weights on P(X) are the wrong tool?

### Takeaway

Nothing identifies the new conditional from unlabeled inputs alone. Designs that do not need target labels either change the labels by construction (synthetic decoys), optimize a pessimistic source functional (worst slice, DRO), or substitute a proxy (agreement, confidence, merge rate). Designs that score the true shifted conditional need labels on that slice. Source-slice scores remain valid rankings of the test risk only when the slice conditional has not changed and the slice weight matches the test prevalence.

### Cited Findings

- IWCV’s unbiasedness is proved under unchanged \(P(y\mid x)\). It is the wrong functional once that fails, regardless of how accurately \(w(x)\) is estimated. — [Sugiyama et al., JMLR 2007](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/sugiyama07a/sugiyama07a.pdf); [Shimodaira 2000](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378375800001154)
- Target accuracy is not identified from labeled source plus unlabeled target without further assumptions. — [Garg et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04234)
- Group DRO minimizes the worst training-group loss. On over-parameterized nets that already fit every training point, worst-group training loss is already zero, so the gain comes from stronger \(\ell_2\) or early stopping, not from the DRO algebra. Reported checkpoints are chosen by worst-group validation accuracy, which requires group labels on the validation set. The Waterbirds validation set was deliberately balanced while training stayed skewed, so that this selection would be stable; the authors note that a skewed validation set would make worst-group tuning noisier. Measured gains under that oracle-style selection were about 10–40 percentage points of worst-group accuracy on their NLP and image tasks, with average accuracy kept high. — [Sagawa, Koh, Hashimoto, Liang, ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.08731v2)
- A 2026 comparison of group DRO, CVaR, and \(\chi^2\) DRO selects the robustness radius by maximizing worst-group validation accuracy. The robust objective can be group-free; the selection rule in that study is not. — [arXiv:2601.21324, Appendix H](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21324v1)
- There is a model-selection criterion aimed at worst-group accuracy that does not use group labels, built by proxy scores inside a two-stage reweighting procedure. On the authors’ image and tabular tasks it beat other no-group-label baselines and approached methods that use oracle group labels. The criterion estimates worst-group source behavior. It does not observe a target conditional. — [Bardenhagen, Țifrea, Yang, OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=-D8l1ifCHYi)
- Agreement-on-the-line: when ID accuracy tracks OOD accuracy, ID agreement between neural nets also tracks OOD agreement, with nearly the same slope and bias. OOD agreement is computable from unlabeled target inputs, so OOD accuracy can be predicted with no target labels. The phenomenon is reported for neural classifiers, not for arbitrary tabular stackers. The predictor still worked on some shifts where accuracy itself was off the line, in their experiments. — [Baek, Jiang, Raghunathan, Kolter, NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13089); [Miller et al., ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/miller21b.html)
- ATC predicts unlabeled-target accuracy from a source-chosen confidence threshold and needs no target labels. The hardness theorem in the same paper bounds when that transfer is valid. — [Garg et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04234)
- Meinshafen and Bühlmann stability selection is a subsampling method for controlling false selections in variable selection. It is not a dataset-shift evaluation metric. It does not use target labels and it does not rank classifiers under a changed \(P(Y\mid X)\). — not given a separate URL in this pass; do not treat it as a validation design for this problem. The relevant “stability” objects that were actually checked are disagreement (Baek et al., above) and DomainBed’s training-domain rule.
- Synthetic near-duplicate generation is a record-linkage tradition: Febrl draws real-frequency base records and then inserts, deletes, substitutes, and transposes characters, and swaps fields, at user-chosen rates. Christen’s IDEAL 2005 study built three files of 175,211 records with 5%, 10%, and 20% duplicates and deduplicated them. That varies the duplicate rate and the corruption process. It produces hard positives (noisy true matches), not a labelled set of hard negatives whose conditional was copied from a test set. No ranking reversal between matchers is stated in the slides. — [Christen, IDEAL 2005 slides](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~Peter.Christen/publications/ideal2005-slides.pdf)
- Ditto augments entity-matching training data with difficult examples so the matcher learns invariances, and reports up to 31% relative F1 over prior systems on public EM benchmarks, plus 96.5% F1 on a company-matching holdout (789k and 412k records) after blocking. That is a training augmentation and a same-distribution holdout number, not a test of selection under a shifted decoy rate. — [Li et al., arXiv:2004.00584](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00584)
- A 2026 entity-resolution practice paper treats precision separately from recall: hard per-field vetoes set the match probability to 0 when both records have an identifier field and similarity is below a field floor. Purity rose by 3.6 points on MusicBrainz and 32.8 points on Restaurants. One false-positive link can collapse clusters under transitive closure. These are measured modeling effects on standard benchmarks, not a shifted leaderboard. — [arXiv:2607.26298, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26298v1)

### Inferences

- Split the designs by whether they need target labels, and do not call the label-free ones estimators of test macro F0.5.

| Design | Needs target labels? | What it actually ranks | When it tracks a test set whose \(P(Y\mid X)\) changed on a rare pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IWCV / \(X\)-reweighting of dev F0.5 | No | Test risk under invariant \(P(Y\mid X)\) | Does not. Wrong functional. |
| BBSE / Saerens prior reweighting of dev F0.5 | No, if the confusion matrix is invertible | Test risk under label shift | Does not, unless the slice change factors as a global prior change with \(P(X\mid Y)\) fixed. |
| Source-slice F0.5 on a pattern defined from \(X\), using dev labels | No target labels; needs source labels on the slice | \(P_{\mathrm{dev}}(Y\mid X)\) restricted to the pattern | Only if that conditional equals the test conditional. Reweighting the slice to the test prevalence (0.8%→2.5%) is then ordinary covariate shift on a binary feature. If the conditional moved, the source slice ranks the wrong risk. With a 95% positive slice, the negative count is small, so the source-slice estimate is high variance (Sagawa’s remark on skewed validation). |
| Synthetic decoy injection: add pairs that satisfy the pattern and label them non-matches, or edit a true match’s house number by one last digit and label the new pair a non-match | No | A risk the analyst invented | Tracks the test ranking only to the extent the injected \(P(Y\mid\pi)\) matches the unknown test conditional. It is a sensitivity sweep, not an unbiased estimate. It does not need target labels. Relabeling existing true matches as non-matches confounds recall; adding new negatives is the intervention that matches “extra false merges.” |
| Worst-slice / group DRO selection on predefined groups | Yes for the standard Sagawa rule (worst-group validation accuracy). No for Bardenhagen’s proxy criterion | Worst source-group risk, or a proxy for it | A pessimistic source criterion. It prefers models that do not sacrifice the named slice. It does not equal the test conditional if labels on that slice flipped. Selecting a CVaR or \(\chi^2\) radius by worst-group accuracy, as in the 2026 note, still needs group labels. |
| Agreement-on-the-line, ATC, pattern-restricted disagreement or merge rate | No | A proxy for accuracy or for aggressiveness on unlabeled inputs | Valid only under the paper’s assumption (linear agreement law; confidence tracks correctness). Confident false merges are exactly the errors ATC is not built to see. A raw merge rate on unlabeled pattern pairs ranks “who merges this pattern more,” which correlates with false-merge damage only if extra merges of that pattern are false. The dev conditional says they are 95% true, so the proxy and the dev labels disagree until the test conditional is assumed to be worse. |
| Oracle slice labels (judge on the pattern in test) | Yes | The actual test conditional on that slice | This is the only design in the list that estimates the shifted \(P(Y\mid X)\) rather than replacing it. Out of scope for a label-free recipe, but it is what “works” in the identification sense of Garg et al. |

- Synthetic decoys do not estimate a leaderboard. They answer a different question: as the assumed non-match rate on this pattern moves from the dev 5% toward a worse rate, which frozen file loses more macro F0.5? That question is well-posed without target labels. It is the same idea as Christen’s controlled corruption, except the injected objects are hard negatives rather than noisy duplicates, and except that Christen did not publish a model-ranking reversal from that control.
- Worst-slice selection on the dev conditional will not prefer the public-leaderboard file if both files are nearly perfect on a 95% positive slice and the leaderboard damage is a conditional the dev labels do not contain. Sagawa’s Waterbirds setup is the cautionary analogue: they had to build a validation distribution less skewed than training before worst-group selection was stable. Balancing validation is changing \(P(X)\) (and, in their construction, the group mix), not changing \(P(Y\mid X)\).
- Agreement methods need no target labels and can be computed on unlabeled test pairs in the pattern. They are not justified by accuracy-on-the-line here, because these files already violate the line (dev up, public score down). Baek et al. report that agreement can still predict OOD accuracy for neural nets on some off-the-line shifts. That experiment is not macro F0.5, not entity resolution, and not a rare house-number slice. Using it as a ranker for these files would be an untested transfer.
- Stability selection in the Meinshausen–Bühlmann sense should be dropped from the candidate list. It answers a variable-selection question.

### Gaps

- Slice Finder (Chung et al., ICDE 2019) and Domino (Eyuboglu et al., ICLR 2022) were not re-read. Both are source-label error slicing methods. They cannot see a conditional that exists only on unlabeled test. No citation in this pass shows them restoring a shifted ranking.
- No paper found states a label-free estimator that is consistent for \(P_{\mathrm{te}}(Y\mid X)\) on a subclass when that conditional is allowed to be arbitrary. Garg et al. argue this is not an oversight. A 2026 result that breaks that hardness result was not found.
- Bardenhagen et al. are cited from an OpenReview PDF and an abstract-level claim. Peer-review status and the exact tabular numbers were not re-checked beyond the abstract’s comparison to group-annotated methods.
- Jiang, Nagarajan, Baek, and Kolter, “Assessing Generalization of SGD via Disagreement” (ICLR 2022), was not opened. It is an i.i.d. disagreement-tracks-error result, not a concept-drift result. It is not used as evidence here.

## In entity resolution or fraud/duplicate detection, how have teams built a dev set that reproduces a test set with a higher decoy rate?

### Takeaway

No public entity-resolution competition writeup found in this pass built a dev set with a higher hard-negative rate and then showed that this dev set correctly reordered two named submissions the way a shifted test did. The closest documented product-matching case (Shopee) added extra candidates and upweighted false positives because ordinary group CV stopped agreeing with the leaderboard, but did not publish the resulting rank order. A gallery-size threshold adjustment on Shopee made the private score worse. Fraud writeups that fixed a dev/LB break used time or client structure, not decoy injection.

### Cited Findings

- Shopee Price Match Guarantee, 4th place: a split by `label_group` initially agreed with the leaderboard. Later, with stronger models, ensembling, and post-processing, that agreement failed. They tracked three scores: (1) F1 on out-of-fold predictions; (2) the same F1 but with in-fold embeddings added as extra candidates (“noise”); (3) score (2) with false positives weighted by 2. They state that (2) and (3) are leaky, because in-fold embeddings are easier to separate and because cross-links leak into post-processing, and that plain OOF on a 5-fold split covers under about 7k rows, around 10% of test. They do not state which of the three restored the correct order of their final candidates. — [4th place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238295)
- Shopee, 7th place: group-5-fold stratified by label-group size; best threshold fell as the number of images grew; they lowered the test threshold to follow that curve. Private leaderboard got worse (they report 0.762 and 0.757). — [7th place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238174)
- Foursquare Location Matching scores mean intersection-over-union of place-id sets, not macro F0.5. The 4th-place team clustered countries and, on the last day, copied train matches into test when cleaned name plus latitude/longitude rounded to 5 decimals collided. That moved them from 0.939 (15th) to 0.957 (4th). They describe this as train/test row overlap, not as a higher decoy rate. — [4th place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/youri-vincent-th-o-4th-place-solution); [competition metric](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching)
- IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection: adversarial validation reached AUC about 0.999 because the client population changes over time, not because the fraud concept changes for a fixed client. About 68.2% of private-set rows were clients absent from train, 16.4% were shared, 15.4% uncertain, in the winner’s accounting. The winner did not trust one split: train on the first months and predict the last, with a gap month, plus month-grouped CV, plus separate scores for known vs unknown clients. An XGBoost without the client-id features scored LB 0.9510; adding those features, which adversarial validation had pointed at, raised that model to about LB 0.9602 and local AUC by about 0.01. A 19th-place team likewise abandoned shuffled stratified folds for forward time splits specifically “to avoid huge shakes.” — [Deotte, 1st place, part 1](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ieee-fraud-detection/discussion/111284); [Deotte on UIDs and the 0.9510→0.9602 XGB](https://www.kaggle.com/c/ieee-fraud-detection/discussion/111510); [Deotte, part 2, validation list](https://www.kaggle.com/c/ieee-fraud-detection/discussion/111308); [19th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ieee-fraud-detection/writeups/19th-place-solution-summary)
- Febrl’s generator controls duplicate percentage and per-field corruption. The published IDEAL experiment varies the duplicate rate (5/10/20%), which changes how many true matches exist, not how many lookalike non-matches a test set contains. No algorithm ranking before vs after that change is given in the slides. — [Christen, IDEAL 2005 slides](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~Peter.Christen/publications/ideal2005-slides.pdf)
- Ditto’s company-data result is 96.5% F1 on a holdout after blocking, not a comparison of two dev distributions with different decoy rates. — [Li et al., arXiv:2004.00584](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00584)
- The 2026 ER practice paper’s hard identifier vetoes improve purity where different entities share conflicting identifiers (MusicBrainz +3.6 purity points, Restaurants +32.8). House-number last-digit clashes are the same error family. The paper does not rebuild a dev set to mimic a higher test decoy rate, and it does not report a leaderboard reorder. — [arXiv:2607.26298](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26298v1)
- Fan and Davidson’s selection experiment includes credit-card fraud under sample selection bias, with the 84% vs 40–68% selection rates cited above. That is a change in how models are ranked under biased sampling, not a constructed increase in near-miss non-matches. — [KDD 2006 PDF](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~davidson/Publications/kdd06.pdf)

### Inferences

- The Shopee 4th-place scores (2) and (3) are the nearest public design to “put more decoys in the dev metric”: extra in-fold candidates raise the comparison rate, and a ×2 false-positive weight mimics a metric that punishes false merges more than the raw F1 does. Macro F0.5 already punishes false merges more than F1 (\(\beta=0.5\)). Their writeup is evidence that product-matching teams reached for this when group CV diverged from the leaderboard. It is not evidence that the modified score would have ordered a 0.985 file above a 0.98475 file, and they themselves call the modified scores leaky.
- The Shopee 7th-place private-score drop is a measured negative result about size shift. Extrapolating a threshold along “larger gallery → lower threshold” hurt the private set. That is the same family of mistake as reweighting or resimulating a dev set using split-size effects (counts, IDFs, number of images) and expecting the leaderboard order to appear. It matches the brief’s statement that simulating a marginal shift failed, but it is a different competition and a different metric.
- IEEE-CIS is the cleanest fraud case in which dev and leaderboard disagreed and a documented fix moved the public score by about 0.01. The adversary was right that train and test differed, and wrong if read as “fraud \(P(Y\mid X)\) drifted.” The winning description says the nature of fraud did not change radically; the clients did. The fix that moved LB was a feature (stable client id) plus time-aware folds, i.e. a model of the subpopulation, not an importance weight and not synthetic hard negatives. High adversarial AUC is therefore not, by itself, a reason to reweight.
- Foursquare’s 0.018 jump is an overlap leak between train and test places. Copying that idea (match test rows back to train ids) would target a different shift than extra lookalike non-matches. It should not be proposed as the decoy-rate fix. The metric is also mean IoU, with mandatory self-matches, so the numeric gaps are not comparable to macro F0.5.
- Across these writeups, the pattern that actually got published is: when random or group CV diverged from the leaderboard, teams changed the unit of the split (time, client, label group) or changed the decision rule (threshold, veto, copied ids). They rarely showed a table of “method A beats B on decoy-augmented dev and also on the private test.” Absence of that table is a gap in the public record, not evidence that decoy augmentation fails.

### Gaps

- No primary writeup was found, with a before/after ranking, for Quora Question Pairs, Avito Duplicate Ads, or a business-entity competition using macro F0.5, in which a dev set was rebuilt to a measured higher decoy rate and the public order followed. Those competitions were not established as matches for this brief.
- DeepMatcher / Magellan negative-sampling ratios (Mudgal et al., SIGMOD 2018) were not re-read; no sampled-negative ratio is cited from them here.
- Christen's later generator papers (frequency-realistic households, PAKDD-era Febrl notes) were not needed beyond the IDEAL 2005 slides and were not checked for a ranking table.
- The Shopee 4th-place page did not return full text on a direct fetch; the three-KPI description is from the discussion excerpt indexed in search. If the full thread contains a sentence that one KPI matched private LB, this note does not have it.

## A practical recipe that can be run in a day on CPU, using only train labels plus unlabelled test inputs, and that would have ranked a 0.985 file above a 0.98475 file if the damage was extra false merges of a known pattern

### Takeaway

The procedure below is a proposal. It uses train labels and unlabeled test inputs, targets extra false merges of a pattern that is a function of the inputs, and is designed so that a file which merges more decoys of that pattern loses more score as the assumed non-match rate rises. It has not been run on these files. No cited experiment shows that it would place the 0.985 submission above the 0.98475 submissions. The arithmetic of macro F0.5 only says the public gap is the size of a small number of entity-level failures; it does not say these two files differ on the house-number pattern.

### Cited Findings

- Macro F0.5 weights precision more than recall (\(\beta=0.5\) in the standard \(F_\beta\) definition). The brief adds that a correct empty prediction scores 1.0, so a false merge on an entity whose gold set is empty changes that entity from 1.0 to a failure. Both facts make extra false merges more expensive than extra misses. This is a property of the metric in the brief, not a measured difference between the two files.
- Team measurement: dev and public leaderboard already rank these files in opposite directions, by a few \(10^{-4}\). Reweighting dev and adding ownerless records did not restore the leaderboard order. The last-digit house-number pattern is 3× more common on test among confident US pairs and is 95% a match on labelled dev. — task brief.
- A weighted dev metric is unbiased for the test risk only under covariate shift, and only if the weights are the right input ratio (known in the proof, estimated in practice). — [Sugiyama et al., JMLR 2007](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/sugiyama07a/sugiyama07a.pdf)
- Full importance weights are high variance; the finite-sample optimum is often a flattened weight, selected by an information criterion rather than set to \(p_{\mathrm{te}}/p_{\mathrm{tr}}\). — [Shimodaira 2000](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378375800001154); [Sugiyama review](http://www.ms.k.u-tokyo.ac.jp/sugi/2012/CSAreview.pdf)
- Density ratios should be estimated directly (KLIEP) rather than as a quotient of two high-dimensional density estimates, and the ratio model’s own CV should pick the kernel width. The inputs to that fit are a modeling choice. — [Sugiyama et al. 2008](https://www.ism.ac.jp/editsec/aism/60/699.pdf)
- Case-2 selection correction does not identify the risk when selection depends on \(x\) and \(y\) together. — [Zadrozny, ICML 2004](https://www.math.kth.se/matstat/gru/sf2935/zadrozny.pdf)
- Target accuracy is not identified without an assumption on the shift. — [Garg et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04234)
- Adjusting a match threshold for gallery size hurt a private duplicate-matching score in one published case (0.762, 0.757). Adding extra candidates and upweighting false positives is a published reaction to CV/LB divergence, without a published proof that it recovered the winner. — [Shopee 7th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238174); [Shopee 4th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238295)
- Hard negatives, not relabeled positives, are the object that stresses false merges. Hard identifier vetoes are a modeling response to the same clashes, with measured purity gains on other datasets only. — [Christen slides](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~Peter.Christen/publications/ideal2005-slides.pdf); [arXiv:2607.26298](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26298v1)

### Inferences

- Proposal, not a result. Run it as four scores with different assumptions. Keep the leaderboard number out of the fitting loop. Do not average the four scores into one “corrected dev.”

1. Freeze nuisance features before any reweighting (covariate-shift control; no target labels). Refit the dev-vs-test adversary inside the same score band after dropping counts and IDFs, or after recomputing them on one fixed corpus for both splits. If AUC collapses, the 0.88 / 0.83 figures were mostly split-size artifacts and must not be used as \(w(x)\). If house-number features still separate the splits, keep those as the covariates for step 2. This is a diagnostic. It does not by itself rank files. KLIEP on the reduced feature set is the CPU-day density-ratio method if a weight is still required (Sugiyama 2008). Clip or flatten the weights (Shimodaira); record effective sample size. If that effective size cannot resolve a \(10^{-4}\) gap, say so and do not treat the weighted ranking as evidence.

2. Prevalence-only reweight (still assumes the dev conditional). Upweight dev entities or pairs in the named pattern so its rate matches the unlabeled test rate (2.5% vs 0.8% in the confident US band), and separately upweight ownerless records by 1.9×. Score macro F0.5. The brief already says a version of this failed. Repeat it only on the cleaned features from step 1, as a negative control. If the leaderboard winner is still not on top, stop using covariate shift as the explanation for these files. Do not tune the weight until the winner is on top.

3. Decoy sweep (the part aimed at extra false merges; changes \(P(Y\mid\pi)\) by construction; no target labels). Define \(\pi\) from inputs only. The brief’s pattern is: house numbers differ only in the last digit, among confident US pairs. Add other patterns only if they are already known from train errors, not from test labels.
   - Build new negative pairs. Do not flip gold matches to non-matches. Two constructions that use only train labels and unlabeled inputs: (a) copy a dev true match, change the last house-number digit, label the copy a non-match; (b) take unlabeled test pairs with \(\pi=1\) and assign them the label non-match at a rate \(q\). Construction (a) can be scored only if the frozen matcher can be run on the new pairs. Construction (b) can be scored from a submission file only as a pairwise or cluster-level exposure, because entity-level macro F0.5 needs the other gold links for that test entity, which are not available.
   - Sweep \(q\), do not pick one \(q\). Natural grid: the dev negative rate on the pattern (about 5%, from the 95% figure), then 20%, 50%, and 80%. Also keep a \(q\) arm that adds pattern pairs without changing the conditional (prevalence only). That arm should agree with step 2. The arms that increase \(q\) are the concept-drift stress test.
   - For each frozen file and each \(q\), compute the same entity-level macro F0.5 where gold is known (dev plus injected pairs), with empty-correct = 1.0. On test submission files that cannot be re-run, report instead the count of predicted merges with \(\pi=1\), and the pairwise precision under the hypothetical label. Label that second number as a proxy. F0.5 and the proxy answer different questions; do not present the proxy as the leaderboard metric.
   - Rank files by how fast the score falls as \(q\) rises, not by the score at a single \(q\) chosen after seeing which file wins. A file that merges more of the injected non-matches falls faster. That is the sense in which the recipe is sensitive to extra false merges of this pattern. It ranks the 0.985 file above the others only if that file in fact commits fewer of those merges and the rest of the metric does not offset them. That comparison was not computed. Publishing a single \(q\) that happens to match the public order would be fitting the leaderboard.

4. Report source worst-slice F0.5 beside the sweep (no target labels, dev conditional only). Slices: last-digit house number, ownerless records, and the country the file is applied to, using dev labels. This is Sagawa-style selection without claiming the Waterbirds gains. With a 95% positive pattern, expect the slice gap between files near 0.99 to be noisy. If the two files tie on every source slice and separate only as \(q\) increases, the damage is outside the dev conditional, which is the situation the brief is worried about.

5. Do not use ATC or agreement-on-the-line as the decision rule for this gap. The files already break accuracy-on-the-line, the errors of interest are high-confidence merges, and those methods were not characterized on macro F0.5. Optional diagnostic, still label-free: disagreement between the files on unlabeled test pairs with \(\pi=1\), and each file’s merge rate on those pairs. A large merge-rate gap means the decoy sweep in step 3 has something to bite on. A zero gap means no label-free pattern score can separate these files, and the \(2.5\times 10^{-4}\) public gap is somewhere else.

- Scale, as arithmetic, not as a measurement. A public gap of \(0.00025\) macro F0.5 equals a total entity-level F drop of \(0.00025 \times N\), where \(N\) is the number of scored reference entities. \(N\) is not in the brief. If a false merge turns one correct-empty entity from 1.0 into a zero, the gap is about \(0.00025 \times N\) such entities. On a large test set that is tens or hundreds of entities, not a diffuse \(10^{-4}\) noise floor, but the count is not identified without \(N\). A dev set whose pattern rate is 0.8% and whose pattern is 95% positive contains few of those negatives, which is why a \(10^{-4}\) dev movement can point the wrong way and still look stable. An injected set of a few hundred decoys makes each extra false merge a large fraction of the synthetic score. That amplification is intentional and is why the synthetic score must not be reported as an estimate of 0.985 vs 0.98475.
- What would falsify the proposal on these files, without any new test labels: after step 3, the file with public score 0.985 does not lose less synthetic F0.5 as \(q\) increases. Then either these files do not differ on this pattern, or the public gap is not “extra false merges of this pattern.” Both are consistent with the brief. The recipe is not allowed to search over patterns until the public order appears.
- CPU-day constraint: steps 1, 2, 4, and the merge-rate half of step 5 are aggregates over pairs that already exist. Step 3a is a day on CPU only if features for the edited house-number pairs can be computed without rebuilding the full blocking graph. If the stacker is a black-box submission CSV and cannot be executed on new pairs, only step 3b (hypothetical labels on pairs the file already scored) is available, and it is a proxy, not macro F0.5. Say which of the two was run.

### Gaps

- The recipe was not executed. There is no measured synthetic-F0.5 table for the 0.99030 / 0.99079 / 0.99061 dev files, and no claim that any \(q\) reproduces 0.985 vs 0.98475.
- \(N\), the public test’s reference-entity count, is unknown, so the number of false merges that equals a 0.00025 gap is unknown.
- It is unknown whether the stacked models can be re-scored on edited pairs or only their submission files remain. Those two implementations are not interchangeable.
- It is unknown whether the judge-rejected stage-2 matches are the last-digit house-number pattern or a different lookalike. The conflict rate (0.015 vs 0.145 / 0.100 / 0.295) and the pattern prevalence (0.8% vs 2.5%) were not shown to be the same pairs.
- No public competition result was found that can be cited as “this recipe recovered the private order under a higher decoy rate.” Shopee’s FP-weighted noisy CV is the closest design and does not report that outcome. The Shopee threshold extrapolation is a measured failure of a size adjustment, on a different task.
