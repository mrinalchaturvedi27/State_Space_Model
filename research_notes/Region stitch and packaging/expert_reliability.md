# Label-free reliability of a one-bit expert when the prior and the error rates both move

Scope: one binary veto in a bin of a judge-free score. The veto rate is the mixture \(r = f\pi + t(1-\pi)\), with \(f = P(\mathrm{veto}\mid\mathrm{match})\), \(t = P(\mathrm{veto}\mid\mathrm{non\text{-}match})\), and \(\pi = P(\mathrm{match}\mid\mathrm{bin})\). All three may differ between labelled dev and unlabelled test. The constant-likelihood prior correction (freeze \((f,t)\), invert \(r\) for \(\pi\)) is already falsified on this dev. It is not a candidate fix below. Figures quoted as team-reported are the measurements supplied with this question; they were not re-estimated here. Anything not in those measurements or in a cited source is marked unmeasured.

## Given only \(r\), what bounds exist on \(f\) and \(t\), and what extra assumption is required to conclude that \(t\) rose rather than \(f\)?

### Takeaway
One veto rate does not bound \(f\), \(t\), or \(\pi\) separately: each marginal identified set is \([0,1]\). The only sharp, assumption-free fact is joint, \(\min(f,t) \le r \le \max(f,t)\). A rate above the dev value of \(t\) rejects frozen \((f,t)\), including by a negative implied prior, but it does not force \(t\) to have risen: the same \(r\) is produced by \(\pi=1\) and \(f=r\) with \(t\) left unchanged. Concluding that \(t\) rose requires a maintained restriction that blocks that point, either \(f \le t\) or a cap \(f \le \bar f < r\). Neither restriction is identified from the test rate.

### Cited Findings
- Under contaminated sampling with a known mixing weight, a component event probability is only partially identified. If \(P(y\in B) = p\,P(y\in B\mid w=0) + (1-p)\,P(y\in B\mid w=1)\) and the unknown component probability lies in \([0,1]\), the sharp region for the target component is \([0,1] \cap \big[(P(y\in B)-p)/(1-p),\; P(y\in B)/(1-p)\big]\). The argument is complete for a binary outcome. Point identification is not obtained. — [Horowitz and Manski, Econometrica 1995, as restated with the binary-outcome proof in Manski's identification monograph excerpt](https://ebin.pub/identification-problems-in-the-social-sciences-9780674265790.html); [Horowitz and Manski, Econometrica 63(2):281–302](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2951627)
- The same program starts from the no-assumption identified set and adds restrictions only when they are credible. Manski's law of decreasing credibility: the credibility of an inference falls as the maintained assumptions get stronger. — [Imai lecture notes quoting Manski 2007](https://imai.fas.harvard.edu/teaching/files/bounds.pdf)
- With misclassified data, the law \(P(\tilde Y) = P(Y)\,\Pi\) does not point-identify the clean distribution. Restrictions on the misclassification matrix are what shrink the identification region, and the region is sharp for those restrictions. Without a restriction the researcher does not get a point. — [Molinari, Journal of Econometrics 2008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304407607002564)
- Finite mixtures of binomial distributions are not identifiable. Finite mixtures of one-dimensional normal distributions are identifiable. A thresholded bit is in the first family; an unthresholded continuous score is in the second only under a normal model that was not assumed here. — [Yakowitz and Spragins, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 1968](http://dml.mathdoc.fr/item/1177698520); [Teicher, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 1963](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703862)
- Black-box label-shift estimators (BBSE, and the MLLS characterisation that shares its confusion-matrix condition) are consistent only when \(p(x\mid y)\) is invariant, hence only when the predictor's confusion matrix is invariant. The identifying equation is \(\mu = C w\). When a class is rare on the target, the solved weights can be negative; the BBSE training repair is to clip negative weights to 0. Clipping is an optimisation patch, not a new identified value. — [Lipton, Wang, and Smola, ICML 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03916); [same paper, PDF, negative-weight clip](https://assets.amazon.science/53/21/14143d6c4ecbb9b1ccdbd71162bd/detecting-and-correcting-for-label-shift-with-black-box-predictors.pdf); [Garg, Wu, Balakrishnan, and Lipton, NeurIPS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07554)
- A 2026 label-shift estimator still states the maintained assumption as "\(p(x\mid y)\) does not change, implying no concept drift," and its accuracy corollary requires the confusion matrix to be the same on validation and test. No 2020–2026 source found in this pass point-identifies a one-bit expert's \(t\) once that invariance is dropped. — [Zhang, Batista, and Kanhere, arXiv:2604.01651, 2 Apr 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01651v1)

### Inferences
- Sharp set given only \(r\in[0,1]\). A triple \((f,t,\pi)\in[0,1]^3\) is observationally equivalent to the data if and only if \(r = f\pi + t(1-\pi)\). That identity holds if and only if \(\min(f,t) \le r \le \max(f,t)\), with \(\pi = (t-r)/(t-f)\) when \(f\neq t\), and with \(\pi\) free when \(f=t=r\). This is the mixture bound in the question. It is elementary convex combination; Manski's theorem is the sharper interval you get only after the weight is known, which it is not here. Horowitz–Manski with a known weight still leaves an interval, because the other component is free in \([0,1]\).
- Marginal projections are trivial. For any candidate \(f_0\in[0,1]\), the triple \((f_0, r, 0)\) matches \(r\). For any \(t_0\), \((r, t_0, 1)\) matches \(r\). For any \(\pi_0\), \((r, r, \pi_0)\) matches \(r\). So the identified set of \(f\), of \(t\), and of \(\pi\) is all of \([0,1]\). A single rate, however extreme, is not evidence that the expert got better or worse.
- Frozen dev parameters are rejected exactly when the new rate leaves the interval with those endpoints. If \(r > \max(f_0,t_0)\) and \(t_0>f_0\), then \(\pi = (t_0-r)/(t_0-f_0) < 0\). If \(r < \min(f_0,t_0)\), the implied prior is above 1. Team-reported leave-one-country inversion on labelled dev already produces priors from \(-0.42\) to \(-1.53\) for \(s_0<0.8\), against labelled truth \(0.03\)–\(0.32\), and on unlabelled test several implied priors are negative (US rates \(0.72 / 0.60 / 0.21\) in bins \(0.5\)–\(0.8 / 0.8\)–\(0.95 / 0.95\)–\(0.99\), against dev rates about \(0.10 / 0.09 / 0.03\)). Those signs are the specification test of constant \((f,t)\). They are not an estimate of the new prior. Clipping them to 0, as BBSE does for negative weights, would invent "all non-matches" and is not justified by the bound.
- Why "\(t\) rose" does not follow from \(r > t_0\). The triple \((f,t,\pi) = (r, t_0, 1)\) reproduces \(r\) for any \(t_0\). If the bin were all matches, the entire rate would be a false-veto rate and \(t\) would not appear. The story "\(f\) rose to \(r\) and \(t\) did not move" is inside the identified set whenever \(r\) is inside \([0,1]\).
- Extra assumption that makes "\(t\) rose" a theorem. Maintain \(f \le t\) on the test bin (the veto is still more common on non-matches than on matches; no label flip). Then \(f \le r \le t\). If also \(r > t_0\), then \(t \ge r > t_0\), so \(t\) rose. The competing story "\(f\) rose and \(t\) stayed at \(t_0 < r\)" would require \(f \ge r > t_0 \ge t\), hence \(f > t\), which this assumption forbids. The assumption does not keep \(f\) at its dev value: any \(f\in[0,r]\) is still feasible, so \(f\) may have risen too. It also does not bound how reliable a veto is. The point \((f,t,\pi)=(r, t', 1)\) with any \(t'\ge r\) satisfies \(f\le t\) and makes every veto a veto of a match.
- Stronger assumption that attributes the rise to \(t\) rather than to \(f\), and that does bound reliability. Maintain \(f \le \bar f\) with \(\bar f < r\). Then both components cannot sit below \(r\), so \(t \ge r\). If \(r > t_0\), \(t\) rose, and \(f\) cannot have risen past \(\bar f\). The bound is sharp: \(t=r\) is attained at \(\pi=0\). Under the same cap the share of vetoes that are matches has sharp upper bound
  \[
  P(\mathrm{match}\mid\mathrm{veto}) = \frac{f\pi}{r} \le \frac{\bar f\,(1-r)}{r\,(1-\bar f)},
  \]
  attained at \(f=\bar f\), \(t=1\), \(\pi=(1-r)/(1-\bar f)\). The false-veto mass as a fraction of the whole bin is \(f\pi \le r\) with no assumption at all; that caps exposure, not the conditional error of a veto.
- What dev actually supports, and what it does not. Team-reported \(f\) on the two labelled countries lies in about \(0.005\)–\(0.027\), while team-reported \(t\) in the high bins is about \(0.37\)–\(0.50\) (US) and \(0.60\)–\(0.76\) (India). The ordering \(f \le t\) holds on that labelled dev, and a cap \(\bar f = 0.027\) is the top of the reported \(f\) range, not a confidence interval and not a test-set measurement. Test \(f\) is unmeasured. Using the cap on test is an extrapolation. It is more credible than the same extrapolation for \(t\), because dev already falsified transport of \(t\), but credibility is not identification (Manski's law, cited above).
- The high-bin inversion that "roughly tracks" does not validate \(t\). Team-reported example: inverted prior \(0.967\) against truth \(0.976\) for some bin at \(s_0\ge 0.8\). When \(\pi\) is near 1, \(r\) is near \(f\) and barely depends on \(t\). Illustration, not a refit of their bin: \(f=0.01\), \(\pi=0.97\), \(t=0.70\) gives \(r=0.0307\); inverting with the wrong \(t=0.40\) returns \(\pi\approx 0.947\). A large \(t\) error hides inside a small prior error precisely where their inversion looked acceptable. The failure for \(s_0<0.8\), where \(\pi\) is not near 1 and \(r\) is driven by \(t\), is the informative one.
- Numerical bounds under the dev cap \(\bar f=0.027\), using team-reported test rates. These are sharp for that cap. They are not estimates of \(t\) or of \(\pi\).
  - US \(r=0.72\): \(t\ge 0.72\), \(\pi\le 0.288\), at most \(1.1\%\) of vetoes are matches.
  - US \(r=0.60\): \(t\ge 0.60\), \(\pi\le 0.411\), at most \(1.8\%\) of vetoes are matches.
  - US \(r=0.21\): \(t\ge 0.21\), \(\pi\le 0.812\), at most \(10.4\%\) of vetoes are matches.
  - US/India \(r\approx 0.001\) at \(s_0\ge 0.999\): \(r<\bar f\), so the cap does not bind. \(P(\mathrm{match}\mid\mathrm{veto})\) can be 0 or 1. Exposure satisfies \(f\pi\le r\approx 0.001\).
  - France \(r=0.049\) at \(s_0\ge 0.999\): \(t\ge 0.049\), \(\pi\le 0.977\), and up to \(53.9\%\) of vetoes can be matches. The cap does not make this veto trustworthy.
- Matching dev's \(r\) is not matching \((f,t)\). US and India at \(s_0\ge 0.999\) have team-reported \(r\) about \(0.0009\)–\(0.0011\), close to dev. Every triple on the fiber of that \(r\) fits. France's \(0.049\) in the same nominal bin does not by itself reject a shared \((f,t)\): two rates are allowed under one pair \((f,t)\) as long as both lie between them, and team-reported \(t\) in that bin is almost unidentified (15–19 labelled non-matches). Whether France's \(0.049\) is above dev \(t\) in that bin is unmeasured.

### Gaps
- No source states a named "Manski theorem" whose conclusion is exactly \(\min(f,t)\le r\le\max(f,t)\) for an unknown weight. That inequality is the convex-combination identified set. Manski's cited bounds need a known contamination fraction, which this team does not have on test.
- Test \(f\), test \(t\), and test \(\pi\) by bin and country are unmeasured. The dev range \(0.005\)–\(0.027\) is not a standard error for test \(f\).
- The event count behind the 15–19 top-bin non-matches was not in the question, so a binomial interval for that \(t\) was not computed. The team's own judgement that \(t\) is almost unidentified there is the measurement.

## Is there a label-free method that point-identifies \(t\) under joint prior and likelihood shift?

### Takeaway
No method in the list point-identifies \(t\) from this team's bin-wise rates once both \(\pi\) and \((f,t)\) can move. Each method either needs \((f,t)\) constant across bins or groups, which dev already rejects, or it needs a pure anchor, a third conditionally independent bit on the same pairs, or a parametric family for a continuous score. The bin-wise rates supply none of those. EM does not create identification that the likelihood does not have.

### Cited Findings
- For two latent classes, a mixture of \(p\) Bernoulli coordinates is generically identifiable up to label swapping only when \(p \ge 2\lceil\log_2 2\rceil + 1 = 3\). With fewer binary coordinates the parameter count exceeds the dimension of the observed table. Generic identifiability still leaves the label swap; strict identifiability of Bernoulli mixtures fails even with many coordinates. — [Allman, Matias, and Rhodes, Annals of Statistics 2009, arXiv:0809.5032, Corollary 5 and the discussion of Bernoulli products](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5032)
- Dawid–Skene identification arguments use co-occurrence of several annotators. Published guarantees assume two disjoint subsets of annotators and full-rank confusion matrices, or third-order moments across annotators. One annotator's marginal is not the input those theorems use. — [Ibrahim, Fu, Kargas, and Sidiropoulos, arXiv:1909.12325](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12325)
- Class-conditional noise rates are identifiable from the contaminated feature distributions if and only if the total noise is below 1 and the two clean class-conditional feature laws are mutually irreducible (neither is a nontrivial mixture containing the other). The prior is a separate object: Menon's statement of the same framework marks the mixture weight as strongly non-identifiable when it is left arbitrary, and marks the two noise rates as non-identifiable unless an extra anchor condition holds. The anchor condition used in the lecture form is weak separability: the clean posterior attains 0 and attains 1, after which the noise rates are read off the minimum and maximum of the corrupted posterior. — [Blanchard, Flaska, Handy, Pozzi, and Scott, arXiv:1303.1208](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.1208); [Menon, van Rooyen, Ong, and Williamson, ICML 2015, PMLR 37:125–134](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/menon15.html); [Menon et al. slides: noise rates non-identifiable without assumptions; weight strongly non-identifiable; weak separability](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/6d89/84600e3cd9ae9d6b803f43f2410fa5c0ad0b.pdf)
- Instance-dependent label noise, where the flip probability depends on the instance and not only on the class, is unidentifiable from noisy data alone. The papers that estimate it add an assumption (bounded rates, parts, anchors, or a causal representation). A \(t\) that changes with the score bin is instance-dependent noise. — [Xia, Liu, Han, et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2006.07836](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07836)
- Fellegi–Sunter EM estimates \(m = P(\mathrm{agree}\mid\mathrm{match})\) and \(u = P(\mathrm{agree}\mid\mathrm{non\text{-}match})\) from unlabelled agreement patterns only with several fields. Winkler's unsupervised EM needs five favourable conditions: match proportion above \(0.05\), the classes separated, typographical error not dominating, redundant identifiers, and a useful conditional-independence fit. There is no a priori guarantee that the EM classes are the match and non-match classes the user named. Separately, \(P(\mathrm{agree}\mid M)\) and \(P(\mathrm{agree}\mid U)\) "can vary dramatically from one pair of files to another" and "can vary substantially from an urban region to an adjacent suburban region" with the same fields. That is \(m\) and \(u\) both moving, which is this team's \((f,t)\) both free to move. — [Winkler, JSM 2002](http://www.asasrms.org/Proceedings/y2002/Files/JSM2002-000648.pdf)
- BBSE and MLLS do not estimate a moving confusion matrix. They assume it fixed and estimate only the prior. See the citations in the previous section. The 2026 incremental-prior method keeps the same assumption. — [Lipton, Wang, and Smola 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03916); [Garg et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07554); [Zhang, Batista, and Kanhere 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01651v1)

### Inferences
- Multiple bins, nothing else. Bin \(b\) contributes one equation \(r_b = f_b\pi_b + t_b(1-\pi_b)\) and three unknowns. \(B\) bins give \(B\) equations and \(3B\) unknowns. The team's bin-wise rates are exactly this table. They are not enough. Countries do not multiply the equations in a useful way if each country has its own \((f,t,\pi)\): three countries give \(3B\) equations and \(9B\) unknowns.
- A monotone judge-free score. Suppose only that \(\pi(s)\) is nondecreasing in \(s_0\), with a separate \((f,t)\) in each bin. Monotonicity is an inequality across bins. It does not cut the identified set down to a point. The team-reported US test rates fall with \(s_0\) (\(0.72\), \(0.60\), \(0.21\), then about \(0.001\)). That shape is consistent with \(\pi\) rising and \(t>f\). It is also consistent with many other paths of \((f_b,t_b,\pi_b)\). Not enough.
- Constant \((f,t)\) across bins, which is the only way the bins share parameters. Then \(f \le r_b \le t\) for every bin under the ordering \(f\le t\), so \(f \le \min_b r_b\) and \(t \ge \max_b r_b\). That is a bound. It becomes a point only if some bin is a pure non-match anchor (\(\pi=0\), so \(r=t\)) and some bin is a pure match anchor (\(\pi=1\), so \(r=f\)), which is Menon's weak separability. The top bin is where matches concentrate, not a pure non-match bin, and dev \(t\) there is almost unidentified, so the team does not have those anchors. Even the bound form is the constant-likelihood model. Dev already rejects it below \(s_0=0.8\) (negative inverted priors) and rejects a common \(t\) in the high bins (US \(0.37\)–\(0.50\) versus India \(0.60\)–\(0.76\)). Applying \(\hat t = \max_b r_b\) on test would be another constant-\((f,t)\) point estimate. It should not be used.
- Joint histogram of bin and veto, still with constant \((f,t)\). The observed law of \((\mathrm{bin},\mathrm{veto})\) has \(2B-1\) free cells. Parameters: one prior, two error rates, and two multinomials on \(B\) bins, totalling \(2B+1\). Short by two parameters even before instance-dependent noise. Mutual irreducibility can close a nonparametric mixture, but Scott's hypothesis is about the class-conditional laws of the features, and it still uses class-conditional rather than instance-dependent noise. Whether \(P(s_0\mid\mathrm{match})\) and \(P(s_0\mid\mathrm{non\text{-}match})\) are mutually irreducible on this test set is unmeasured. Dev's country gap in \(t\) already says the noise is not a single class-conditional constant. Bin-wise rates are not enough to invoke the theorem.
- ROC shape constraints. The team observes one threshold of one judge. An ROC constraint binds a curve of thresholds, or a model for the score given the class. One bit per bin has no curve. If the pre-threshold score were still stored and were modelled as a finite normal mixture, Teicher's theorem would identify that mixture up to label swap, and Yakowitz–Spragins would still say the Bernoulli mixture of the thresholded bit is not identifiable. Identifiability of a normal mixture is not robustness to the wrong family, and Winkler's EM can lock onto latent classes that are not match and non-match. Whether the raw judge score was retained is unmeasured. The bin-wise rates are not that score.
- Invariant \(f\) across countries, \(t\) and \(\pi\) free. Per bin and \(G\) countries: \(G\) rates, and \(1+2G\) unknowns. For \(G=3\), three equations and seven unknowns. Not a point. If both \(f\) and \(t\) are shared and only \(\pi\) varies, every country's rate must lie between \(f\) and \(t\); the extreme rates are bounds, not the endpoints, unless some country is a pure anchor. US \(r\approx 0.001\) and France \(r=0.049\) in the top bin can sit in one shared interval. They do not point-identify \(t\). The middle-bin rates that fall outside dev \((f,t)\) reject sharing the dev pair. They do not deliver the new \(t\).
- Anchor points, noise-contrastive estimation, confident learning. All of these read \(t\) off instances that are essentially pure non-matches, or off a known noise distribution the user can sample. The corrupted posterior has to vary with a feature, and the noise has to be class-conditional. One number \(r\) has minimum equal to its maximum, so the anchor formula is vacuous. Noise that depends on the bin is the instance-dependent case Xia et al. call unidentifiable from noisy labels alone. The team's rates are not enough.
- EM with more than one feature. The likelihood of an i.i.d. bit depends on the data only through \(r\). Every triple on the fiber has the same likelihood. EM returns a point that depends on the initialisation; it does not choose the true one. Winkler's redundant-identifier condition fails for a single veto. Allman's count says a second binary feature is still short (observed table dimension \(3\), parameters \(5\)); a third conditionally independent binary feature on the same pairs is the smallest saturated case, and only generically, up to the label swap. The team's extra column is a score used to define the bin, not a second rater inside the bin. Once the data are reduced to \(r\) inside the bin, that score has been conditioned away. Bin-wise rates are not enough.
- Three countries are not three raters. Allman and the Dawid–Skene guarantees need the joint table of repeated labels on the same items. Three marginal veto rates on three different populations are three separate mixtures. The joint-table theorem does not apply.

### Gaps
- No paper found that gives a point-identifying formula for \(t\) from bin-wise marginal rates when \(f\), \(t\), and \(\pi\) are all allowed to depend on the bin and the country. The 2026 label-shift paper still assumes the likelihood fixed. Absence from this pass is not a proof that no such paper exists; it is what the search returned.
- Whether any within-bin feature other than the veto was retained, and whether the judge's pre-threshold score was retained, is unmeasured. Those would be different experiments, not a reanalysis of the rate table.
- Mutual irreducibility of the class-conditional laws of \(s_0\) on test is unmeasured. It should not be assumed in order to revive a constant-\((f,t)\) correction.

## What would a second unlabelled observable have to be to separate \(f\) from \(t\)?

### Takeaway
A second bit from a second expert does not separate them. A third conditionally independent expert on the same pairs can, up to a label swap that an ordering \(f<t\) breaks. A mass of known non-matches identifies \(t\) only for the distribution those non-matches were drawn from, and leaves \(f\) tied to \(\pi\). Two regimes with known, distinct priors and the same \((f,t)\) identify both error rates by linear algebra. One known prior does not.

### Cited Findings
- Two binary coordinates and two latent classes are below Allman's threshold. Three binary coordinates meet \(p\ge 3\) and are generically identifiable up to swapping the class names. The swap is resolved by an assumption such as each expert being better than chance, the same role the total-noise condition plays for Scott et al. — [Allman, Matias, and Rhodes 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5032); [Scott et al. 2013, total noise below 1 breaks the permutation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.1208)
- Dawid–Skene proofs that recover confusion matrices use several annotators' co-occurrence, not one annotator's base rate. — [arXiv:1909.12325](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12325)
- With a known contamination weight and an unrestricted contaminating distribution, the target probability is an interval, not a point. — [Horowitz and Manski 1995, via the Manski monograph excerpt](https://ebin.pub/identification-problems-in-the-social-sciences-9780674265790.html)
- Record-linkage practice estimates the non-match agreement probability from the file at hand because that probability moves across files and across neighbouring regions. The estimate is not a licence to reuse \(m\). Unsupervised EM still needs redundant fields and a match proportion that is not tiny, and its latent classes need not be the classes the user intended. — [Winkler, JSM 2002](http://www.asasrms.org/Proceedings/y2002/Files/JSM2002-000648.pdf)
- Random-pair estimates of a non-match probability are the standard label-free \(u\) in current Fellegi–Sunter software. They are computed on the files being linked, by sampling pairs, and they are not the same object as \(u\) inside a blocked or high-score subset. — [Splink Fellegi–Sunter parameter guide, `estimate_u_using_random_sampling`](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html); [Splink estimation tutorial](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/demos/tutorials/04_Estimating_model_parameters.html)

### Inferences
- Second expert, same pairs, conditionally independent given match status. The \(2\times 2\) table has 3 free probabilities. Parameters: \(\pi, f_1, t_1, f_2, t_2\), five of them. Not identified. The team's bin-wise rates are not this table anyway.
- Third expert, same conditions, confusion matrices of Kruskal rank high enough that no expert has \(f=t\). Observed dimension \(7\), parameter count \(7\). Allman's corollary says generic identifiability up to swapping "match" with "non-match". The ordering \(f_j < t_j\) for each expert, or Scott's total-noise condition, picks the labelling. If any expert is useless (\(f=t\)), the rank condition fails and the guarantee is void. Three countries' separate rates are not a substitute: the experts have to label the same pairs.
- A mass of known non-matches. The veto frequency on that mass is \(t\), but only for the distribution the mass was drawn from. It does not identify \(f\). Substituting the estimated \(t\) into \(r = f\pi + t(1-\pi)\) leaves one equation in \((f,\pi)\). A second mass of known matches from the same bin would identify \(f\) directly; \(\pi\) would then be \((t-r)/(t-f)\) if \(t\neq f\). Known non-matches that are random pairs, which is the Splink estimator, identify \(t\) for easy chance pairs. They do not identify \(t\) for non-matches that landed in a high \(s_0\) bin. Dev already shows why the distinction matters: at high \(s_0\), \(t\) differs between two labelled countries (\(0.37\)–\(0.50\) versus \(0.60\)–\(0.76\)). An anchor from the wrong slice estimates the wrong \(t\). A usable negative anchor has to be drawn from test non-matches inside the bin. The team has no such labelled test mass. Whether a hard constraint on the test file produces pairs with the same \(s_0\) distribution as the bin's non-matches is unmeasured.
- A policy that changes \(\pi\) and holds \((f,t)\) fixed. Two regimes with known priors \(\pi_1\neq\pi_2\) give the linear system \(r_k = f\pi_k + t(1-\pi_k)\). The unique solution is a point. One known prior gives the Horowitz–Manski interval for each error rate, not a point, because the other rate is still free. A policy that moves \(\pi\) by an unknown amount, and is observed only through a change in \(r\), does not point-identify anything: both rates still lie in \([\min(f,t),\max(f,t)]\) if \((f,t)\) really stayed fixed, and if \((f,t)\) moved the change in \(r\) is ambiguous. Holding \((f,t)\) fixed is itself an assumption. Dev shows \(t\) moving across countries at similar scores, so a policy that changes the country mix or the difficulty mix can move \(t\) while it moves \(\pi\). A clean version is an injected pure non-match slice (\(\pi=0\)) that is exchangeable with the bin's own non-matches: then \(t=r\) on the slice, and \(f\) is still not identified from the original bin. How to build that slice on this test file without labels is unmeasured.
- What would not be enough, even though it is a second number. A second bin's rate, a second country's rate, or a second leaderboard scalar is one more mixture equation. Each adds a number and, under joint shift, at least as many new unknowns.

### Gaps
- No design in the sources builds, from unlabelled test inputs alone, a set of known non-matches whose score distribution matches the non-matches inside \(s_0\ge 0.8\). Random-pair \(u\) is the available label-free analogue, and it targets the wrong conditional distribution for these bins.
- Whether a second model already scores the same test pairs, and whether its errors are close to conditionally independent of this judge given the label, is unmeasured. Dependence between two judges trained on the same features would break the Allman condition even if a third judge existed.

## With one or two leaderboard uploads and no target labels, is the upload the only arbiter?

### Takeaway
The upload is the only arbiter of the leaderboard number, and it does not point-identify \(t\). It is not needed to reject dev-calibrated use of the veto: any bin whose rate lies outside \([\min(f_{\mathrm{dev}},t_{\mathrm{dev}}),\max(f_{\mathrm{dev}},t_{\mathrm{dev}})]\) has already falsified frozen \((f,t)\), and the middle bins are in that class on the team-reported rates. There is also a pre-upload reliability bound, but only under a cap on \(f\). Under the dev cap \(f\le 0.027\), a high veto rate makes the veto a reliable non-match call, and a low veto rate does not. Without that cap, no bin has a nontrivial lower bound on how often a veto is correct.

### Cited Findings
- Negative solved weights are a known symptom when a confusion matrix estimated on the source is applied to a target that violates the invariance. The published repair used inside BBSE training is to clip them to 0 so the weighted risk stays bounded. That is not an estimate of the target prior. — [Lipton, Wang, and Smola PDF](https://assets.amazon.science/53/21/14143d6c4ecbb9b1ccdbd71162bd/detecting-and-correcting-for-label-shift-with-black-box-predictors.pdf)
- Partial identification reports the set of parameters consistent with the data and the maintained assumptions, rather than a point the assumptions do not support. Stronger assumptions narrow the set and are less credible. — [Molinari 2008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304407607002564); [Imai notes on Manski 2007](https://imai.fas.harvard.edu/teaching/files/bounds.pdf)
- One binary margin does not identify the three mixture parameters. Finite Bernoulli mixtures are the non-identifiable case in the classical mixture taxonomy. — [Yakowitz and Spragins 1968](http://dml.mathdoc.fr/item/1177698520)

### Inferences
- Do not spend an upload to re-estimate a frozen-\((f,t)\) prior. The test is pre-upload and already failed. Team-reported middle bins have rates that cannot be written as a convex combination of dev \((f,t)\), in all three countries, with negative implied priors. US and India at \(s_0\ge 0.999\) matching dev's rate is not a pass, because the fiber of a single \(r\) is large and dev \(t\) in that bin rests on 15–19 non-matches. France at \(0.049\) is a different rate, not by itself a rejection of dev \((f,t)\), for the same reason. The constant-likelihood update, BBSE, MLLS, Saerens-style EM, and clipping a negative prior to 0 are the same rejected model. None of them is the next submission.
- Assumption-free statement about the hard veto. The conditional probability that a veto is correct is not bounded by \(r\). It can be anything in \([0,1]\). The only assumption-free action bound is exposure: the fraction of the bin that is both a match and vetoed is \(f\pi \le r\). A bin with team-reported \(r=0.72\) can lose up to 72% of its pairs to false vetoes, and can lose every match if \(\pi\le r\). A bin with \(r\approx 0.001\) cannot lose more than about \(0.1\%\) of its pairs to false vetoes, but the vetoes it does issue can all be wrong. Exposure is not reliability. There is no assumption-free rule of the form "the veto in this bin is untrustworthy" that is separate from "the dev calibration in this bin is untrustworthy."
- Pre-upload bound that does say when a veto is trustworthy, and when it is not, under one explicit assumption. Maintain \(f\le 0.027\), the top of the team-reported labelled range. Then, on the team-reported US rates, at most \(1.1\%\), \(1.8\%\), and \(10.4\%\) of vetoes are matches in the bins with \(r=0.72\), \(0.60\), and \(0.21\), and \(t\) is at least the rate itself. In those bins the reading "non-matches got easier to reject, so \(t\) rose" is what the cap implies, and the hard veto is a reliable rejector under that cap. The same cap says the opposite at the top of the score: US/India \(r\approx 0.001\) does not determine whether a veto is right, and France \(r=0.049\) still allows up to \(53.9\%\) of vetoes to be matches. The bins where the bound says "do not trust the veto" are the low-rate bins, not the high-rate ones. Dropping the cap reverses the logic: high \(r\) is then compatible with \(f=r\) and every veto being a false veto. The cap is not identified on test. It is a sensitivity assumption. Dev makes it more plausible than a cap on \(t\), because \(f\) was the stable one across two labelled countries and \(t\) was not. Two countries are a thin base, and test \(f\) was not measured.
- The leaderboard scalar does not replace the bound and does not estimate \(t\). One upload returns one number for one policy. The map from \((f,t,\pi)\) to that number is unmeasured, so a higher score does not isolate which parameter moved. Two uploads can compare two policies, for example veto disabled against veto enabled only in bins where the cap gives a tight bound, or the reverse. That comparison is the decision quantity. It is still two numbers, not an identification of \(t\). Spending both uploads on two different prior corrections would repeat a model the dev labels already rejected.
- Practical split. Reject, with no upload, any submission whose scores depend on inverting test \(r\) through dev \((f,t)\). If the team is willing to maintain \(f\le 0.027\), the middle-bin veto is a supported hard reject and the top-bin veto, especially France's \(0.049\), is not a supported hard reject; the upload is then best used on the policy the bound does not settle. If the team is not willing to maintain the cap, no bin's veto has a pre-upload reliability guarantee, exposure is the only bound, and the upload is the only arbiter of whether turning the veto on helps. In that case the upload still will not reveal \(t\).

### Gaps
- The leaderboard metric, and the algebraic effect of a false veto on that metric, are unmeasured in this pass. The bounds above are on \((f,t,\pi)\) and on \(P(\mathrm{match}\mid\mathrm{veto})\), not on the scoreboard delta.
- Test bin sizes are unmeasured, so sampling error in the team-reported rates \(0.72\), \(0.60\), \(0.21\), \(0.049\), and \(0.001\) was not propagated. The negative-prior rejections are only as strong as those rates.
- No upload already run on a veto-on versus veto-off pair was in the question. The recommendation about how to spend one or two uploads is a design inference, not a measured outcome.
