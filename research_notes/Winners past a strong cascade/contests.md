# Winners past a strong cascade

Scope: contests whose task is record linkage, entity resolution, deduplication, or place/product matching, 2022–2026, with pre-2022 SIGMOD ER editions included only where they are the primary evidence for a decision-layer win. Foursquare 2022 and Shopee 2021 were not re-investigated. No new primary writeup that changes the known Foursquare leak story (honest ceiling ~0.921–0.933 IoU; official 0.977 was a leak) or the Shopee embedding-plus-threshold result was located in this pass.

Eligibility filter used below: external geocoders, commercial ER APIs, and non-Apache/MIT models over 8B parameters are flagged ineligible for this team. Scores are only those printed by a primary writeup, paper, or organizer page. Participant self-reports during a live contest are labeled as such and are not treated as winners.

## Which 2023–2026 contests are actually record linkage or dedup, and what were the stages?

### Takeaway

No finished Kaggle, KDD Cup, SIGMOD, DrivenData, or WDC contest from 2023 through 26 Sep 2026 is business, address, customer, or product record linkage with a published winner. SIGMOD’s last ER editions were 2020 (matching), 2021 (matching), and 2022 (blocking only); 2023–2025 SIGMOD contests were not ER. The only 2026 contest that is actually one-to-many business ER is the Amazon ML Challenge 2026, and it was still in its 72-hour hackathon on 26 Sep 2026, so it has no winner. The architectures that did win earlier SIGMOD ER rounds were rule or mock-label systems (2020, 2021) or a tiny distilled transformer plus a Jaccard re-rank under a hard candidate budget (2022), not a larger model.

### Cited Findings

- SIGMOD programming contests after the ER series were not entity resolution: 2023 approximate k-nearest-neighbor graph construction, 2024 hybrid vector search, 2025 in-memory join pipeline. The archive lists no 2026 contest and no ER task after 2022. — [SIGMOD Programming Contest Archive](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/); [SIGMOD 2025 task page](https://sigmod-contest-2025.github.io/)
- Organizers’ 2023 lessons paper: three ER editions (2020, 2021, 2022). 2020 and 2021 focused on the matching step; 2022 moved to blocking. Participation they report: 53 teams / 16 countries (2020), 51 teams / 12 countries and about 1500 submissions (2021), 60 teams / 10 countries and about 2500 submissions (2022). The same paper’s authors ran earlier DI2KG satellite challenges at KDD 2019 and VLDB 2020, outside this window. — [De Angelis et al., SIGMOD Record 2023 PDF](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/47dab96f-c387-4c2d-804c-674a6c833c65/09_Reports_DeAngelis.pdf)
- Team-count conflict for 2022 only: the winner’s university announcement (3 May 2022) and an 11th-place README both say 55 teams; the 2023 lessons paper says 60. — [Universität Mannheim](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/dws/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/); [BringBackML README](https://github.com/theodoratrz/sigmodContest2022); [lessons PDF](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/47dab96f-c387-4c2d-804c-674a6c833c65/09_Reports_DeAngelis.pdf)

**SIGMOD 2020 (cameras / product offers; pre-window, included because it is the clean decision-layer case).** Task: pair-wise ER on ~29,787 camera specifications from 24 e-commerce sites; F-measure on a secret set, rounded to two decimal places; tie broken by resolution runtime. Labelled pairs: dataset Y 46,665 pairs (3,582 matches); dataset W 297,651 pairs (44,039 matches). — [Zecchini, Simonini, Bergamaschi, DI2KG 2020](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf); [Blacher et al., DI2KG 2020](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf)

Winner stages (PictureMe / Jena; mock labels, not a neural matcher):
1. Semi-automatic mock labels (~2,800) from clean page titles (stop-word cuts), Wikipedia synonymous camera names, and eBay brand+model fields. That set alone scored F 0.97 on the hidden set.
2. About 200 hand-added labels, taken from titles that matched nothing, raised F to 0.99.
3. Labels and titles become sorted integer sets (optional trie). A label matches if every token is in the title. Subset labels are dropped so “EOS 7D” does not swallow “EOS 7D Mark II”. If the full label misses, a simplified label with non-critical tokens removed is tried.
4. Assigned camera ids are clusters; the submission is the Cartesian product of pairs inside each cluster.
All five finalists scored F 0.99. Winner paper’s own laptop timing (not the organizer container clock): PictureMe 0.61 s, runner-up DBGroup@UniMoRe 10.65 s, DBGroup@SUSTech 22.13 s, eats_shoots_and_leaves 28.66 s, DBTHU 63.21 s. — [Blacher et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf)

Runner-up stages (UniMoRe; also not ML): human brand list, alias dictionary, per-brand prefix/suffix/exception/equivalence lists, regex for alphanumeric model tokens, equality join on a canonical `brand_n_model`, exact title join only for rows that failed extraction. Final submission F 0.99, precision 0.99, recall 0.98. — [Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)

Their own strong cascade, which they measured and then did not ship: truncate page title to 4 tokens, drop a hand list of useless words, token blocking (3,914 blocks, 54,000,932 candidate pairs; on labelled W, blocking precision 0.28 and recall 0.99), then Magellan or DeepMatcher. Best full-data configuration was DeepMatcher RNN at F 0.47 (recall 0.85, precision 0.32). The abstract says ML methods were “not reaching 0.49 F-score.” On the labelled sets the same family looked solved: after truncation, DeepMatcher RNN F 0.9826 on Y and F 0.96 on W (attention and hybrid F 0.84 on W; SIF F 0.37). — [Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)

**SIGMOD 2021 (matching across several datasets; pre-window).** Winner: Weibao Fu, Peiqi Yin, Lan Lu (SUSTech). Stages from the winner README, not a neural model: merge fields and lowercase; regex extraction of key fields (brand and similar), with rules written from the data and from brand sites; alias normalization and fill-in of missing keys; identification by complete key tuples; a “recycle” pass that attaches incomplete records using secondary-key combinations; a looser residual id for what remains; equality on those ids emits pairs. Per-dataset scores they report in thousandths: X2 recall/precision/F 971/995/983; X3 991/980/986; X4 980/880/927. Ranking metric was average F over datasets. No second-place score was found. — [Winner README](https://github.com/yinpeiqi/SIGMOD-2021-program-contest); [contest archive](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/2021)

**SIGMOD 2022 (product blocking only; inside the 2022–2026 window).** Metric: average recall of true matches kept in the candidate set (trivial same-id pairs excluded); ties broken by runtime. Candidate budget fixed by organizers: 1 million pairs on D1, 2 million on D2. Hardware cap: CPU only, 16 cores, 32 GB, 35 minutes. — [Contest archive](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/2022); [WBSG README](https://github.com/abrinkmann/acm_sigmoid_2022_challenge)

Winner stages (WBSG, Mannheim: Brinkmann and Peeters):
1. Domain-specific normalization of title (D1) or name (D2).
2. Group tuples with identical text.
3. Embed unique texts with fine-tuned `microsoft/xtremedistil-l6-h256-uncased` (supervised contrastive loss; extra pretraining on schema.org computer offers from Common Crawl). Max length 28 (D1) or 24 (D2). A linear layer projects 256 dimensions to 32.
4. FAISS approximate nearest neighbors.
5. Re-rank by the average of neural cosine and token Jaccard “to smooth the bias of the embedding model.”
6. Emit pairs from the top group pairs until the 1M / 2M budget.
55 teams in the contemporary announcement; five finalists. Official leaderboard numbers did not render from the leaders page in this pass. An 11th-place README states 1st-place average recall 52.9% at 1914 s, and their own average recall 46.9% (D1 71%, D2 22.7%). Treat 52.9% / 1914 s as participant-reported, not an organizer table. — [Mannheim announcement](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/dws/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/); [leaders page](https://dbgroup.ing.unimore.it/sigmod22contest/leaders.shtml); [WBSG README](https://github.com/abrinkmann/acm_sigmoid_2022_challenge); [BringBackML README](https://github.com/theodoratrz/sigmodContest2022)

Runner-up stages (April, Rutgers: Zuo and Wang): no neural model. Sed cleaning, then similarity grouping, exact Jaccard similarity join, and overlap set join; rank the pool by weighted similarity; emit a fixed number of pairs. Example parameters on D1: group Jaccard 0.8, candidate Jaccard 0.57, overlap 9, pool factor 15, 1,000,000 output pairs. They explicitly say the method “doesn’t rely on heavy data preprocessing or fancy manual rules.” Their official recall was not found, so the 1st-vs-2nd delta is unknown. — [April README](https://github.com/rutgers-db/SIGMOD2022-Programming-Contest-Public)

Eligibility: xtremedistil-l6-h256 is far under 8B. This pass did not open the Hugging Face license card, so the Apache/MIT check is unverified. Pretraining used a public research corpus (schema.org offers), not a commercial ER API or a geocoder. — [WBSG README](https://github.com/abrinkmann/acm_sigmoid_2022_challenge)

**Amazon ML Challenge 2026 (business ER; the only in-window contest that matches the task, and it is unfinished).** India engineering-student hackathon. Timeline from a 21 Sep 2026 prep note: registration 7–22 Sep 2026, 72-hour hackathon 25–27 Sep 2026, top-50 results 2 Oct 2026, finale 7 Oct 2026. As of 26 Sep 2026 there is no winner. — [AWS Builder Center prep note](https://builder.aws.com/content/3HiM6zDmFrF98fRzOUETnGFDoqz/amazon-ml-challenge-2026-your-complete-prep-guide-with-live-demo)

Task, from the problem statement copied into entrant repos and from the challenge PDF: three sources, no shared ids. Source 1 is the deduplicated reference. For each Source 1 entity, find zero, one, or many Source 2 and Source 3 records. Scored file is `matching_results.tsv`. Metric is macro F0.5 per Source 1 entity (precision weighted twice recall), singletons included. Public leaderboard is a subset of test. Final zip is audited for blocking, reproducibility, and “fair-play and model-license rules.” Exact parameter cap and whether geocoders are banned were not extracted from an organizer page in this pass. One entrant states “no external data lookup.” — [Challenge PDF](https://d8it4huxumps7.cloudfront.net/files/6ab674645103d_emails_comms_amazon_ml_challenge_2026.pdf); [problem README mirror](https://github.com/venkatanaveen2078909-rgb/AMAZON/blob/main/6ab10eb3b23ba_student_resource/student_resource/README.md); [Siva402-ai documentation](https://github.com/Siva402-ai/amazon-ml-challenge-2026/blob/main/Documentation_template.md); [DevanshSrajput README](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge)

Entrant pipelines (not winners; both are the cascade this team already treats as the strong baseline):

Self-reported end-to-end validation macro F0.5: v1 0.9707 (candidate recall 98.1%); v2 with Indic dictionary plus difference/margin features 0.9783 (precision 0.9886, recall 0.9549, candidate recall 98.7%). Threshold sweep on that fold: 0.3 → 0.9725, 0.5 → 0.9773, 0.6 → 0.9782, 0.7 → 0.9783, 0.8 → 0.9774. They say duplicating distractors drops the score only to 0.9759 and leaves 0.7 near-optimal. These are not leaderboard results. — [Documentation_template.md](https://github.com/Siva402-ai/amazon-ml-challenge-2026/blob/main/Documentation_template.md); [pipeline README](https://github.com/Siva402-ai/amazon-ml-challenge-2026/blob/main/business_entity_resolution/README.md)
- DevanshSrajput (“Deadline Dodgers”): blocking to 9.65 candidates per Source 1 entity; LightGBM; threshold τ = 0.65. Self-reported validation macro F0.5 0.8899 on 40,000 held-out entities; blocking-recall ceiling 0.9415; micro precision/recall 0.9718/0.8013; singleton accuracy 0.8910; trivial predict-nothing baseline 0.0558; a leaderboard number 0.875. The two entrants’ validation scores (0.978 vs 0.890) are not comparable: different splits, and neither is an official result. — [DevanshSrajput README](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge)

### Inferences

- Inside 2023–2026 there is no published “winner past a cascade” on business, address, customer, or product dedup, because there is no finished contest of that type. The live Amazon 2026 task is the right shape, and the public pipelines racing it are already blocking plus LightGBM plus a threshold.
- The last time a product-ER programming contest crowned a winner, the thing that separated 1st from 2nd was either runtime at a tied F (2020) or an unpublished recall gap between a tiny embedding index plus Jaccard re-rank and a pure set-similarity join (2022). Neither is “train a bigger matcher.”
- OAEI instance matching and SNOMED span linking are the nearest finished shared tasks, and both are a different problem (KG alignment; clinical entity linking). Using their deltas as if they were place or product dedup would be a category error.

### Gaps

- SIGMOD 2022 official average recall and runtime for WBSG and for April. The leaders HTML table did not return rows. 52.9% and 1914 s exist only in an 11th-place README.
- SIGMOD 2021 second place and the official average F. Only the winner’s three per-dataset F-scores were found.
- Lessons-paper body beyond the abstract and the opening participation counts (the PDF fetch was blocked). Any score table inside that paper was not read.
- Exact Amazon 2026 model-license and parameter cap, and whether external geocoders or web lookup are forbidden. Entrants mention an audit and one says no external lookup; the clause text was not captured.
- No organizer-published winner, or even a frozen leaderboard, for Amazon ML Challenge 2026.
- Second-place private score for Make Data Count 2025.
- DogMa’s method (rules vs embedding vs LLM) for OAEI 2025.
- Hugging Face license string for `xtremedistil-l6-h256-uncased`.
- A fresh primary Foursquare or Shopee writeup with a lesson different from the ones already known. This pass did not re-read those threads.

## Did a hand rule or a decision-layer change beat a fine-tuned transformer, and by how much?

### Takeaway

Yes, once, with a large and well-documented gap, but in 2020 rather than 2023–2026: on SIGMOD 2020 camera ER, human regex and canonical-model equality (F 0.99) beat the same team’s blocking-plus-DeepMatcher cascade (F 0.47) by about 0.52 F, and every finalist was a non-neural system tied at 0.99. In 2024, on a different task (SNOMED CT entity linking), a dictionary beat a fine-tuned encoder by 0.0008 IoU, a gap the organizers say is not statistically significant, while both crushed a BERT two-stage benchmark (0.1794) by about 0.24. No 2022–2026 product or business ER winner was found whose published margin over second place was a threshold, a cluster repair, or a one-slice rule rather than the rest of the system.

### Cited Findings

- SIGMOD 2020, rules vs a cascade that was already blocking plus a neural matcher. UniMoRe’s DeepMatcher RNN on the full camera set: F 0.47, recall 0.85, precision 0.32, after token blocking that itself had recall 0.99 and precision 0.28 on the labelled pairs. Their shipped regex system: F 0.99, precision 0.99, recall 0.98. Delta on the secret set: +0.52 F (0.99 − 0.47). The abstract’s ceiling for the ML attempt is “not reaching 0.49.” On labelled W the same RNN scored F 0.96, so the labelled-set number and the secret-set number disagreed by about 0.49 F. They name the failure mode: matches turn on brand-specific one-character or one-digit edits, and many brands in the full set never appear in W, so similarity features do not transfer. — [Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)
- SIGMOD 2020, 1st vs 2nd was not accuracy. Five finalists, all F 0.99. The winner’s extra change after a 0.97 mock-label system was ~200 manual labels (to tie at 0.99) plus a sorted-integer-set matcher that made resolution 0.61 s versus the runner-up’s 10.65 s on the winner’s laptop. Score delta vs 2nd: 0.00 F. The decision layer that created the tie was label equality, not a bigger model. — [Blacher et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf)
- SIGMOD 2021 winner is the same family: regex key fields, alias correction, equality on the resulting id, with a secondary-key recycle for incomplete rows. Reported F 0.983 / 0.986 / 0.927 on X2 / X3 / X4. No transformer baseline and no second-place delta were published in the sources found. — [Winner README](https://github.com/yinpeiqi/SIGMOD-2021-program-contest)
- SIGMOD 2022 winner did use a fine-tuned transformer, but a deliberately tiny one, and the decision layer is explicit: re-rank FAISS neighbors by the mean of cosine and Jaccard, then cut at a hard pair budget (1M / 2M). There is no ablation that isolates the Jaccard re-rank’s recall delta. Second place used no transformer at all (Jaccard join + overlap join + weighted rank + fixed output count). Without April’s official recall, it is not valid to say the neural system won by a measured amount, only that the organizers ranked WBSG first and April second. — [WBSG README](https://github.com/abrinkmann/acm_sigmoid_2022_challenge); [Mannheim announcement](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/dws/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/); [April README](https://github.com/rutgers-db/SIGMOD2022-Programming-Contest-Public)
- SNOMED CT Entity Linking Challenge (DrivenData, concluded March 2024). Not record linkage. Metric: class-macro character IoU. Organizer benchmark was already a cascade in the span sense: fine-tuned BERT span detector, then a linker, score 0.1794. 1st KIRI dictionary 0.4202; 2nd SNOBERT encoder (NER then concept classification) 0.4194; 3rd MITEL-UNIUD LLM span prompt plus FAISS retrieval plus LLM classification 0.3777. Delta 1st vs 2nd: +0.0008. Bootstrap 95% CIs overlap (KIRI 0.408–0.433, SNOBERT 0.407–0.432); organizers say the difference is not significant at α = 0.05. Delta vs the BERT benchmark: +0.2408. KIRI’s own account: they started toward an LLM, kept a dictionary as the baseline, and switched effort to the dictionary because it was better on this data. Post-process: prefer longer mentions and section-specific keys; expand mentions using SNOMED relations. On concepts absent from training, dictionary IoU 0.270 vs 0.235 and 0.231 for the neural systems; on seen concepts, 0.526 vs 0.521 vs 0.470. — [JAMIA paper](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/9/1397/8199759); [DrivenData winner post](https://drivendata.co/blog/snomed-ct-entity-linking-challenge-winners)
- KIRI is eligible (dictionary, no large model). SNOBERT is an encoder; size was not checked but the description is a segmentation network, not an >8B LLM. MITEL’s LLM is unspecified; treat 3rd place as possibly ineligible until the checkpoint is known. — [DrivenData winner post](https://drivendata.co/blog/snomed-ct-entity-linking-challenge-winners)
- Weak analog, not linkage: Learning Agency Lab PII detection (Kaggle). The official winner walkthrough says post-processing in total moved public LB by about +0.009 and private LB by +0.007, and that the gap mattered because standings were close. Final ensemble private score stated as 0.96988. Second-place score was not retrieved, so this is not a proven “post-process won the contest” delta. Task is NER, not dedup. — [Kaggle solution walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v98oFyLj0CA)
- A 2026 methods paper, not a contest, states the same decision-layer split on six ER benchmarks: precision needs hard rule vetoes, recall needs more diverse blocking, and one false-positive link can chain-merge unrelated entities if transitivity is assumed. Under ≤10k training rows, LightGBM and DeepMatcher each won 3/6 benchmarks on pair-F1 and a GAT won none (restaurants LightGBM 0.969 vs DeepMatcher 0.948; Cora DeepMatcher 0.968 vs LightGBM 0.891). Not a leaderboard result. — [arXiv:2607.26298](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26298v1)

### Inferences

- Where a hand rule beat a fine-tuned neural matcher by a margin that would matter, the setting was identifier-like strings (camera model tokens) and a labelled sample that did not cover the brands in the hidden set. The cascade’s labelled F (0.96) was the misleading number; secret F (0.47) was the real one.
- A dictionary beating an encoder by 0.0008 on SNOMED should not be copied as “rules beat transformers by a lot.” The real gap there is both systems versus a weak BERT benchmark (+0.24), and the dictionary’s extra value is concentrated on concepts missing from training (+0.035 to +0.039 IoU).
- SIGMOD 2022 does not support dropping the neural blocker. It supports keeping the blocker small and letting a symbolic score (Jaccard) re-rank it, under a pair budget. The gain of that re-rank was not measured in the writeups found.

### Gaps

- Any 2023–2026 business/product ER contest in which a threshold, cluster repair, candidate-union, or one-slice rule beat a fine-tuned transformer by a published delta. Not found.
- Ablation of WBSG’s cosine-plus-Jaccard re-rank versus cosine alone.
- April’s recall, which is the missing 2022 delta.
- PII 2024 second-place private score, needed to know whether +0.007 post-processing was the winning margin.
- SNOMED 3rd-place LLM name and parameter count.

## Did any winner ignore validation because it ranked submissions backward, and what did they ship?

### Takeaway

No 2022–2026 entity-resolution winner was found who wrote that validation ordered their submissions backward and that they therefore shipped a different system. The closest primary evidence is SIGMOD 2020, where a fine-tuned neural matcher’s labelled-set F (0.96 on W, 0.98 on Y) collapsed to 0.47 on the secret full set, and the teams that tied for the win shipped mock labels or regex instead. That is a validation reversal of method class, not a Kaggle-style choice between two submissions. Foursquare’s known public-leaderboard leak was not re-opened.

### Cited Findings

- On SIGMOD 2020 the organizer feedback during the contest was F on a hidden portion of D, and the last pre-deadline submission was the one ranked. Separately, UniMoRe report that DeepMatcher/Magellan looked strong on the released labelled pairs (RNN F 0.96 on W, F 0.9826 on Y after title truncation) and failed on full D (F 0.47). They shipped the regex system (F 0.99). The winner shipped mock-label equality (F 0.99) and states that semi-automatic labels alone were F 0.97 until ~200 inspected labels were added. Neither paper says “our validation ranking was the reverse of the leaderboard, so we picked the worse CV run.” — [Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf); [Blacher et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf)
- Make Data Count 2025 (weak analog, citation typing, not ER) shows a large split inside one winning system, not a deliberate discard of CV: full-solution train F1 0.720 (basic) vs 0.889 (adjusted) vs public 0.890 vs private 0.797. DOI-only private 0.350 vs accession-only private 0.664. The indexed 1st-place text does not say they overrode a backward validation ranking. — [1st-place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/make-data-count-finding-data-references/writeups/1st-place-solution)
- Amazon ML Challenge 2026 entrants, not winners, and the contest was still running: one reports validation 0.8899 and a leaderboard 0.875 (same direction, small drop); the other reports a flat threshold curve (0.9725 to 0.9783 from t = 0.3 to 0.7) and says a distractor-shifted rescore stays at 0.9759. Neither describes a reversed ranking. — [DevanshSrajput README](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge); [Siva documentation](https://github.com/Siva402-ai/amazon-ml-challenge-2026/blob/main/Documentation_template.md)

### Inferences

- The reproducible pattern in the ER contests that exist is not “trust the public LB over CV.” It is “a pair-classifier F on a small labelled sample can be far above F on the full dirty set,” and the systems that won had a decision rule whose errors a person could inspect (unmatched titles, missing model tokens).
- If Amazon 2026 finishes with a public/private reversal, that result does not exist yet. Copying Konwinski’s “ignore CV” line onto this ER task would be importing a strategy from a contest with six training rows and an unstable code-repair metric.

### Gaps

- A primary 2022–2026 ER writeup that says validation and the leaderboard ranked the same team’s submissions in opposite orders, plus the submission they actually selected. Not found. Foursquare 2022 is the known case and was out of scope for a rehash.
- Whether SIGMOD 2020’s in-contest hidden-slice feedback ever ranked a neural submission above the regex submission for the same team. The papers compare the two methods on labelled data vs full data; they do not publish a submission-by-submission reversal table.

## What exists on one-to-many matching (several noisy duplicates per reference, mean about 3) rather than one-to-one clustering?

### Takeaway

Amazon ML Challenge 2026 is that problem, and it is unfinished. Two independent entrant EDAs of the training labels agree that a matched Source 1 entity has about 3.7 links (one of them says up to 11), while each Source 2/3 record links to at most one Source 1. The scored decision is therefore assignment of noisy records onto a clean reference, with macro F0.5, not transitive clustering. SIGMOD 2020–2021 are many-to-many dirty ER inside one file (pairwise F, clusters emitted as pair lists). SIGMOD 2022 never matches; it only returns a capped candidate list.

### Cited Findings

- Problem statement: Source 1 is deduplicated. Each Source 1 entity may match zero, one, or many Source 2 and Source 3 records. Example in the statement: truth {S2-00047, S3-00812} against a prediction of three ids gives precision 2/3, recall 1, F0.5 0.714. Macro average is over Source 1 entities. — [Challenge PDF](https://d8it4huxumps7.cloudfront.net/files/6ab674645103d_emails_comms_amazon_ml_challenge_2026.pdf); [problem README mirror](https://github.com/venkatanaveen2078909-rgb/AMAZON/blob/main/6ab10eb3b23ba_student_resource/student_resource/README.md)
- Entrant EDA, Siva402-ai, measured on training labels (not an organizer statistic): 2.21M Source 1 entities, 10.3M Source 2+3 records, 7.64M true links. Every matched Source 2/3 record links to exactly one Source 1. Matches do not cross countries. 26% of Source 2/3 records match nothing. 5.6% of Source 1 entities are singletons. A matched Source 1 has 3.7 links on average, maximum 11. Their test prediction file, which is not ground truth: 1,732,544 Source 1 rows and 5.86M predicted matched records. They also say test has more Source 2/3 rows per Source 1 than train (5.8 vs 4.7), which they interpret as ~40% distractors vs 26% in train. — [Documentation_template.md](https://github.com/Siva402-ai/amazon-ml-challenge-2026/blob/main/Documentation_template.md)
- Entrant EDA, DevanshSrajput, same contest, same direction: singletons 5.58%; matched entities average 3.67 matches. They argue that a precision-weighted F0.5 does not mean “abstain when unsure,” because singletons are only 5.58% and the typical reference has several true links; predict-nothing scores 0.0558 macro F0.5. Eight Indic scripts appear in Source 2/3 while Source 1 names are Latin; their hand transliteration recovers 81% of true pairs that otherwise share no name token. A third note in their PRD (25 Sep 2026): France is 15.0% of test Source 1 (259,452 entities) and has zero training labels. That France figure is one team’s EDA. — [DevanshSrajput README](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge); [PRD](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge/blob/main/docs/PRD.md)
- Both pipelines force one-to-many in the safe direction: many Source 2/3 records may attach to one Source 1, but a Source 2/3 record is given at most one Source 1 (best candidate above a threshold). That matches the training constraint they measured (a noisy record never belongs to two references). It is not union-find clustering and it does not transitively merge two Source 1 entities. — [Siva documentation](https://github.com/Siva402-ai/amazon-ml-challenge-2026/blob/main/Documentation_template.md); [DevanshSrajput README](https://github.com/DevanshSrajput/Amazon-ML-Challenge)
- SIGMOD 2020 is the other many-match setting, but dirty-dirty: organizers applied transitive closure on camera matches, and both the winner and the runner-up emit all pairs inside a cluster (mock-label id, or canonical brand+model). Mean cluster size was not reported in the papers read. Accessories and bundles are in the file and are not camera entities. — [Blacher et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf); [Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)
- SIGMOD 2021 output is also all pairs that share an identification, within each dataset separately. X4 is the weak slice (F 0.927, precision 0.880, recall 0.980), i.e. a precision failure on that dataset rather than a recall failure. No multiplicity statistic. — [Winner README](https://github.com/yinpeiqi/SIGMOD-2021-program-contest)
- SIGMOD 2022 does not solve matching. Recall is “true matches retained / true matches in ground truth,” and systems are forced to stop at 1M (D1) or 2M (D2) pairs. A high-recall blocker can still be a bad matcher; the contest never scored the matcher. — [Contest archive](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/2022); [WBSG README](https://github.com/abrinkmann/acm_sigmoid_2022_challenge)

### Inferences

- The structure “clean reference, mean about 3.7 noisy copies, each copy belongs to at most one reference, metric is macro F0.5” is documented only as training-set EDA on Amazon ML Challenge 2026, by two entrants, not as an organizer white paper. The agreement (3.7 vs 3.67; singletons 5.6% vs 5.58%) makes the multiplicity plausible, but it is not a contest result.
- Because the noisy side is many-to-one onto the reference, cluster repair and union-find are the wrong post-process: they can glue two Source 1 entities through a shared false neighbor. The decision layer the entrants actually use is a per-record argmax plus a threshold. That is assignment, not clustering.
- F0.5 plus a 5.6% singleton rate punishes abstention. A threshold tuned on pair-F1, or a “when in doubt, drop the link” rule copied from a one-to-one dedup contest, is the change most likely to move the score in the wrong direction. One entrant’s sweep shows the macro-F0.5 curve is flat from 0.5 to 0.8 (0.9773 to 0.9774), so threshold noise of 0.1 is worth about 0.001 on their fold, not a win by itself.
- The slice that looks like a rule opportunity, if that EDA holds, is script and country: Indic transliteration is credited with lifting candidate recall on Indic-script records from 91.3% to 99.4% in one repo, and France is claimed to be 15% of test with no training labels. Both claims are entrant measurements during a live contest.

### Gaps

- Organizer-published mean number of matches per Source 1, singleton rate, and country mix. Only entrant EDA.
- Any finished contest from 2022–2026 with the same one-reference-to-several-duplicates structure and a published winning margin. Not found.
- Mean cluster size for SIGMOD 2020 cameras.
- Whether Amazon’s final audit allows the Indic dictionary and forbids web lookup or geocoders. Not confirmed from the official rules text.
