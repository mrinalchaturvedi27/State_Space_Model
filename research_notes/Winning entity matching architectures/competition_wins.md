# Winning architectures in large-scale entity-matching competitions

Scope note (2026-09-26): Closest contests are Kaggle Foursquare Location Matching (2022, noisy multilingual POI name/address/lat-lon, one-to-many, ~1.5M rows), Kaggle Shopee Price Match Guarantee (2021, product image+title matching, one-to-many, ~70k test rows), and ACM SIGMOD Programming Contests 2020 (camera specs), 2021 (multi-domain ER), and 2022 (blocking on ~1M-row product sets). No later (2023–2026) large-scale company/customer/POI record-linkage contest with a documented winner was found. None of the primary metrics below is precision-at-a-fixed-high-recall; they are mean Jaccard, row-wise/micro F1, F-score, or recall inside a hard candidate budget. Kaggle writeup HTML did not render via direct fetch; quotations below are from search-indexed text of the primary writeup URLs, cross-checked across snippets. Where a number exists only in a secondhand summary, that is labeled.

## What was the winning (and 2nd/3rd if documented) architecture of the Kaggle Foursquare Location Matching competition (2022)?

### Takeaway

Official 1st–3rd (July 2022, mean row-wise IoU, leaked train/test overlap) were all multi-stage: high-recall blocking, a pair classifier (GBDT and/or multilingual encoders), a threshold plus graph-style merge, then an optional train–test identity join. The largest official-leaderboard jump was that leak, not the model family. On Kaggle’s later non-overlapping rerun, official 1st (re:waiwai) fell from overlap private 0.977683 to 0.920594 (4th); the best published clean score in the rerun table is team “Ri” at 0.932782, whose pipeline was not found.

### Cited Findings

Competition setup (14 Apr 2022–7 Jul 2022; host Foursquare; 1,079 teams; 22,050 submissions; prizes $12,500 / $7,500 / $5,000):

- Task: predict which of “over one-and-a-half million” noisy place rows (name, street address, coordinates, plus categories/city/state/etc.) are the same POI. Match relation is one-to-many: each `id` submits a space-delimited list of match ids, rows always self-match, and a believed cluster {A,B,C} must be written symmetrically on every member’s row. — [Kaggle competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching)
- Metric: mean per-row Intersection over Union (Jaccard) between predicted and ground-truth match sets. Not precision@k and not pair-F1. — [Kaggle competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching)
- Code competition: CPU notebook ≤ 9 hours or GPU notebook ≤ 9 hours; internet disabled; “Freely & publicly available external data is allowed, including pre-trained models”; file must be `submission.csv`. — [Kaggle competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching)

Official 1st, team re:waiwai, writeup by takoihiraokazu, 9 Jul 2022. Four stages plus a leak merge. — [1st place solution](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution)

- Validation: 2-fold; “In the training for submit, we used all of the 2 fold data.”
- Stage 1, candidates: for each id, 100 neighbors by Euclidean distance on lat/lon and 100 by cosine of a name embedding (`bert-base-multilingual-uncased`, cuML KNN). A LightGBM per retrieval mode, using only a few features (name Jaro, category Jaro were the examples given), keeps the top 20 from each mode (~40 candidates). Inference via ForestInference. Stated “Max IOU: 0.979”.
- Stage 2, cheap ranker: ~120 features drawn from the public LightGBM baseline (character similarities including Levenshtein and Jaro-Winkler). LightGBM, ForestInference, CV 0.875, threshold 0.01, “The number of candidates was reduced to about 10%.”
- Stage 3, heavy ranker: CatBoost on the same features, CV 0.878; `xlm-roberta-large` on text `name + categories + address + city + state` plus ~70 stage-2 features, 3 epochs; `mdeberta-v3-base` on the same text plus ~90 features (added Manhattan and haversine), trained with FGM and EMA, 4 epochs, CV 0.907. They also trained overfit variants after they suspected train/test overlap, then “finally used models that had the highest CV and did not overfit train data.”
- Ensemble (their spelling): “2nd lgb * 0.01 + 3rd catboost * 0.32 + 3rd xlm-roberta-large * 0.29 + 3rd medebeta-v3-base * 0.38”, CV 0.911, threshold 0.5. The LightGBM term is almost a residual (weight 0.01).
- Stage 4, post-process: merge two ids’ match lists if the shared ids exceed 50% from either side; rescore the new pairs with `xlm-roberta-large` at threshold 0.02. CV 0.9166.
- Leak (“Merge train”): join train and test on name, lat, lon. (1) add all train true-positive pairs keyed by POI; (2) drop false-positive pairs among ids tied to train; (3) drop pairs between an id tied to train and an id not tied to train. On a stage-2 submission’s LB: without merge 0.900; (1) alone 0.943; (1)+(2)+(3) 0.971. They were unsure (3) would hold on private data, so final submissions were both (1)+(2) and (1)+(2)+(3).
- Their own Private / Public: overfit `mdeberta-v3-base` (no dropout, 8 epochs) + `xlm-roberta-large` (8 epochs): without train merge 0.946 / 0.947, with train merge 0.976 / 0.976. “Non over fit version1” (the pipeline above): without train merge 0.941 / 0.941, with train merge 0.977 / 0.978. Version2 with only (1)+(2): 0.966 / 0.966. These “without merge” numbers are still on the overlapping test set, not the later clean rerun.
- Authors’ own caveat: “it is truly regrettable that there was leakage”; they suspected it because “There is a large gap between CV and LB. Max IOU is already high, so a gap of this magnitude is unnatural.”

Official 2nd, writeups 9 Jul 2022 by T0m (tomyanabe) and colum2131, with teammates no9more9ria10, tkm2261, yukia18. Authors’ label: “2nd place solution in LB 0.949 (LB 0.971 with leak).” A separate official private-LB cell for this team was not in the rerun excerpt. — [T0m part](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336072); [colum2131 part](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336090); indexed copy on [yukia18 writeups](https://www.kaggle.com/yukia18/writeups)

- Candidate generation (T0m): 128 nearest by lat/lon distance and 128 nearest by name TF-IDF cosine, then a country-specific mix down to 128 total. “Max IoU 0.9895”.
- Transformer blocking: absolute features (lat, lon, country embedding, categories embedding, `xlm-roberta-base` name embedding, TF-IDF-SVD of name and of all text) and relative features (edit distance on name, address, zipcode, phone, url, categories; TF-IDF-SVD cosine). “single LB: 0.918”. Keep pairs with threshold 0.005 and union-find. “average candidates per id 4.1”. “Max IoU 0.986”.
- Stack (colum2131): binary match classification with XGBoost, LightGBM, and `xlm-roberta-base`. GBDT features: per-side lat/lon, ordinal country, ordinal categories; pairwise edit distances on name, categories, address, city, state, zip, url, phone using gesh, Levenshtein, Jaro, and RapidFuzz ratios (`simple_ratio`, `partial_ratio`, `token_set_ratio`, `token_sort_ratio`, `token_ratio`, `partial_token_ratio`, `wratio`, `qratio`); set similarity; char TF-IDF n-grams (1, 3); and the transformer probability. BERT text: lowercased name and categories joined by a SEP token, plus country; log haversine concatenated into a linear layer after the backbone.
- Training leak, separate from the post-process: they replaced `GroupKFold(n_splits=2, group='point_of_interest')` with `GroupKFold(n_splits=5, group='src_id')` so the same records could fall on both sides of the split. “This leakage improved the CV and Public LB because train and test data contained common records.”
- Leak post-process: because `train.csv` is swapped at submit time, they kept a private copy of train, joined test to train on `name`, `latitude`, `longitude`, and overwrote matches with the train POI groups. “The prediction of LB0.949 was overwritten.” Indexed code is on the yukia18 writeup page.

Official 3rd, Psi, writeup 19 Jul 2022. Deep-learning only; no GBDT. — [3rd place solution](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/psi-3rd-place-solution)

- Stage 1: ArcFace metric-learning model that predicts the POI id, so duplicates land near each other. Input is most columns separated by SEP tokens. Latitude and longitude are written into the string and split every third digit so the tokenizer can use them. Example record in the writeup: `<s> Tesco HQ</s></s> Offices</s></s> 18 963 587</s></s> 47 450 562</s>…`
- Stage 2: bi-encoder(s) trained on in-sample candidate pairs from stage 1 to classify true vs false pairs. Pair text interleaves both records’ fields (name, lat, lon, category, country, state, city, zip, address), again SEP-separated. The writeup says “various bi-encoder models”; the specific checkpoint names in the middle of the post were not recovered.
- Decision: blend stage-1 and stage-2 probabilities and cut at a threshold. “I applied some minor QE post processing, but not much was helping additionally.”
- Psi “never suspected the leak” and “never actively searched for it.” Claims the same pipeline “scores very competitively on non-overlapping data” and that the contest was solved “with deep learning only.” Exact self-reported private LB was not in the recovered text. Organizer rerun (below) puts Psi at overlap private 0.967847 and non-overlap private 0.919698.

Official 4th is not a prize place but is the clearest GBDT-only ablation. Youri Matiounine, Vincent Schuler, Théo Viel, 9 Jul 2022. — [4th place solution](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/youri-vincent-th-o-4th-place-solution); code [TheoViel/kaggle_foursquare](https://github.com/TheoViel/kaggle_foursquare)

- Clean names (strip special characters, Unidecode, lower). Build category families and, per family, distances covering 50/75/90/95% of true matches. Also alias-group names, cities, and states.
- Candidates from several rules: near neighbors, name similarity, shared name words, grouped-category equality, cleaned phone equality, same address, TF-IDF (called out for airports and Indian places).
- Two LightGBMs because of memory: a 5-fold model on a few features (distance, name similarity) drops pairs below 0.007; a 20-fold model on “more than 200 features” makes the decision. Both trained on the full ~1.1M-row train set “which was not possible on Kaggle and had to be done elsewhere due to memory constraints.”
- Post-process that they kept: thresholds that depend on the sizes of the groups being merged (“merging 2 places has not the same impact … than merging 2 groups of 10 places”). Graph ideas and a third LGBM on neighbor context were tried and were not the key.
- Leak, found on the last day: key = `cleaned_name + round(latitude, 5) + round(longitude, 5)`. If that key hits train, copy train match ids. “boosted our score from 0.939 (15th) to 0.957 (4th).”

Other documented gold-region pipelines (not prize places), useful because they publish ablations:

- 7th / 1083 as claimed by the authors (Kaggle’s own team count is 1,079 — small conflict): candidate generation, binary classification, then union-find, then NetworkX betweenness-centrality edge pruning, then keep pairs at graph distance ≤ 2. Writeup date on the blog 19 Jul 2022. Full score path not extracted. — [Future Architect blog](https://future-architect.github.io/articles/20220720a/)
- 8th, “SBERT + LightGBM”: distance kNN plus kNN on fine-tuned Sentence-BERT embeddings; LightGBM binary classification; post-processing. Detailed thresholds not recovered. — [ockie1729 writeups](https://www.kaggle.com/mofumofuchan/writeups)
- 11th / 1083 as claimed by the authors (MCD3): per place, top-50 embedding neighbors plus top-10 distance neighbors; a separate GBDT per neighbor rank; cuML ForestInference at inference. Public-LB path they list: 0.922 `bert-base-multilingual-cased`; 0.928 plus `xlm-roberta-base` (2-model ensemble); 0.931 more GBDT features; 0.940 fill missing fields; 0.945 two more BERTs (4-ensemble); 0.947 Dijkstra graph post-process. They also note a GNN (the 13th-place / clean-2nd team) is a richer graph than Dijkstra, and that without the leak that team would have been 2nd. — [MCD3 blog](https://www.mcd3.co.jp/blog/techblog/so-nakashima-4sq)
- 51st, silver, no ensemble: ArcFace `xlm-roberta`, k=10 cosine kNN on the concatenated string plus lat/lon kNN, then LightGBM (FLAML) on cosine, distance, LCS, TF-IDF. Shows the same skeleton one tier down. — [Simple Silver Solution](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/dongkyun-kim-simple-silver-solution)
- 37th (authors; 36th public / 37th private, silver): BallTree haversine (top-30 and all points within 200 m) plus other candidate generators; stage-1 recall 0.9786 only after union, each generator worse alone; post-process is symmetry plus transitivity. — [Trokhymovych, 19 Jul 2022](https://medium.com/@trokhymovych/place-deduplication-solution-overview-afbbfc08f55f)

### Inferences

- The architecture that actually took the money is a cascade, not a single model: geo kNN ∪ name-embedding or TF-IDF kNN → tiny GBDT or transformer gate → string-similarity GBDT and/or a cross-encoder (`xlm-roberta-large`, `mdeberta-v3-base`, CatBoost) → threshold → cluster repair (50% overlap merge, union-find, or a GNN) → and, for the official top 4, a train/test key join.
- On the leaked leaderboard, decision-time use of train labels dominated model choice: re:waiwai’s non-overfit stack moved 0.941 → 0.977, and 4th place moved 0.939 (15th) → 0.957. Psi’s neural-only entry still got official 3rd (overlap private 0.967847) without hunting the leak, so a strong ranker alone was enough for a prize on the dirty test, but not for 1st.
- On the clean rerun the prize order is not the scientific order. re:waiwai 0.920594 and Psi 0.919698 are nearly tied once overlap is removed; both sit behind an unpublished “Ri” system (0.932782) and behind team merge master’s GNN cascade (0.922937). Treat 0.92–0.93 mean IoU as the honest 2022 ceiling that was written down, and 0.97+ as leak.
- One-to-many scoring (per-row Jaccard, symmetric clusters) is why winners spent effort on union-find / shared-neighbor merge / group-size-dependent thresholds rather than on independent pair decisions.

## Are there other competition wins for company matching, customer dedup, POI matching, or product matching?

### Takeaway

Three real record-linkage contests have documented winners besides Foursquare: Shopee product matching (2021; embedding retrieval + union/thresholds, private 0.780) and SIGMOD ER 2020 and 2021 (small product catalogs; rules beat published neural baselines, runtime broke F-score ties). SIGMOD 2022 is blocking-only on ~1M products (recall at a fixed pair budget); the winner was a distilled transformer plus FAISS plus Jaccard, not a large LM and not pure rules. No company-name or customer-dedup contest winner was found. Span-linking contests (SemEval entity linking, DrivenData SNOMED 2024) are the wrong task.

### Cited Findings

**Shopee - Price Match Guarantee (Kaggle), 8 Mar 2021–10 May 2021. Product matching, not name/address.** Strong analog for one-to-many matching and for the match-decision layer; weak analog for geo blocking and for multi-million candidate sets (hidden test “roughly 70,000 images”; group size capped at 50). Prizes $15,000 / $10,000 / $5,000. Code competition (notebook submit). — [Competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching); [data page](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/data)

- Private leaderboard (≈60% of test): 1st Upstage, Making AI Beneficial **0.780**; 2nd lyaka & tkm **0.779**; 3rd Btbpanda **0.779**; 4th Watercooled 0.777; 5th tereka ∪ Ahmet ∪ Alvor 0.767. — [Leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/leaderboard)
- Metric name was not re-read from the official evaluation paragraph in this pass. 2nd place describes the objective as micro-F1 and up-weights small label groups (“Weighted sample with 1 / (label group size) ** 0.4”) because “It is important to predict smaller label groups for micro-F1 score.” — [2nd place solution](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238022)

1st place, Upstage, writeup “From Embeddings to Matches” (Kaggle stamp 13 Jul 2022, after the contest). — [Discussion 238136](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238136)

- Encoders: two `eca_nfnet_l1` image towers (timm); text towers `xlm-roberta-large`, `xlm-roberta-base`, `cahya/bert-base-indonesian-1.5G`, `indobenchmark/indobert-large-p1`, `bert-base-multilingual-uncased`. Loss: ArcFace (batch-norm and L2-norm after pooling; arc margin then softmax). Margin 0.8–1.0 for image, 0.6–0.8 for text. Training stabilizers they list: gradual margin increase, long warmup, larger LR on the cosine head, gradient clipping.
- Author’s running score path (these are development LB figures, higher than the 0.780 private prize score; do not treat 0.793 as the private score): image-only baseline 0.70; text-only 0.64; concat then normalize 0.724; “min2” 0.743; normalize then concat 0.753; full-data training 0.757; union of combined, image, and text matches plus threshold tuning 0.776; Iterative Neighborhood Blending (INB) plus more text models 0.784; using image, text, and combined embeddings inside INB stage 1 and tuning thresholds jointly 0.793.
- INB: their name for a pipeline built from query expansion (QE) and database-side augmentation (DBA). They say most of the idea is shared with QE/DBA “but some details are different.” Neighborhood search, then blend neighbor embeddings, iterated over a list of thresholds.
- Decision layer: they count 10 thresholds (stage-1 text/image/combined, stage-2, stage-3, two stage-1 thresholds that connect straight into the final union, and min2 thresholds). “major score boost came from stage2 and stage3 threshold”; at the end “we only had to tune those 2 numbers against LB.” A 2nd-place summary of this solution adds: matches are the union of image and text neighborhoods; singletons get a looser threshold. — [lyakaap speaker deck, 15 Jul 2021](https://speakerdeck.com/lyakaap/shopee-2nd-place-solutiontoshang-wei-jie-fa-matome)

2nd place, lyakaap & tkm, private 0.779 (public also 2nd of 2426 teams, per the same deck). Primary writeup 2021. — [Discussion 238022](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238022); [speaker deck](https://speakerdeck.com/lyakaap/shopee-2nd-place-solutiontoshang-wei-jie-fa-matome)

- Stage 1 retrieval embeddings. Deck’s summary row for 2nd: image NFNet and DeiT; text BERT, XLM, TF-IDF; loss CurricularFace (they say it beat ArcFace and other ~Face losses by focusing on easy samples early and hard samples late); QE/DBA used.
- Candidates are not all pairs: “We make pairs only union of 50 nearest neighbors using image similarity and text similarity.”
- Stage 2 meta-classifier: LightGBM and a Graph Attention Network. Link-prediction view of the kNN graph. Deck: adding GAT moved LB 0.790 → 0.792.
- Ensemble rule: per model, subtract that model’s best local threshold, sum the centered scores, keep the pair if the sum is > 0, then drop edges that are not mutual.
- Score path after the team merge: 0.777 (TF-IDF + image embeddings, simple LightGBM); +Indonesian-BERT similarities 0.780; +multimodal similarities 0.783; +multilingual-BERT and paraphrase-XLM 0.788; +PageRank feature 0.789. Pre-merge individual scores were 0.767 and 0.765. (These intermediate numbers are the authors’ LB trail, not the final private cell; final private on the leaderboard is 0.779.)

3rd place, Btbpanda, private 0.779. No primary writeup was opened. The 2nd-place team’s summary table (15 Jul 2021) lists 3rd as: image EfficientNet, ViT (DINO), CLIP; text BERT, TF-IDF, CLIP; triplet loss; combiner CatBoost; no QE/DBA checkmark in that row. Treat as secondhand until the Btbpanda post is read. — [speaker deck](https://speakerdeck.com/lyakaap/shopee-2nd-place-solutiontoshang-wei-jie-fa-matome); score [leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/leaderboard)

What did not win Shopee:

- Image-only (0.70) and text-only (0.64) baselines inside the winning writeup.
- A pure “bigger model” story. 18th place (Slawek Biel): replacing the stack with `resnet18+bert+tfidf` still scored 0.749 (silver) after one submit, models trained in under 30 minutes on a 3090, single fold. The same author’s best model score with `nfnet_l0+nfnet_l1+Bert-large+tfidf` was 0.767, and the single largest jump they report is a post-process (“chiseling” predicted group-size distribution to match train) from 0.739 to 0.759. Adding the winner’s neighborhood blending later moved their kernel to 0.762/0.772. — [Discussion 237972](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/discussion/237972)
- No LLM appears in the 2021 winning pipelines (contest predates practical API LLMs, and it was a code competition). Not a tested negative result against modern LLMs.

**ACM SIGMOD 2020 Programming Contest — camera entity resolution.** ~30k JSON product specs from 24 e-commerce sites (cameras plus accessories and bundles). Matching task, F-measure, ties broken by runtime. Small-n analog: structured product attributes, not noisy street addresses and not millions of rows. Organized by Roma Tre. Winner of the contest (time tie-break): Mark Blacher, Julien Klaus, and Matthias Mitterreiter, Friedrich-Schiller-University Jena. — [Contest archive](http://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/2020.html)

- Winner’s method (their finalist paper): “mock labels” — sets of strings that stand in for the real-world entity, built from the training data, then matched with sorted integer sets. They report this was much faster in the resolution step than other top teams. They explicitly discarded slower unsupervised-learning resolution. — [Blacher et al., CEUR Vol-2726 paper2](http://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf)
- Accuracy leader, not the runtime winner: CheetahER (Nan Deng, Wendi Luan, Haotian Liu, Bo Tang, SUSTech) “ranks the 1st in accuracy among 53 teams and completes the task within 20 seconds.” Pipeline: careful cleaning, then a two-phase blocking framework that groups specs by brand and by model, with merge and split operators. “Even though some designs … are specialized for camera datasets.” Rule-structured, not a neural ranker. — [Deng et al., CEUR Vol-2726 paper5](http://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper5.pdf)
- Runner-up paper (Zecchini, Simonini, Bergamaschi): “existing state-of-the-art ML-based methods fall short … not reaching 0.49 F-score.” Their human-written annotated lists and regular expressions reached F 0.99, precision 0.99, recall 0.98. “Other 4 teams were able to reach the top result, so the tie was broken according to the execution time … concluding the contest as runner-up.” — [Zecchini et al., CEUR Vol-2726 paper3](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)
- So: pure neural / published ML matchers did not win (0.49 F vs 0.99 F). Pure rules did win the accuracy tie; the official winner is the fastest system among those high-F entries (mock labels), not a learned ranker. No commercial ER API is described.

**ACM SIGMOD 2021 Programming Contest — multi-dataset ER.** Progressive datasets “of different types (e.g., products, people)”; labeled pairs are transitively closed; score is F-score; hidden instances `Z_i` were not given to participants. Final submissions 30 Apr 2021. “All teams must agree to license their code under an OSI-approved open source license.” Winner: Weibao Fu, Peiqi Yin, and Lan Lu, SUSTech. — [Archive](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/2021); [task page, OSI clause](https://dbgroup.ing.unimo.it/sigmod21contest/task.shtml); [winner repo](https://github.com/yinpeiqi/SIGMOD-2021-program-contest)

- Winner pipeline (repo README), two stages, no neural net: (1) Data cleaning — lowercase, merge columns, extract key fields (e.g. brand) with regexes written from the datasets and from brand sites, map aliases to one form, impute missing keys from other fields on the same row. (2) Entity matching — if the key fields are complete, use them as an id (“complete matching”); else try secondary-key combinations to attach the row to an already solved id (“recycle”); else a looser residual id. Same id ⇒ match.

Whether these are hidden-test or local is not stated in the README. Public sample X4 is only 1,356 instances / 5 attributes / 193 entities; that sample size must not be treated as the hidden test size. — [winner README](https://github.com/yinpeiqi/SIGMOD-2021-program-contest); sample size [archive](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/2021)

Same repo’s internal comparison: Jaccard@0.75 F1 0.559; USE + agglomerative F1 0.88 local vs 0.744 on the leaderboard. — [vinitra/entity-matcher](https://github.com/vinitra/entity-matcher)

In 2021 machine learning achieved a certain relevance, considering for example binary random forest classifiers … or combining XGBoost with a rule-based matcher.” So ML was present among finalists, but the winning artifact that was published is the rule/id pipeline above, and a pure embedding+clustering entry at F1 0.744 was not the winner. — [De Angelis et al. **ACM SIGMOD 2022 Programming Contest — blocking only, not full matching.** Weak analog for the decision layer; the closest analog for multi-million candidate sets. Two product corpora, each “About 1000000” rows: D1 notebooks, candidate-set budget 1,000,000 pairs; D2 Altosight products, budget 2,000,000 pairs. Rank = average recall of true pairs retained; time breaks ties. 55 teams. Winner: WBSG, Alexander Brinkmann and Ralph Peeters, University of Mannheim (announced 3 May 2022). Runner-up: April, Chaoji Zuo and Zhizhi Wang, Rutgers. — [Task archive](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/2022); [finalists page](https://dbgroup.ing.unimore.it/sigmod22contest/leaders.shtml); [Mannheim news, 3 May 2022](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/)

- Hardware constraint stated by the winners: CPU only, 16 cores, 32 GB RAM, finish within 35 minutes. “Implementing a neural approach using Transformers given these restrictions is challenging.”
- Winner stages: (1) domain-specific normalization of title (D1) / name (D2); (2) group tuples with the same description; (3–4) embed descriptions with a task-specific fine-tuned distilled transformer and index with FAISS; (5) approximate nearest-neighbor search, producing ranked group pairs; (6) rerank by the average of neural cosine and Jaccard “to smooth the bias of the embedding model”; (7) expand groups to tuple pairs until the 1M (D1) or 2M (D2) budget is filled. — [Mannheim news](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/); same steps in [theodoratrz README quoting the winners](https://github.com/theodoratrz/sigmodContest2022) and the earlier GitHub overview in search snippets of `abrinkmann/acm_sigmoid_2022_challenge`
- Score, secondhand: 11th-place team BringBackML (NKUA) writes that 1st place average recall was **52.9%** at **1914 s**, versus their own 46.9% (71% on D1, 22.7% on D2). The official leaders HTML did not render numeric cells in this pass, so 52.9% / 1914 s is not independently verified. — [theodoratrz/sigmodContest2022](https://github.com/theodoratrz/sigmodContest2022)
- 2nd place (April) is the explicit non-neural counterpoint: sed cleaning, similarity grouping, Jaccard similarity join, overlap-set join, then rank by weighted similarity and cut to the budget. “Our approach doesn't rely on heavy data preprocessing or fancy manual rules” and does not use a transformer. — [rutgers-db/SIGMOD2022-Programming-Contest-Public](https://github.com/rutgers-db/SIGMOD2022-Programming-Contest-Public)
- Organizer retrospective: 2022 blocking finalists used sorted neighborhood, similarity joins, BERT sentence encodings, “a neural architecture based on a distilled transformer,” and supervised contrastive learning. Large transformers were not the winning point; a tiny one plus a symbolic rerank was. — [De Angelis et al.](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/47dab96f-c387-4c2d-804c-674a6c833c65/09_Reports_DeAngelis.pdf)

### Inferences

- When the catalog is small and the identity is a brand+model string (SIGMOD 2020/2021), hand-built extractors and exact keys won, and published ML matchers lost by a huge F-score gap (0.49 vs 0.99). That result does not transfer to Foursquare, where names are multilingual, abbreviated, and geographically noisy and the winners needed learned similarity.
- When the catalog is ~1M noisy products and the score is recall at a pair budget (SIGMOD 2022), the win is a tiny task-tuned transformer ANN index reranked by Jaccard, with a pure similarity-join system in 2nd. Average recall still only about 53% at 1–2M pairs, so blocking quality—not a second-stage ranker—was the whole contest.
- When the match is one-to-many and multimodal but the set is only ~70k (Shopee), ArcFace/CurricularFace embeddings plus a non-learned union and a carefully tuned threshold (1st, 0.780) essentially tied a GBDT+GAT reranker (2nd, 0.779) and a CatBoost reranker (3rd, 0.779, secondhand). The model family did not separate the podium; how neighborhoods were merged did.
- No contest in this set was won by a pure LLM, a commercial ER API, or an end-to-end neural model that skipped candidate generation.

### Gaps

- Shopee official metric sentence was not re-extracted; “micro-F1” is the 2nd-place team’s wording. Private 0.780 is the leaderboard, while 0.793 is the winner’s development trail.
- Shopee 3rd-place architecture is only the 2nd-place team’s table, not Btbpanda’s own post. Shopee notebook time limit was not copied off the rules page (a commenter asked whether 2nd place “fit into a 2 hour GPU sub,” which is not a rule citation).
- SIGMOD 2022 official recall 52.9% and 1914 s are from an 11th-place README, not a rendered official leaderboard cell.
- SIGMOD 2021 2nd/3rd pipelines were not opened. The retrospective says some 2021 teams used random forests or XGBoost-plus-rules, but those teams are not named in the recovered text.
- SIGMOD 2020/2022 license terms were not re-read (2021’s OSI clause was).
- OAEI instance-matching tracks, FEIII, and any 2023–2026 industry ER challenges were not verified. Absence from these searches is not proof none exist.

## In those wins, where did the gain come from: candidate generation, features, model family, or the decision/threshold layer?

### Takeaway

Candidate generation sets a hard recall ceiling and was the whole score at SIGMOD 2022. On Foursquare and Shopee the podium gaps after a decent blocker were mostly thresholding, cluster repair, and (Foursquare only) the leak—not swapping GBDT for a transformer. Model family mattered inside a band of about 0.02–0.04 mean IoU or a few thousandths of F1, except on the small SIGMOD camera task, where rules beat generic ML by ~0.5 F.

### Cited Findings

Candidate generation:

- Foursquare 1st: after geo-100 ∪ name-embedding-100 and a tiny LightGBM cut to ~40, max IoU already 0.979. Later models cannot recover a pair that never entered this set. — [1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution)
- Foursquare 2nd: geo ∪ name-TF-IDF, country-specific mix, max IoU 0.9895; after transformer blocking, max IoU still 0.986 at 4.1 candidates per id and single-model LB 0.918. Blocking discarded almost no true pairs relative to the 0.9895 ceiling, and the LB of 0.918 shows most of the remaining loss is precision / cluster assembly, not recall of the blocker. — [T0m](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336072)
- Foursquare 37th: stage-1 recall 0.9786 “only by combining different approaches … which independently had worse results.” — [Trokhymovych](https://medium.com/@trokhymovych/place-deduplication-solution-overview-afbbfc08f55f)
- Foursquare 11th ablation (their LB): a single multilingual BERT embedding baseline already 0.922; a second encoder 0.928; GBDT features 0.931; imputation 0.940; two more BERTs 0.945; Dijkstra post-process 0.947. The late graph step (+0.002) is smaller than cleaning and features, and the whole path from a decent embedding blocker to the full stack is +0.025. — [MCD3](https://www.mcd3.co.jp/blog/techblog/so-nakashima-4sq)
- SIGMOD 2022 score is recall at 1M or 2M pairs. Winner vs a competent similarity-join runner-up is exactly a blocking-quality gap. Reported winner recall 52.9% (unverified official cell) means even the best blocker missed about half of true pairs at that budget. — [task](https://transactional.blog/sigmod-contest/2022); [11th-place README](https://github.com/theodoratrz/sigmodContest2022)
- Shopee 2nd: pairs restricted to the union of 50 image neighbors and 50 text neighbors. All later GBDT/GAT gains happen inside that set. — [2nd place](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238022)

Features:

- Foursquare prize GBDT features are classic ER string metrics (Jaro, Levenshtein, RapidFuzz token ratios) plus haversine/Manhattan, ordinal country/category, and embedding cosines—not learned end-to-end. 1st place stage-2 LightGBM on ~120 such features: CV 0.875. — [1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution); [2nd colum2131](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336090)
- 4th place needed “more than 200 features” in the second LightGBM after a cheap distance/name gate. They also say category-specific distance priors and alias dictionaries were necessary setup, i.e. feature design rather than a new model class. — [4th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/youri-vincent-th-o-4th-place-solution)
- Shopee 2nd’s trail after a working image+TF-IDF LightGBM (0.777): Indonesian BERT similarities +0.003, multimodal similarities +0.003, more text towers +0.005, PageRank +0.001. Feature/embedding diversity, not a new family, accounts for the post-merge climb. — [2nd place](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238022)
- SIGMOD 2020/2021: the winning “features” are extracted brand/model keys and alias lists. Generic similarity features inside published ML matchers did not clear 0.49 F. — [Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf); [SUSTech 2021 README](https://github.com/yinpeiqi/SIGMOD-2021-program-contest)

Model family:

- Foursquare 1st, same candidate pool: LightGBM CV 0.875 → CatBoost CV 0.878 → `mdeberta-v3-base` CV 0.907 → weighted ensemble CV 0.911. The transformer reranker is worth about +0.03 CV over the GBDT; ensembling three heavy models adds +0.004. The ensemble weight on LightGBM is 0.01, i.e. the GBDT is not carrying the blend. — [1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution)
- Foursquare clean rerun: GBDT+transformer+GNN (team merge master, 0.922937) beats official 1st’s heavy ensemble once the leak is removed (0.920594) by a small margin, and both beat Psi’s deep-learning-only entry by only ~0.001–0.003 (Psi 0.919698). Model family did not decide the clean top group. The unpublished Ri system (0.932782) is +0.01 over the best published clean pipeline; its family is unknown. — [Discussion 338035](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/338035)
- Shopee podium: ArcFace union+INB 0.780, CurricularFace+LightGBM+GAT 0.779, triplet+CatBoost 0.779 (3rd architecture secondhand). Family swap is inside 0.001 private. — [Leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/leaderboard)
- Shopee 18th: `resnet18` instead of NFNet still silver (0.749) when the decision code is held fixed. Their model upgrade to NFNet+BERT-large reached 0.767, a smaller move than chiseling (0.739 → 0.759). — [Discussion 237972](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/discussion/237972)
- SIGMOD 2022: distilled transformer+FAISS+Jaccard beat pure similarity joins (2nd). The gain is “small neural retriever plus symbolic rerank,” not “larger model.” A full-size transformer was infeasible in 35 minutes on 16 CPU cores / 32 GB. — [Mannheim](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/); [April repo](https://github.com/rutgers-db/SIGMOD2022-Programming-Contest-Public)
- SIGMOD 2020: model family was the wrong investment. ML F < 0.49 vs rules F 0.99. — [Zecchini et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper3.pdf)

Decision / threshold / cluster layer:

- Foursquare 1st: threshold 0.5 on the ensemble, then a 50%-overlap merge, then a second threshold 0.02 on newly proposed pairs. CV 0.911 → 0.9166 (+0.0056) from that post-process. Leak rules on the same stage-2 submission: 0.900 → 0.943 → 0.971. The leak is an order of magnitude larger than the honest post-process, and larger than the transformer-vs-GBDT gap. — [1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution)
- Foursquare 2nd: union-find at 0.005 during blocking; final leak overwrite of an LB 0.949 prediction. — [T0m](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336072); [yukia18](https://www.kaggle.com/yukia18/writeups)
- Foursquare 4th: “the key idea was to adapt the thresholds to the sizes of the groups we were merging,” then the leak (+0.018, 15th → 4th). — [4th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/youri-vincent-th-o-4th-place-solution)
- Foursquare clean 2nd: GNN on the 2-hop graph, about +0.02 CV and LB, the author’s “most important part,” still smaller than the leak deltas above. — [GNN writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/team-merge-master-13th-place-solution-gnn)
- Shopee 1st: union of image/text/combined matches  → 0.776 from a 0.757 full-data embedding model; INB plus joint threshold tuning → 0.793 on their trail. They explicitly say the major boost, once thresholds were in a sane range, was two stage-2/stage-3 cutoffs. — [1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238136)
- Shopee 2nd decision rule (center each model by its own threshold, sum, require mutual edges) is a threshold layer on top of the rankers. GAT itself was only +0.002 on their deck (0.790 → 0.792). — [speaker deck](https://speakerdeck.com/lyakaap/shopee-2nd-place-solutiontoshang-wei-jie-fa-matome); [writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238022)
- Shopee 18th: forcing the predicted group-size histogram to match train (“chiseling”) was “a largest single change,” 0.739 → 0.759, with “No thresholds!” as an earlier failed approach (hard cosine cutoffs had to be retuned per test size). — [Discussion 237972](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/discussion/237972)

### Inferences

- For a Foursquare-like problem the order of leverage in the honest (non-leak) systems is: (1) union of complementary blockers until max-IoU is ~0.98–0.99, (2) a precise ranker—transformer cross-encoder or a wide string-feature GBDT, about +0.03 CV if you only had a weak GBDT, much less if both are already strong, (3) cluster-aware thresholds / GNN / union-find, about +0.005 to +0.02. Skipping (1) cannot be repaired downstream.
- The official Foursquare prize order is not evidence that CatBoost+`xlm-roberta-large`+`mdeberta-v3-base` is the right production stack. It is evidence that a train/test identity join was worth ~0.03–0.07 IoU on a contaminated test, larger than every modeling delta those teams published.
- Shopee says the same thing without a leak: once embeddings retrieve the right 50 neighbors, threshold policy and neighborhood blending separate 0.75-class systems from 0.78-class systems, and 1st/2nd/3rd then tie inside 0.001.
- SIGMOD 2020 is the exception that proves domain dependence: if a human can write the identity key, model family is noise and the contest collapses to engineering speed. Do not cite it as evidence against GBDT/transformers on noisy POI text.

### Gaps

- No team published a full factorial (same candidates, swap only the ranker, swap only the threshold) on the Foursquare private set. The CV and LB trails above are the best available, and they mix feature changes with model changes.
- Ri’s +0.01 clean-IoU over team merge master is unexplained. It could be blocking, features, or the decision rule.
- Shopee development LBs (0.793, the 0.777→0.789 trail) are not private-LB ablations. The private gap between 1st and 3rd is 0.001, so those trails overstate how much any one stage mattered on the hidden slice.
- No contest here reports precision at a fixed recall (for example P@R=0.95). Gains on mean IoU or micro-F1 can come from either side of the error. Foursquare authors sometimes quote max IoU (recall ceiling of the candidate set) separately from LB, which is the closest split available.

## What constraints did winners face that resemble an 8B-parameter Apache/MIT model cap and no commercial ER API?

### Takeaway

Winners never had an 8B-parameter or Apache/MIT clause by that name. They had a harder practical cap: offline inference, a few hours of GPU or a 35-minute CPU box, and only public checkpoints. Every documented winning model is an open encoder or GBDT far below 8B (BERT/XLM/mDeBERTa class, NFNet, or a distilled transformer). Internet-off or local-only execution made a commercial ER API or a hosted LLM unusable. SIGMOD 2021 additionally required an OSI license on the submitted code.

### Cited Findings

- Foursquare code rules, copied from the competition page: “CPU Notebook <= 9 hours run-time”; “GPU Notebook <= 9 hours run-time”; “Internet access disabled”; “Freely & publicly available external data is allowed, including pre-trained models.” — [Competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching)
- Models that fit that box for the prize teams: `bert-base-multilingual-uncased` (name kNN), `xlm-roberta-base`, `xlm-roberta-large`, `mdeberta-v3-base` / `mdeberta-base-v3`, CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost. No team in the recovered writeups used a multi-billion-parameter model or an external API. 1st place used ForestInference specifically “to reduce inference time.” — [1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution); [2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336090); [GNN team](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/team-merge-master-13th-place-solution-gnn)
- Memory, not parameter license, is what they complain about. 4th place trained a 20-fold LightGBM on 1.1M rows “which was not possible on Kaggle and had to be done elsewhere due to memory constraints,” while inference still had to run in the notebook. 11th place used rank-conditional GBDTs and ForestInference “to save memory” and rented Vast.ai for BERT training after Colab was too slow; inference stayed on Kaggle. 21st place: five SBERT models concatenated for blocking reached max IoU > 0.99, but “Couldn't get mDebertaV3 to work” in their stack, and they used cuML forest inference with a noted float64→float32 accuracy drop. — [4th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/youri-vincent-th-o-4th-place-solution); [MCD3](https://www.mcd3.co.jp/blog/techblog/so-nakashima-4sq); [21st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/bulian-ai-21st-place-solution)
- Shopee winning checkpoints are the same size class: `eca_nfnet_l1`, `xlm-roberta-large` / base, Indonesian BERT, `bert-base-multilingual-uncased`, DeiT, EfficientNet, CLIP, DistilBERT, TF-IDF, LightGBM, GAT, CatBoost. 18th place’s point was that a ResNet-18 fit in a short GPU train and still medaled. Exact notebook hour cap was not copied from the rules. — [1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238136); [2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/shopee-product-matching/discussion/238022); [18th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/discussion/237972); [deck](https://speakerdeck.com/lyakaap/shopee-2nd-place-solutiontoshang-wei-jie-fa-matome)
- SIGMOD 2022 winner constraint, stated by the team: “CPU-only machine with 16 cores and 32GB RAM and had to finish within 35 minutes.” Response: “an extremely small distilled Transformer” plus FAISS, not a large encoder. 55 teams, local execution, no API. — [Mannheim, 3 May 2022](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/)
- SIGMOD 2021: “All teams must agree to license their code under an OSI-approved open source license. By participating in this contest, each team agrees to publish its source code.” That is a code-license constraint, not a weight-license cap, and it did not push anyone toward an 8B model; the winner shipped regex and Python. — [Task page](https://dbgroup.ing.unimo.it/sigmod21contest/task.shtml); [winner repo](https://github.com/yinpeiqi/SIGMOD-2021-program-contest)
- SIGMOD 2020 winner and accuracy leader likewise shipped custom C++/Python rule systems (mock labels; brand/model blocking). No pretrained LM is part of those papers. — [paper2](http://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper2.pdf); [paper5](http://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2726/paper5.pdf)
- Commercial ER APIs are absent from every winning writeup above. Foursquare’s own later product system (Batch Place Match API, blog 4 Dec 2025: sentence-transformer embeddings, FAISS top-10, H3 geoblock at 1,000 m, then a reranker; claimed +15% Recall@10 and +10% recall at 0.95 precision versus their previous system) is a vendor description, not a contest entry, and would have been illegal to call from a 2022 notebook because internet was disabled. — [Foursquare blog, 4 Dec 2025](https://foursquare.com/resources/blog/data/introducing-the-new-fsq-place-match-api/); internet rule [competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching)

### Inferences

- An 8B Apache/MIT cap is looser than what these winners actually ran. They won with public encoder checkpoints in the 10^8-parameter class plus GBDT, because the binding constraints were inference latency, RAM, and no network. Raising the cap from “BERT-large” to “8B MIT” would not, on this evidence, match how the points were scored: extra parameters were not the lever once a cross-encoder or a wide GBDT was in the cascade.
- “No commercial ER API” matches the contests exactly. Foursquare and Shopee forbade internet at submit time. SIGMOD solutions were packed and rerun on the organizer’s machine (2021 via ReproZip). A Senzing-style or Foursquare-API call could not have been submitted.
- The license constraint that did appear (SIGMOD 2021 OSI on source code; Foursquare “publicly available” pretrained weights) pushed toward open checkpoints and open code, which is the same direction as an Apache/MIT weight rule, but nobody was asked to stay under a parameter budget.
- Where the box got truly small (SIGMOD 2022: 32 GB, 16 cores, 35 min), the winning adaptation was distillation plus ANN plus a symbolic rerank, not a quantized LLM. That is the closest template for “you may not run a large model at query time.”

### Gaps

- Parameter counts and SPDX identifiers for `xlm-roberta-large`, `mdeberta-v3-base`, and NFNet were not re-checked against model cards in this pass. “Far below 8B” is safe; an exact “560M, MIT” claim is not sourced here.
- Shopee’s numeric runtime cap and whether external pretrained weights had to be attached to the notebook (they did, in practice) were not quoted from the rules page.
- SIGMOD 2020 and 2022 OSI wording was not re-read. Only 2021’s clause is cited.
- No contest stated an 8B-parameter limit. The resemblance is inferred from runtime, offline, and public-weight rules, not from a written parameter cap.
- Foursquare’s 2025 Place Match API numbers are a vendor blog, not an independent benchmark, and are not a competition result.
