# Geographic and language shift, and the precision-heavy one-to-many decision

Scope note for the report writer. The target task is clean-reference linkage: one deduplicated Source-1 business (about 1.7 million) may match about 3.4 Source-2/3 records or zero. Fields are only name, address, and country. Train countries are the US and India; France is unlabeled at test time. The metric is macro F0.5, so a false merge of lookalikes at the same address costs more than a miss. Almost every published number below is pairwise F1 on product, citation, person, or voter data, not macro F0.5 on business name+address with a held-out country. Where a number was not found, it is marked unmeasured.

## What published methods handle geographic or language shift in record linkage (2020–2026)?

### Takeaway
The measured language-shift results are almost all product matching inside Latin script, not business name+address and not a held-out country. Zero-shot transfer from English drops hard when the matcher is a bag-of-words or an English-only encoder, and much less when the encoder was pre-trained multilingually; adding a little labeled data in a related language, or in the target language, recovers most of the loss. No paper found reports a US/India to France drop, a macro F0.5, or a measured result for E5 or BGE-M3 on entity matching. Self-training recovers F1 inside one dataset, not across a held-out country.

### Cited Findings
- Peeters and Bizer, cross-language product matching on phone offers (English training pairs added to German; test is German; WWW Companion 2022). With 1,800 German pairs only, F1 is SVM 71.00, English BERT 65.27, German BERT 73.43, mBERT 87.69, XLM-R 73.40. Adding 7,200 English pairs changes those to 71.05, 74.29, 89.83, 91.44, 86.98 (deltas +0.05, +9.02, +16.40, +3.75, +13.58). English-only BERT is the worst monolingual starting point; mBERT is the best and the least dependent on extra English data. With only 450 German pairs, adding 7,200 English pairs raises mBERT from 67.11 to 87.97 F1 (+20.86). At 3,600 German pairs the same addition is only +0.83, to 94.46. The published table does not include a pure zero-German column. — [Peeters and Bizer, arXiv:2110.03338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03338)
- Same group, student benchmark with manually checked English, German, Spanish, and French offers (phones and toys). French and Spanish are test-only. In-language multi-class F1 is about 0.97 (toys) and, for transformers, 0.95–0.96 (phones). Zero-shot multi-class transformers trained on English fall to 0.56–0.62 (toys) and 0.60–0.76 (phones); translated-English baselines fall to 0.67–0.71 (toys) and 0.62–0.70 (phones), so classical features beat zero-shot transformers in the multi-class toy setting. Pairwise, the zero-shot drop is smaller: transformers fall to 0.82–0.91 (toys) and 0.69–0.85 (phones), still well above co-occurrence and Magellan baselines at 0.66–0.72 (toys) and 0.60–0.71 (phones). A visible pairwise cell: English-trained, Spanish toys, mBERT 0.8214 versus a Magellan baseline 0.6581; the English-to-French co-occurrence baseline is 0.7005 (transformer cells for that row were truncated in the fetched table). Training on English plus German, with no Spanish or French labels, recovers 7 F1 points (toys) and 29 points (phones) in multi-class French, and 5 points (toys) and 7 points (phones) in pairwise French, relative to English-only zero-shot. — [Mannheim cross-lingual product matching project](https://data.dws.informatik.uni-mannheim.de/Web-based-Systems-Group/StudentProjects/2020/Cross-lingual-Product-Matching-using-Transformers/)
- Alves, Baptista, Barbosa, and Araújo, English WDC products to Brazilian Portuguese invoice descriptions (SAC 2024). Zero-shot (no Portuguese labels): mBERT 78.9 F1, Albertina-PT-BR 75.0, BERTimbau 73.5, in-domain e-commerce BERT 68.3, English BERT 62.5, XLM-R 57.8. Joint learning with about 600 Portuguese pairs (10% of the target training set) brings every model to about 90 F1; XLM-R jumps from 57.8 to 90.4 (+32.6 points). Best joint-learning result in the table is mBERT at 50% Portuguese data, 93.8 F1. A later JL/CL+ schedule reaches 95.4–96.5, against a Portuguese-only reference of 95.3, so once the target language is well labeled the cross-lingual gain shrinks to about one point. — [Alves et al., SAC 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3605098.3636001)
- STEPMatch (Alves et al., ICEIS 2025), short Portuguese invoice descriptions, English WDC as the source language. A multilingual BERT trained with joint learning on 50% English+Portuguese and then cascade-tuned on the remaining Portuguese data scores F1 98.6 (precision 98.9, recall 98.2) against a Portuguese-only baseline of F1 94.3. The thesis summary states 98.11% accuracy for STEPMatch on a test scenario. This is recovery with target-language labels, not a country holdout. — [Alves et al., ICEIS 2025](https://doi.org/10.5220/0013285000003929)
- Peeters, Steiner, and Bizer, LLM versus fine-tuned PLM matchers (arXiv:2310.11244, EDBT 2025 version). The shift is unseen products, not unseen languages. In-domain, Ditto F1 is 84.90 (WDC), 91.31 (Abt-Buy), 86.39 (Walmart-Amazon), 80.07 (Amazon-Google), 94.31 (DBLP-Scholar); zero-shot GPT-4 is 89.61, 95.78, 89.67, 76.38, 89.82, winning three of five sets by roughly 4 F1 and losing the other two. On products absent from training, fine-tuned Ditto drops 36–53 F1 points and RoBERTa 22–47 points relative to the in-domain WDC figure of 84.90; zero-shot GPT-4 beats the best transferred PLM by 34 F1 points, and even the weakest listed LLM (StableBeluga2) beats that transferred PLM by 8 points. The paper’s own wording is “% F1”; the comparison to 84.90 reads as absolute points. — [Peeters et al., arXiv:2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11244)
- WDC Products benchmark (Peeters, Der, and Bizer): every evaluated matcher, including Ditto, HierGAT, and R-SupCon, drops on entities that never appear in training. Contrastive learning is more label-efficient than cross-encoders. A 2022 Bizer talk, citing the dual-objective BERT paper, shows four unlabeled columns of unseen low-similarity product F1 with deltas from −4.41 to −30.91 versus seen products; the columns are not named in the fetched slide, so the deltas are not assigned to models here. — [WDC Products](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09521); [Bizer IC@PFIA slides](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/media/Einrichtungen/dws/Files_Research/Web-based_Systems/pub/Bizer-IntegratingProductDataFromTheSemanticWeb-ICatPFIA2022.pdf)
- Zhang, Li, Calixto, Groth, and Schelter (arXiv:2607.24688, July 2026), Qwen3 family, nine datasets, 1,215 fine-tunes. The shift is cross-dataset, not cross-country. Mean off-diagonal F1 (train on one dataset, test on another): bi-encoders 30.3 (mean retention 36.9), cross-encoders 64.9 (retention 79.1), generative matchers 73.8 (retention 83.6). Larger Qwen3 cross-encoders get worse, not better: F1 97.6 at 0.6B versus 85.0 at 8B, driven by precision falling from 96.9 to 76.4 while recall stays near 99. That is over-acceptance, not over-rejection, and it is schema/dataset shift. — [Zhang et al., arXiv:2607.24688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24688)
- Zhang, Groth, Calixto, and Schelter (EDBT 2025): eight matchers, 11 datasets, no target labels (cross-dataset, not cross-lingual). Fine-tuned small models match prompted large models; data-centric transfer beats model-centric transfer; a good small model is orders of magnitude cheaper than a commercial LLM at similar F1. AnyMatch on LLaMA-3.2 is reported on par with trillion-parameter GPT-4 in their average. — [Zhang et al., EDBT 2025](https://doi.org/10.48786/edbt.2025.75)
- Sudowoodo (Wang, Li, and Wang, ICDE 2023, arXiv:2207.04122): contrastive self-supervision on unlabeled records, then optional fine-tuning. Not a language or country split. Unsupervised F1 beats ZeroER by 7.7 on average and Auto-FuzzyJoin by 8.9, and sits about 4 F1 below the same system with 500 labels. With 500 labels the reported average is 78.3 F1 (Abt-Buy 81.1, Amazon-Google 59.3, DBLP-ACM 95.2, DBLP-Scholar 89.9, Walmart-Amazon 66.1) versus about 69.9 for Ditto and 72.3 for Rotom under the same label budget in a later reproduction. Pseudo-labeling is part of the fine-tune, but the unlabeled pool is the same dataset. — [Sudowoodo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04122); [Megagon summary](https://megagon.ai/sudowoodo-data-integration-apps/)
- IGEA (iterative cross-attention alignment of geographic KGs and OSM): on real multi-country geo data, up to +18 F1 points over baselines; the iterative high-confidence pseudo-label step adds up to +6 F1 on Wikidata and +7 on DBpedia. The fetched abstract does not name which country was held out, and the entities are places in a KG, not businesses. — [IGEA, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-47240-4_12)
- Schumacher, Mayfield, and Dredze (MRL 2022): zero-shot cross-language entity linking with XLM-R “does reasonably well”; their training changes raise recall on most sets, “often matching the in-language performance”; they also state that in-language annotation remains necessary for an accurate linker. No per-language F1 drop was extracted. — [Schumacher et al., ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.mrl-1.4.pdf)
- Temporal, not geographic: ICE-ID census linkage. Transformer-style tabular models lose up to 30 F1 points on censuses 50 years after the training period; the symbolic NARS matcher degrades less. — [ICE-ID, arXiv:2506.13792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13792)
- Transliteration evidence that is adjacent but not this task. An unsupervised 69-language transliterator matches gold transliterations at top-1/20/100 averages of 32.85% / 60.44% / 83.20%, and scores false-friend detection at F1 0.890 (French) and 0.825 (Spanish). That is lexicon induction, not record linkage. — [Hermjakob et al., arXiv:1611.06722](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06722). A name-matching note on British and Russian company directors reports that transliteration plus phonetic matching plus a Bayesian correction beat plain Jaro-Winkler, which cannot cross Cyrillic and Latin; the numeric table was not extracted. — [Schwarzthal name matching](https://schwarzthal.tech/resources/name-matching-en.pdf). Google’s India POI system transliterates Latin names into 10 Indic scripts and claims up to about 20× coverage; it does not report match F1. — [Google India blog](https://india.googleblog.com/search?updated-max=2021-02-17T21:36:00-08:00&max-results=7). Amazon has a patent on matching both the original record and a transliterated copy; no accuracy is published. — [US patent application](https://trea.com/information/transliteration-of-data-records-for-improved-data-matching/patentapplication/18d81949-45c7-432d-94e8-968675958ad2). An Indian government farmer-linkage note translates regional-language land records to English via phonemes, then uses Soundex blocking and Jaro-Winkler, Jaccard, and cosine features; no F1 is stated. — [NIC name-matching note](https://ai.nic.in/AI/NameMatchingCaseML)
- Lexicon-free string features. Magellan’s pair features (Jaccard, Dice, cosine, overlap, Levenshtein, Jaro, Jaro-Winkler, Monge-Elkan, Smith-Waterman, exact match) are the feature set MERAI actually trains on, and the Mannheim baselines use the same family plus word co-occurrence. On the Latin-script product sets above they lose to mBERT/XLM-R once any target or related-language labels exist, and they also lose the pairwise zero-shot comparison, but they beat zero-shot transformers in the multi-class toy setting. No paper found isolates character n-grams versus token features on a held-out country. — [MERAI, arXiv:2508.03767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767); [Mannheim project](https://data.dws.informatik.uni-mannheim.de/Web-based-Systems-Group/StudentProjects/2020/Cross-lingual-Product-Matching-using-Transformers/)
- Company linkage with an explicit geographic score, but no country holdout: IBM’s MinHash company matcher (name, location, industry) reports 91% recall against 73% for its baseline, scaling linearly with nodes. — [Gschwind et al., arXiv:1907.08667](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08667)
- Multilingual product matching in Polish (Mozolewski et al., arXiv:2205.15712): mBERT and XLM-R fine-tuned on English WDC match or beat Ditto on several English size splits (for example cameras xlarge, XLM-R 93.78 versus Ditto 89.21 in one reported cell) and are then applied to a new Polish set. A zero-shot English-to-Polish F1 was not in the fetched text. — [arXiv:2205.15712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15712)
- E5 and BGE-M3. The only numbers found are a Portuguese/Spanish FAQ retrieval paper, not entity matching: off-the-shelf BGE-M3 Accuracy@1 73.97 (Portuguese) and 69.92 (Spanish), multilingual E5-base 68.98 and 70.14, BM25 76.14 and 70.86. Domain-tuned multilingual E5 reached about 90 Accuracy@1. These are not match decisions and not a country holdout inside ER. — [SciTePress 2025 embeddings paper](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2025/138212/138212.pdf)
- Qwen as a matcher backbone appears in the 2026 factorial study above (dataset shift, not language). A July 2025 note uses Qwen 2.5 among interface LLMs on 65 Portuguese company cases and claims traditional string metrics above 92% accuracy with 20–40% false positives, and interface LLMs above 93% accuracy and 96 F1 with “lower false positives (40–80%)”. The false-positive clause contradicts itself, and n=65 is not a held-out-country evaluation. Do not treat it as a recovery number. — [arXiv:2507.11086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11086)

### Inferences
- France is Latin-script, like the US side of the training distribution. The closest measured analogue is English to French or Portuguese product matching, not a script shift. On that analogue, mBERT or XLM-R zero-shot pairwise F1 lands in the 0.69–0.91 band against in-language scores around 0.93–0.95, and a few hundred target-language pairs (Alves: ~600) or even labels from a third language (German helping French) close most of the gap. India matters only if training names are Indic-script or heavily transliterated; none of the product papers measure that.
- A country field is useful as a block, not as a lexicon. Businesses rarely match across countries, so blocking inside country does not require French labels. What fails to transfer is a matcher whose tokens, legal-form list, or address dictionary were fit on US and Indian strings.
- Character and edit features are the part of the stack that does not need a French lexicon. The product experiments still show they are not sufficient on their own for pairwise zero-shot: mBERT beat Magellan by about 16 F1 points on the one fully visible English-to-Spanish cell. They are a floor, not the winner, once a multilingual encoder is allowed.
- Self-training on unlabeled French pairs is plausible because Sudowoodo and IGEA both gain from high-confidence pseudo-labels, but neither experiment is a held-out country of business records. Calling it a recovery method for France is unmeasured.

### Gaps
- No 2020–2026 paper was found that holds out a country in business name+address+country linkage and reports the F1 or F0.5 drop and the recovery. Geographic KG alignment (IGEA) and census temporal shift (ICE-ID, up to −30 F1) are the nearest geo/time numbers, and they are different tasks.
- No entity-matching number was found for E5, BGE-M3, or a Qwen embedding model under a language holdout. The Qwen3 ER study is cross-dataset.
- No measured comparison of transliteration versus raw multilingual encoding on French or Indian business names.
- Macro F0.5 under language shift: unmeasured. Every score in this section is F1 or accuracy unless noted.

## For one-to-many or collective ER, which decision layer won?

### Takeaway
Nothing in the literature runs the bake-off this task needs (independent thresholds versus Hungarian versus F-beta thresholding versus connected components versus a rival-aware matcher) under macro F0.5 and a 3.4-to-1 clean reference. What is measured: on clean-clean one-to-one data, greedy unique mapping and a Hungarian-style heuristic beat bipartite connected components on F1, and every one-to-one rule trades recall for precision. On one-anchor-to-many-candidates data, a matcher that sees the rival candidates beats independent pairwise decisions by about 5–13 F1. Connected components are the default industrial clusterer and are the wrong constraint if two distinct reference businesses can be bridged by a lookalike.

### Cited Findings
- Papadakis, Efthymiou, Thanos, Hassanzadeh, and Christen (VLDB Journal 2023; arXiv:2112.14030): eight bipartite matching algorithms, ten clean-clean datasets, more than 700 similarity graphs. The task is one-to-one. All eight methods push precision above recall. Unique Mapping Clustering (sort edges, commit a pair only if both ends are free) is the most balanced (precision−recall gap about 0.015–0.017) and is the best or statistically tied for best F1, together with KRC; CNC (connected components on the bipartite graph) has precision almost twice its recall and is second-worst on F1, ahead only of a stochastic assignment heuristic. Exact Hungarian is excluded as O(n^3). On balanced data, UMC with syntactic edge weights is described as the best effectiveness/runtime trade-off; on some sets KRC leads UMC by about 1.5–2.4 F1. These F1 gaps are small. The paper does not recompute F0.5. UMC is exactly greedy one-to-one assignment, not one-to-many. — [Papadakis et al., VLDBJ 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00778-023-00791-3); [arXiv:2112.14030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.14030)
- The same paper points at Gemmell et al. (2011) for explicit one-to-many and many-to-many algorithms (MutualFirstChoice, Greedy, MaxWeight). It does not reproduce their one-to-many numbers. — [Papadakis et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.14030v3)
- Jaro (1989), as restated in the R Journal: one-to-one linkage is a transportation problem (maximize total weight with degree ≤ 1 on both sides). The same note says n-to-1 links are the right constraint when many admissions belong to one patient, and n-to-m when linking employees to employers. No F-score bake-off is in the fetched page. — [R Journal, RJ-2019-008](https://journal.r-project.org/articles/RJ-2019-008)
- Python `recordlinkage` implements three post-processors and labels them experimental: greedy one-to-one, greedy one-to-many (a record on the “many” side links to at most one record on the “one” side), and connected components for deduplication. No comparative F1 is published on that documentation page. — [recordlinkage documentation](https://readthedocs.org/projects/recordlinkage/downloads/pdf/stable)
- Splink’s default clusterer is connected components at a match-probability threshold (`cluster_pairwise_predictions_at_threshold`). The documentation’s own example shows that moving the threshold from 0.95 to 0.999 splits one component into two and then three clusters, so the threshold is the precision knob and transitivity still glues records that share a path. A second method, `cluster_using_single_best_links`, keeps at most one record from each dataset listed in `duplicate_free_datasets`, and only if the link is mutually best. Ties can be dropped or broken by lowest id. That is the implemented one-to-many constraint when only the reference file is marked duplicate-free. No paper was found that reports its F1 or F0.5 against plain thresholding or connected components. A May 2026 user discussion reports that running an extra one-to-many collapse after connected components threw away true many-side links; that is an anecdote, not a benchmark. — [Splink clustering API](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/api_docs/linker_clustering.html); [linked data as graphs](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/linked_data_as_graphs.html); [GitHub discussion 3049](https://github.com/moj-analytical-services/splink/discussions/3049)
- MERAI (Kannangara et al., arXiv:2508.03767): pairwise supervised classification, then for deduplication a graph of pairs above the decision, connected components, then a greedy split into disjoint cliques that deletes the lower-weight edges in overlapping cliques. They do this because a component is not a clique. For record linkage they do not cluster at all; the published numbers are pairwise. See the industrial section for the F1 table. They never compare thresholds, Hungarian, and cliques on one metric. — [MERAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767)
- Dedupe clusters with hierarchical agglomerative clustering and centroid linkage, after active learning sets the match threshold. No head-to-head against Hungarian or F-beta is given. — [MERAI’s description of Dedupe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767); [dedupe](https://github.com/dedupeio/dedupe)
- FAMER multi-source clustering (Saeedi, Peukert, Rahm, and later clean/dirty variants): source-consistent hierarchical clustering refuses a merge that would put two records from a clean source in one cluster. On the SIGMOD 2020 camera data, as the fraction of clean sources rises, the source-consistent single-link variant’s F-measure improves relative to generic clustering; the fetched slide does not give the F numbers, only the qualitative pattern (high recall and, with more clean sources, high precision). — [University of Leipzig multi-source slides](https://dbs.uni-leipzig.de/files/2023-07/multi-source-DI-jan2022.pdf)
- GNEM (Chen, Shen, and Zhang, WWW 2021): one record is scored jointly against its candidate set with a gated GNN so each pair sees rival pairs. Up to +8.4 F1 versus prior pairwise neural matchers. On the textual Abt-Buy set the average gain is +4.39 F1 (+8.9%) over the pairwise models and +4.76 F1 (+11.0%) over the same model with interactions removed. This is rival-awareness, not a cardinality constraint, and the metric is F1. — [GNEM, WWW 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3442381.3450119)
- ComEM (Wang et al., COLING 2025, arXiv:2405.16884): LLM strategies on eight ER datasets. Comparing two candidates beats independent pairwise prompting by 5.24 F1 on average. Selecting the match from the whole candidate list is best on 6 of 8 datasets and adds up to 8.15 F1 over comparing. Across the study, selecting beats the matching strategy by 13.39 F1 on average. Open-source detail: Mistral-7B-Instruct mean F1 is 45.91 (matching), 54.82 (comparing), 73.80 (selecting); Mixtral-8x7B goes from 62.39 to 79.09. Selecting is also the cheapest of the three (under half the matching cost) but suffers position bias and weak long-context behavior. ComEM’s two-stage filter-then-select adds up to 8.46 F1 over selecting alone and cuts cost. A follow-up notes that ComEM with 3–11B zero-shot models still did not beat the best supervised pairwise matchers; the gap is attributed to model size, not to the selective formulation. Not F0.5, not a geographic shift. — [ComEM, arXiv:2405.16884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16884)
- CaRL-EM (arXiv:2609.01195, September 2026): a learned controller chooses Match, Compare, or Select per anchor. Reported macro of two F1s: Ditto 59.75 (F1 on identities 73.52, F1 on “none” 45.99), ComEM 73.22 (85.66 / 60.78), best CaRL-EM (GPT-oss) 79.14 (85.41 / 72.91). Ablation: dropping Select hurts identity F1; dropping Match hurts the no-match cases. This is the closest measured “there may be zero matches” signal, but it is not macro F0.5 and not one-to-many output. — [CaRL-EM](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01195v1)
- Thresholds and F-beta. Tilores’ engineering note says to tune the auto-merge threshold to F0.5 when a false merge is more costly than a miss, and to F2 in the opposite case. It gives an arithmetic example (precision 89%, recall 80% → F0.5 87%, F1 84%, F2 81%) and no linkage experiment. — [Tilores](https://tilores.io/content/precision-and-recall-in-entity-identity-resolution/). Wang et al. (arXiv:1509.03302) prove that pairwise precision of a fixed threshold falls as the file grows, and show that tuning the threshold on an estimated precision lower bound tracks true precision. They do not optimize F0.5 and do not compare to assignment or clustering. No paper was found that sets an ER threshold by expected F-beta and then beats clustering on a test set.
- Older collective constraint result, movies not businesses: a global one-to-one greedy matcher agreed with 12,695 of 13,005 Freebase Netflix–IMDB links (98%), and one-to-one beat an unconstrained logistic-regression matcher on both head and tail movies. The same note shows a case where lowering the threshold flips a true positive into two false positives under a maximum-weight matching. — [arXiv:1108.6016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1108.6016)

### Inferences
- Cardinality first, then the score. Source 1 is duplicate-free and a source record should attach to at most one reference business, while a reference may keep many source records (mean about 3.4) or none. That is many-to-one, which `recordlinkage.OneToManyLinking` and Splink’s `cluster_using_single_best_links` (reference marked duplicate-free, sources not) already implement. Hungarian and Papadakis UMC force one-to-one and would discard true extra matches; plain connected components can chain two reference businesses through a shared address lookalike, which is exactly the expensive error. Neither failure was re-measured on this schema.
- Because published one-to-one methods already sacrifice recall for precision, an F0.5 objective would likely rank a high threshold plus a many-to-one constraint above connected components. That ranking is an inference from Papadakis’s precision/recall gaps and from MERAI’s decision to break components into cliques. It is not a measured macro F0.5.
- Rival-aware scoring is the piece with a real gain on lookalikes: GNEM +4 to +8 F1 and ComEM selecting +13 F1 over independent pairs, on sets where several candidates compete for one anchor. Those papers then still emit independent labels, so a many-to-one cleanup can still be required on top. Combining them is unmeasured.
- Expectation under F-beta, as a decision rule, was not found as a winning ER layer. Threshold search on a validation F0.5 is standard classification practice (Tilores states it; the 2015 precision-bound paper shows why an uncalibrated threshold will not hold as the file grows to 1.7 million). No entity-resolution bake-off says it beats assignment.

### Gaps
- No measured bake-off of independent thresholds, Hungarian, expected F-beta, connected components, and rival-aware matchers on one-to-many linkage, and none under macro F0.5.
- Gemmell et al. 2011’s one-to-many numbers were not retrieved.
- How macro F0.5 treats a reference with zero true matches (empty prediction versus a false merge) is undefined in the papers above. CaRL-EM’s separate “none” F1 is the only related split found, and it is not F0.5.
- Whether a many-to-one constraint helps or hurts when a source file itself contains duplicates of one business is unmeasured. The 3.4 average says duplicates exist on the source side, so the constraint belongs on the reference side only.

## Do LLM judges beat GBDT stackers under domain shift, or do they over-reject on unseen languages?

### Takeaway
No paper found compares an LLM judge, pairwise or listwise, with a GBDT stacker under a held-out language, and none measures over-rejection on an unseen language. On the shifts that were measured (unseen products, unseen datasets), zero-shot GPT-4 beats a fine-tuned cross-encoder that was trained on a different slice, while fine-tuning the LLM can destroy that robustness. Small fine-tuned matchers match prompted LLMs when the transfer set is built carefully. Larger fine-tuned encoders in one 2026 study over-accept (precision falls), which is the opposite of over-rejection.

### Cited Findings
- Unseen products, not unseen languages (Peeters, Steiner, and Bizer, arXiv:2310.11244). Fine-tuned Ditto and RoBERTa drop 36–53 and 22–47 F1 points on products that were not in the training pairs. Zero-shot GPT-4, which was never fine-tuned on the task, beats the best of those transferred PLMs by 34 F1 points. In-domain, GPT-4 zero-shot is only about 4 F1 ahead on three of five datasets and behind on Amazon-Google (76.38 versus Ditto 80.07) and DBLP-Scholar (89.82 versus 94.31). The later version adds that fine-tuning GPT-mini keeps cross-dataset generalization, while fine-tuning Llama models reduces it. — [arXiv:2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11244)
- A 2024/2025 fine-tuning study of Llama-8B and GPT-4o-mini for entity matching: Llama-8B zero-shot F1 is 53.36 (WDC), 56.57 (Abt-Buy), 49.16 (Amazon-Google), 42.04 (Walmart-Amazon), 85.52 (DBLP-ACM), 67.69 (DBLP-Scholar). After fine-tuning on WDC, in-domain WDC rises to 69.19, but DBLP-ACM falls from 85.52 to 74.52, and the paper’s cross-domain column is summarized as a −30% gain. The authors state that every fine-tuning recipe they tried underperformed Llama’s own zero-shot on cross-domain sets. GPT-4o-mini improved when trained with explanations but still did not beat its zero-shot cross-domain scores. Precision and recall are not split, so this is not evidence of over-rejection specifically. — [Fine-tuning LLMs for entity matching](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08185v2)
- Cross-dataset, Qwen3, 2026 (arXiv:2607.24688). Generative (LLM-style) matchers retain the most target F1 under dataset shift: mean off-diagonal F1 73.8 versus 64.9 for cross-encoders and 30.3 for bi-encoders. Scaling the cross-encoder from 0.6B to 8B drops precision from 96.9 to 76.4 at nearly constant recall. The failure mode measured here is false matches, not refusals. — [arXiv:2607.24688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24688)
- EDBT 2025 cross-dataset study (Zhang et al.): fine-tuned small models perform on par with prompted large models; how the transfer data is built matters more than which model family is used; cost differs by orders of magnitude. This is the strongest published case that a small supervised stacker is not dominated by an LLM judge when the target dataset is new. The stacker in that study is a fine-tuned language model (AnyMatch / Ditto / Unicorn), not a GBDT on string features. — [EDBT 2025](https://doi.org/10.48786/edbt.2025.75)
- Listwise versus pairwise LLM judges, in-domain (ComEM, COLING 2025): the listwise select prompt beats pairwise matching prompts by 13.39 F1 on average, and beats a pairwise compare prompt by up to 8.15 F1. Six of eight datasets prefer selecting. Mistral-7B selecting mean F1 73.80 versus matching 45.91. Position bias is reported, which can drop a true match that is not near the front of the list. No GBDT and no language split. — [arXiv:2405.16884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16884)
- Distilling LLM explanations into a smaller matcher (2024): a model trained on WDC cameras loses about 15 F1 when tested on WDC computers. Training the small model on natural-language explanations raises average F1 by 22.32 (cross-schema), 14.47 (cross-domain), and 13.67 (cross-distribution). Again dataset shift, and the gain is from distillation, not from using the LLM as the production judge. — [ResearchGate record of the explanations paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381404644_Learning_from_Natural_Language_Explanations_for_Generalizable_Entity_Matching)
- The nearest GBDT-style stacker under a shift that was actually benchmarked is MERAI’s supervised model (Magellan string features; decision tree, naive Bayes, SVM, XGBoost, and random forest were all tried) against Splink’s unsupervised Fellegi-Sunter model, on US voter snapshots rather than a new language. MERAI’s pairwise F1 is higher in every dedup and linkage cell; Splink at probability 0.99 over-matches in deduplication (precision 60–76.5% at recall 85.7–98.4%) and under-recalls in linkage (recall 78.3–85.2% at precision 88.6–94.1%). Which of the five classifiers won is not reported, so “GBDT beat probabilistic linkage” is only true if XGBoost was the deployed model, which the paper does not say. Ditto is not in the comparison. — [MERAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767)
- SGER (ACL Industry 2026) fine-tunes an LLM on Indian person names in two stages (parse the name, then match). Held-out accuracy 99.02% and F1 0.994 on 50,000 real pairs, above GPT-4o few-shot and above single-stage fine-tuning. The test is in-domain Indian names, not an unseen language, and the entities are people. It does show that a prompted frontier model lost to a fine-tuned smaller model on the noisy transliterated names a production system actually had. — [ACL Anthology 2026 industry](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.101/)
- The 65-case Portuguese company note (arXiv:2507.11086) claims interface LLMs (Copilot, Qwen 2.5) beat Jaccard, cosine, and Levenshtein on F1 (above 96 versus accuracy above 92 for the string metrics) with a confused false-positive statement. It does not include a GBDT, and it is not a shift experiment with a training country and a test country.

### Inferences
- Under distribution shift, the robust published pattern is a model that was not fine-tuned into the source country’s idiosyncrasies: zero-shot GPT-4 on unseen products, and generative Qwen3 matchers on unseen datasets. Fine-tuning Llama on the source erased its cross-domain advantage. A GBDT on character features would be expected to behave more like the Magellan baselines in the French product study (a real drop, but not a collapse) than like an English-only BERT. That comparison was not run.
- Over-rejection on an unseen language is not supported by any number found. The measured large-model failure on a new dataset is the opposite: precision falls, recall stays high. Listwise LLM judges have a different, measured failure: position bias, which can miss a true match rather than merge a lookalike. Whether a French prompt makes an LLM refuse more often than a US prompt is unmeasured.
- For a precision-heavy objective, an LLM judge that over-accepts (the Qwen3 scaling result) is the dangerous direction. A high decision threshold on a calibrated GBDT is the lever MERAI actually uses when false positives are unacceptable. Whether that threshold still calibrates on French is unmeasured; the 2015 precision-bound paper says an uncalibrated threshold will not hold as the candidate set grows.

### Gaps
- No LLM-versus-GBDT comparison under a held-out language or country.
- No precision/recall split that shows LLMs over-rejecting unseen languages in entity matching.
- No macro F0.5 for either family on business records.
- MERAI never names the winning classifier among XGBoost, random forests, SVM, naive Bayes, and decision trees.

## What do industrial systems at this scale actually run, and do they claim to beat Ditto or a GBDT?

### Takeaway
At millions to tens of millions of records the published production pipelines are blocking, then a pairwise scorer (Fellegi-Sunter or a supervised model on string features), then a cluster or a link threshold. MERAI is the one system with a head-to-head at this scale: it beats Dedupe and Splink on pairwise F1 and runs 15.7 million voter records and internal jobs up to 33 million, while Dedupe dies in memory past about 2–3 million. It does not compare itself to Ditto. Its scorer is a supervised model on Magellan’s string features, so it is a GBDT-style stacker rather than a claim to beat one. Zingg publishes scale, not a Ditto or GBDT bake-off. Splink is unsupervised Fellegi-Sunter plus connected components, with an optional one-record-per-clean-file constraint and no claim to beat Ditto.

### Cited Findings
- Splink (Linacre and the UK Ministry of Justice; IJPDS 2022, cited by MERAI). Stages: blocking rules, Fellegi-Sunter comparisons (m and u probabilities, term-frequency adjustments, λ prior), u estimated by random sampling, m and λ estimated by expectation maximisation, pairwise match probability, then connected components at a user threshold, or single-best-links if some inputs are duplicate-free. No labeled training is required. The MoJ Data First deployment links prison, probation, and courts in two layers (deduplicate inside a system, then link across systems) and assigns a person id to pairs above a threshold. An ONS write-up uses Splink to link 2021 Census data to itself; the fetched pages do not give the row count or the F1. Downloads were reported above 7 million by November 2023. Splink does not claim to beat Ditto or a GBDT. In MERAI’s voter experiment, at a 0.99 probability cutoff, it is the least precise deduplicator (precision 60–76.5%) and a lower-recall linker (recall 78.3–85.2%). — [Fellegi-Sunter topic guide](https://github.com/moj-analytical-services/splink/blob/master/docs/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.md); [Splink README](https://github.com/moj-analytical-services/splink); [Real World Data Science, 22 Nov 2023](https://realworlddatascience.net/applied-insights/case-studies/posts/2023/11/22/splink.html); [MoJ algorithmic transparency record](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/moj-data-first-splink); [MERAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767)
- Dedupe (Gregg and Eder). Stages: active learning (the library asks for matches and non-matches; at least 10 of each), Fellegi-Sunter-style weights, a learned threshold, then hierarchical agglomerative clustering with centroid linkage and a cluster confidence. MERAI: Dedupe is faster than MERAI below about 10,000 records; clustering time blows up past 1 million; a 3 million record run was killed after 24 hours on a memory error; experiments were stopped at 2 million. The abstract says Dedupe “failed to scale beyond 2 million records.” The project documentation, as cited by MERAI, already warns about memory above 10,000 records. No Ditto comparison. — [MERAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767); [dedupe](https://github.com/dedupeio/dedupe)
- Zingg. Stages, from the project docs and the vendor’s own write-ups: active learning on a few hundred pairs labeled match / non-match / unsure; a learned blocking model that, by their claim, touches 0.05–1% of the pairs; a second similarity classifier scored only inside blocks; `match` for deduplication and `link` for cross-source; output is a cluster id. Stated runtimes, which are vendor or user reports rather than an independent paper: 120,000 records in 5 minutes on a 4-core 10 GB Spark node; 9 million records and 3 fields in 45 minutes on an AWS m5.24xlarge (96 cores, 384 GB); 80 million records and 8–10 fields in under 2 hours on one driver plus eight workers, “user-reported, without optimization.” The README says the learner is meant to avoid the failure modes of Fellegi-Sunter and of “entity-centric matching.” No table against Ditto, against a GBDT, or against Splink was found. — [Zingg repository](https://github.com/zinggAI/zingg); [Zingg blocking post, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.zingg.ai/post/entity-resolution-at-scale-part-3-blocking)
- MERAI (Commonwealth Bank of Australia; arXiv:2508.03767, August 2025; IEEE BigData 2025). Stages, in order: (1) profile each column and clean with regular expressions, drop constant columns and dummy phones; (2) blocking on all non-empty subsets of chosen attributes, with list-valued attributes expanded (two addresses × three phones → six rows), values dictionary-encoded to integers, and any block larger than a cap dropped (1,000 rows is what they used on customer profiles); (3) Magellan token and edit similarities on every attribute, all similarities kept rather than Magellan’s length heuristic, then a supervised classifier trained on human labels (they tried decision trees, naive Bayes, SVM, XGBoost, and random forests) that emits a probability so the threshold can be raised when false positives are unacceptable; (4) for deduplication only, connected components and then a greedy disjoint-clique extraction that throws away the lighter edges when cliques overlap. Linkage stops at the pairwise probabilities. Scale: public North Carolina voter snapshots of 9.7, 6.7, and 15.7 million records; indexing emitted on the order of 80–250 million pairs (2020 dedup 208.7 million, 2010–2020 link 253.6 million); internal bank jobs up to 33 million records. Dedupe could not be run at the full size. They explicitly did not time Splink further because its accuracy was already too low for them. Accuracy, pairwise, from the paper’s prose because the PDF table is column-scrambled in extraction: deduplication, MERAI precision 100% and recall 92.5–95%, best F1 in every cell; Dedupe precision perfect or near-perfect with recall 75–96.8%; Splink precision 60–76.5% and recall 85.7–98.4%. Linkage, MERAI precision 92.6–96.7%, recall 98.2–100%, F1 above 96% in every linkage experiment; Dedupe recall 99.4–100% but precision only 71.6–73.7%; Splink precision 88.6–94.1% and recall 78.3–85.2%. Runtime shape: MERAI indexing stays on the order of 40 minutes from 10,000 to 2 million records on 10 CPUs, while Dedupe’s total time steepens sharply (about 37 minutes at 10,000 records versus multi-hour clustering once past 1 million, in their tables). They do not mention Ditto. The conclusion says the features are still Magellan’s and that a custom feature stage is future work. So the claim is “beats Dedupe and Splink on F1 at 10^7 records,” not “beats Ditto” and not “beats a GBDT”: the matcher is itself a tree/linear model on GBDT-style features. — [MERAI, arXiv:2508.03767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767)
- Ditto, for the comparison these systems do not make (Li et al., VLDB 2021, arXiv:2004.00584): a cross-encoder (BERT, DistilBERT, or RoBERTa) on the serialized pair, plus data augmentation and domain knowledge, up to +29 F1 over the previous benchmark SOTA and up to +9.8 F1 from the extras, and the previous SOTA with at most half the labels. The production-scale demonstration is two company files of 789,000 and 412,000 rows, 96.5% F1 on a holdout, with blocking making the end-to-end run up to 3.8× faster. That is large but an order of magnitude under MERAI’s 15.7 million, and it is not one-to-many macro F0.5. — [Ditto](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.00584)
- Magellan is the feature and learning toolkit underneath MERAI, with optional deep models, and it has no built-in clustering; MERAI says so and adds the clique stage for that reason. — [MERAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03767)

### Inferences
- For 1.7 million clean references the only architecture in this set with both a published accuracy table and a demonstrated 10-million-row run is MERAI’s: aggressive blocking with a max block size, lexicon-free string features, a supervised probability, and a high threshold when false merges matter. Splink is the one that already has the right many-to-one cluster primitive, but on the one shared benchmark it was the precision problem in dedup and the recall problem in linkage. Zingg’s two-model active-learning design is the same shape as MERAI plus learned blocking, without a published F1 against either.
- Beating Ditto is unmeasured for all three industrial systems. Ditto’s own company-scale number (96.5 F1 at a few hundred thousand rows) is not comparable to MERAI’s voter F1, and Ditto has the robustness failure on unseen products documented by Peeters et al. A stacker on character features is the safer base model for an unlabeled France slice; a multilingual cross-encoder is the piece that recovered French and Portuguese in the product studies. No industrial paper runs both.
- MERAI’s choice not to cluster linkage outputs matches a precision-heavy one-to-many read of the problem: transitivity is where lookalikes at one address get glued together. Their dedup clique splitter exists specifically to cut those edges. Applying connected components across Source 1 would reintroduce the error they spent the clustering section removing.

### Gaps
- No industrial system above claims, or shows, a win over Ditto.
- MERAI does not say whether XGBoost beat random forests. “Beats a GBDT” cannot be answered from the paper; the system is a sibling of a GBDT stacker.
- Zingg’s 9 million and 80 million timings are vendor or user reports, not an independent evaluation, and they have no accuracy next to them.
- Splink’s census-scale F1 and row counts were not in the fetched ONS/MoJ pages.
- None of the four systems reports macro F0.5, a held-out country, or a one-to-many business linkage with mean degree 3.4.
